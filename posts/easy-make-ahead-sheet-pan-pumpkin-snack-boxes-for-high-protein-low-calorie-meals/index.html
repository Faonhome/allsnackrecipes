Easy Make-Ahead Sheet Pan Pumpkin Snack Boxes for High Protein Low Calorie Meals

I’ve always struggled with feeling hungry on low-calorie diets, especially when cutting. Small, calorie-dense snacks just don’t satisfy me, and I hate feeling like I’m eating air. That’s why I started experimenting with sheet pan pumpkin recipes that pack a ton of volume and protein but stay light on calories. Pumpkin is amazing because it’s naturally low calorie but high in fiber, which helps fill you up. Adding chickpeas brings in that protein punch, making this snack perfect for afternoons when I need to refuel without breaking my calorie budget. What I love most about this recipe is how easy it is to make a big batch ahead of time. Roasting everything on one pan means minimal cleanup and maximum flavor. I’ve found that tossing the pumpkin and veggies with just a touch of olive oil and spices brings out a warm, cozy flavor without adding unnecessary calories. Plus, adding a dollop of zero-fat Greek yogurt on top gives it a creamy finish that feels indulgent but isn’t. I tried a few batches where the pumpkin was too watery or the chickpeas didn’t crisp up, but roasting at the right temperature and spacing the ingredients out fixed that. The result is a snack that looks huge on your plate but clocks in under 200 calories with over 18 grams of protein. It’s become my go-to for meal prep Sundays and an easy way to eat a big snack while watching Netflix or packing my gym bag. Honestly, my friends keep asking how I eat so much and still lose weight! This recipe is my secret weapon.

What You'll Need

6 meal-prep snack boxes, approximately 1.5 cups volume per serving

  • 2 cups pumpkin puree, fresh or canned, unsweetened
  • 1.5 cups cooked chickpeas, drained and rinsed
  • 1 cup diced bell peppers, raw
  • 1 cup chopped celery, raw
  • 1/2 cup chopped red onion
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1/2 tsp ground cumin
  • 1/4 tsp black pepper
  • 1/4 tsp salt
  • 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice
  • 1 tsp olive oil, optional for roasting
  • 1/4 cup non-fat Greek yogurt (0%) for serving or mixing

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 400°F (200°C). Line a large sheet pan with parchment paper to prevent sticking and make cleanup easier. Spread the pumpkin puree evenly on the pan in a thin layer. This helps the pumpkin roast rather than steam, intensifying its natural sweetness and reducing excess moisture that can make the snack soggy. Avoid piling the pumpkin thickly to ensure it dries slightly and develops a nice texture during roasting.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes prep
✓ Doneness: Pumpkin looks slightly caramelized around edges and no longer watery
Step 2
Add the chickpeas, bell peppers, celery, and red onion on top of the pumpkin layer, spacing them out evenly. Drizzle with olive oil if using, then sprinkle smoked paprika, cumin, salt, and pepper over everything. Toss gently with your hands or a spatula to coat all ingredients evenly with spices and oil. This step is crucial because the spices add depth and the oil helps the chickpeas crisp up without adding too many calories. Don’t skip the seasoning—it makes a huge difference!
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Ingredients look evenly coated and vibrant in color
Step 3
Place the sheet pan in the oven and roast for 20-25 minutes. Halfway through, use a spatula to gently flip the chickpeas and vegetables to ensure even roasting. This flipping prevents burning and promotes crispness, especially for the chickpeas, which are your main protein source. Roasting at 400°F caramelizes the pumpkin’s sugars and crisps the chickpeas without drying out the veggies, preserving volume and texture.
⏱ Time: 20-25 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chickpeas are golden and crunchy, vegetables are tender but not mushy
Step 4
Once roasted, remove the sheet pan from the oven and let it cool slightly. Then transfer the mixture into 6 portion containers for meal prep or serving. The cooling step helps the flavors settle and the texture firm up, preventing the pumpkin from being too soft or watery when eaten. Portioning immediately after roasting keeps the snack fresh and ready to grab.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is warm but not steaming, easy to portion
Step 5
Before serving, add a spoonful of non-fat Greek yogurt on top or mix it in for creaminess. This adds protein and a tangy contrast that balances the sweetness of the pumpkin. Greek yogurt also improves mouthfeel, making the snack feel more indulgent without extra calories or fat. If packing for later, keep the yogurt separate until ready to eat to avoid sogginess.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Yogurt is thick and creamy, enhances flavor and texture
Step 6
For an extra protein boost, sprinkle a tablespoon of unflavored whey protein isolate over the snack before serving. Mix it quickly to avoid clumps. The protein powder blends best with the warm pumpkin and yogurt, and it adds bulk without changing the flavor much. This step is optional but great for post-workout or higher protein needs.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Protein powder fully incorporated, no chalky texture
Step 7
If you want a crunchier texture, broil the snack for the last 2-3 minutes, watching closely to prevent burning. This intensifies the roasted flavors and crisps the chickpeas more. Broiling is a quick fix if the snack feels too soft or if you prefer a more textured bite. Just be careful not to overdo it as the pumpkin can burn easily.
⏱ Time: 2-3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Edges are crisp and golden, chickpeas crunchy
Step 8
To store leftovers, let the snack cool completely before sealing in airtight containers. Refrigerate for up to 4 days. When reheating, warm gently in the microwave or oven to keep the pumpkin moist but avoid overheating which dries the chickpeas. Adding a splash of water or almond milk before reheating can help maintain moisture.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Snack reheated evenly, maintains texture
Step 9
For meal prep, assemble snack boxes with a side of raw celery or cucumber slices to add crunch and volume without calories. This combo makes the snack feel even bigger and more satisfying. Plus, the fresh veggies add a refreshing contrast to the warm roasted pumpkin mix.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Snack boxes look colorful and inviting

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using pumpkin pie filling instead of pure pumpkin puree.

Why it happens People often grab canned pumpkin without checking the label, assuming all pumpkin products are the same. Pie filling contains sugar and spices that add calories and alter flavor.

Fix Always use unsweetened pumpkin puree. If you want spice, add it yourself with measured cinnamon or nutmeg to control calories.

Skipping rinsing canned chickpeas.

Why it happens For convenience, some skip this step, unaware it affects texture and sodium content.

Fix Rinse chickpeas thoroughly under cold water to remove excess starch and sodium, which helps them roast crispier and lowers sodium intake.

Adding too much oil during roasting.

Why it happens Assuming oil is needed in large amounts for flavor or crispness.

Fix Use only 1 tsp olive oil or less. Over-oiling adds unnecessary calories and defeats the purpose of a low-calorie snack.

Not flipping ingredients halfway through roasting.

Why it happens Rushing the process or forgetting to turn ingredients.

Fix Set a timer to remind yourself to flip. This ensures even cooking and prevents burning or sogginess.

Mixing Greek yogurt into the snack too early.

Why it happens Trying to save time by mixing everything at once.

Fix Add yogurt fresh at serving time to keep creaminess and prevent watery separation.

Pro Tips

Spread pumpkin puree thinly on the sheet pan for roasting.
A thin layer allows the pumpkin to roast rather than steam, intensifying its natural sweetness and preventing watery texture. Thick layers trap moisture and make the snack soggy, which diminishes volume and mouthfeel.
Rinse canned chickpeas thoroughly before roasting.
Rinsing removes excess sodium and starch that can make chickpeas gummy. Well-rinsed chickpeas crisp better during roasting, improving texture and flavor without adding calories.
Add Greek yogurt just before serving.
Adding yogurt fresh keeps its creamy texture intact. Mixing it too early causes watery separation, especially in zero-fat yogurts, which reduces the snack’s appeal and volume.
Flip chickpeas and veggies halfway through roasting.
Turning ingredients ensures even browning and crispness. Without flipping, chickpeas may burn on one side while veggies stay undercooked, leading to uneven texture and flavor.
Use smoked paprika and cumin for warm, cozy flavor without calories.
These spices add depth and complexity, making the snack feel indulgent. Spices stimulate taste buds and can reduce cravings for higher-calorie snacks.
Don’t overcrowd the sheet pan.
Crowding traps steam and prevents roasting. Ingredients need space to crisp and caramelize, which enhances texture and flavor while keeping calories low.
Store leftovers in airtight containers and reheat gently.
Proper storage preserves texture and flavor. Gentle reheating prevents drying out chickpeas or making pumpkin mushy, ensuring the snack stays satisfying.

Variations

Spiced Pumpkin and Egg White Bake

Swap chickpeas for whipped egg whites folded into the pumpkin puree with cinnamon and nutmeg for a fluffy, protein-packed bake. This lowers carbs and calories further while keeping volume high. Expect a lighter texture and subtle spice notes.

Note: Whip egg whites to soft peaks before folding gently to maintain airiness. Bake at moderate temperature to avoid drying out.

Pumpkin and Edamame Sheet Pan Snack

Replace chickpeas with shelled edamame for a soy-based protein alternative. Edamame adds a fresh, slightly nutty flavor and similar protein content. Roast as usual but watch timing as edamame cooks faster.

Note: Edamame requires less roasting time; add it halfway through to prevent overcooking.

Savory Pumpkin and Fat-Free Cheese Medley

Add diced fat-free mozzarella or ricotta to the roasted pumpkin and veggies for a creamy texture and extra protein. This variation tastes richer but stays low calorie. Cheese melts during roasting, creating gooey pockets.

Note: Add cheese in the last 5 minutes of roasting to prevent drying out.

Pumpkin Protein Snack with Cinnamon and Stevia

Sweeten the pumpkin puree with cinnamon and a pinch of stevia for a dessert-style snack. Mix in vanilla whey protein isolate for a sweet, creamy treat under 200 calories. Great for those craving a sweet but healthy snack.

Note: Use unflavored or vanilla protein powder and adjust sweetness to taste to avoid aftertaste.

Chunky Pumpkin and Roasted Cauliflower Mix

Add roasted cauliflower florets to the sheet pan for extra crunch and fiber. Cauliflower adds volume with minimal calories and a mild flavor that complements pumpkin. This variation increases vegetable diversity and texture.

Note: Roast cauliflower at the same temperature but cut florets small for even cooking.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Because this snack contains cooked pumpkin and chickpeas, it’s best not to leave it at room temperature for more than 2 hours. Cover loosely if you need to keep it out briefly, but refrigeration is recommended to maintain freshness and prevent bacterial growth. Water separation may occur if warm, so stirring before eating helps.

Refrigerator

Store in airtight containers for up to 4 days. The snack firms up when chilled but tastes great cold or gently reheated. Volume and texture hold well, though the pumpkin may soften slightly. Adding fresh yogurt at serving time keeps it creamy.

Freezer

Freeze in portioned airtight containers for up to 2 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge. Texture may become slightly watery after freezing due to pumpkin’s high moisture, so stir well and add a splash of almond milk if needed when reheating.

Reheating

Warm gently in the microwave or oven at medium heat to avoid drying. Avoid overheating which can make chickpeas hard and pumpkin mushy. Adding a teaspoon of water or unsweetened almond milk before reheating helps maintain moisture. Do not reheat with yogurt added.

Make Ahead

Prepare the full sheet pan and portion into containers. Store pumpkin mixture and veggies together but keep yogurt separate until serving. This keeps the snack fresh and prevents sogginess. Perfect for meal prep Sundays to grab and go all week.

FAQ

Can I use canned pumpkin pie filling instead of pumpkin puree?
It’s best to avoid canned pumpkin pie filling because it contains added sugars and spices that increase calories and alter flavor. Use plain unsweetened pumpkin puree for accurate macros and authentic taste.
What protein powder works best in this recipe?
Unflavored or vanilla whey protein isolate is ideal due to its low calorie and high protein content. Avoid blends with maltodextrin or added fillers that add calories. Whey isolate mixes well with yogurt and pumpkin without chalkiness.
How do I prevent the snack from being watery?
Spread pumpkin thinly and roast at a high temperature to evaporate excess moisture. Don’t add yogurt until serving. Also, flipping ingredients halfway helps even moisture release and crispness.
Can I make this snack vegan?
Yes! Swap Greek yogurt for a plant-based zero-fat yogurt and omit any dairy protein powders. Use chickpeas or edamame for protein. Note that texture and flavor will differ slightly.
How long does this snack keep in the fridge?
Stored properly in airtight containers, it lasts up to 4 days. Flavors may intensify and texture soften slightly but it remains tasty and filling.
Is this snack good for post-workout?
Absolutely! It provides a solid protein hit with carbs and fiber to replenish glycogen and aid recovery, all while keeping calories low.
Can I add nuts or seeds for extra texture?
You can sprinkle a small amount (1 tbsp) of seeds like pumpkin or sunflower seeds for crunch, but be mindful of calories as nuts and seeds are calorie-dense.
What if I don’t have a sheet pan?
You can use a baking dish but spread ingredients thinly for even roasting. A sheet pan works best for airflow and crispness.
