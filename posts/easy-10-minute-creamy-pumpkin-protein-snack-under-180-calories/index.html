Easy 10-Minute Creamy Pumpkin Protein Snack Under 180 Calories

This creamy pumpkin snack blends zero-fat Greek yogurt with pumpkin puree for a filling, high-protein treat that clocks in under 180 calories. I love how the pumpkin adds natural sweetness and fiber, making it perfect for an afternoon pick-me-up or post-workout fuel!

What You'll Need

Approximately 1 large bowl per serving, 6 snack servings, about 1.5 cups volume each

  • 1 cup zero-fat Greek yogurt, chilled
  • 3/4 cup canned pumpkin puree, unsweetened
  • 2 scoops vanilla whey protein isolate powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon pumpkin pie spice
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 tablespoon powdered erythritol or preferred zero-calorie sweetener
  • 1/4 teaspoon xanthan gum (optional, for creaminess and thickness)
  • Ice cubes (optional, to add volume and chill)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by combining the zero-fat Greek yogurt and pumpkin puree in a large mixing bowl. Use a whisk or hand mixer to blend them thoroughly until smooth. This step is crucial because the yogurt provides creaminess and protein, while the pumpkin adds fiber and natural sweetness. Whisking well ensures the texture is uniform and prevents lumps. Avoid rushing this step — if you don’t mix fully, you’ll get uneven pockets of pumpkin or yogurt that affect mouthfeel.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Smooth, creamy mixture with no visible lumps or streaks of pumpkin
Step 2
Add the vanilla whey protein isolate powder, pumpkin pie spice, vanilla extract, and powdered erythritol to the bowl. Stir gently at first, then use a hand mixer on low speed to incorporate the protein powder fully. Protein powder can clump if not mixed well, especially in low-fat mixtures, so take your time. If you skip this, you’ll end up with chalky bits. The protein powder hydrates and thickens the snack, giving it a rich protein boost without calories.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture thickens slightly and is free of dry protein clumps
Step 3
If you want extra creaminess and thickness, sprinkle in the xanthan gum slowly while mixing on low speed. Xanthan gum is a zero-calorie thickener that mimics fat’s mouthfeel, so it’s perfect here. Add it gradually to avoid over-thickening, which can make the texture gummy. This step helps maintain volume and prevents watery separation common in zero-fat dairy snacks.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Mixture holds shape well and feels velvety without being sticky
Step 4
For a chilled, voluminous effect, add a few ice cubes and blend the mixture again briefly. The ice adds bulk and a refreshing chill without calories. This technique also helps create a light, fluffy texture that feels indulgent but stays low-calorie. Don’t over-blend or the ice will melt too much and water down the snack.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Snack is cold, creamy, and slightly airy with visible volume
Step 5
Taste the mixture and adjust sweetness if needed by adding a pinch more erythritol. Because protein powders and pumpkin can sometimes dull sweetness, this final tweak ensures the snack tastes balanced. Avoid adding too much sweetener to keep calories low and prevent aftertaste.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Sweetness is pleasant but not overpowering or artificial
Step 6
Divide the creamy pumpkin mixture evenly into six serving bowls or containers. Using uniform portions helps with calorie tracking and meal prep. This recipe’s large volume means you get a satisfying portion each time, perfect for beating hunger pangs without guilt.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Each container holds about 1.5 cups of creamy snack
Step 7
Chill the servings in the refrigerator for at least 30 minutes before eating. Chilling firms the texture, allowing flavors to meld and the protein to set. If you eat it right away, it might feel a bit loose or watery. The cold temperature also enhances the creamy mouthfeel.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Snack feels thick and creamy, not runny, when scooped
Step 8
When ready to eat, stir gently to fluff the snack if any liquid has separated. This is common with low-fat dairy mixtures but doesn’t affect taste. Fluffing restores volume and texture, making it feel like you’re eating more than you actually are.
⏱ Time: 30 seconds
✓ Doneness: Snack looks light and fluffy again, no watery layer
Step 9
Enjoy immediately as a post-workout snack, afternoon energy boost, or part of your meal prep routine. Store leftovers in airtight containers to maintain freshness and texture. This snack is best eaten cold but can be brought to room temperature if preferred.
⏱ Time: Immediate
✓ Doneness: Snack tastes fresh, creamy, and satisfying

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the xanthan gum and ending with a watery snack.

Why it happens Many assume zero-fat yogurt and protein powder will thicken enough on their own, but without a binder, the mixture separates quickly.

Fix Add a small amount of xanthan gum or another hydrocolloid to stabilize the texture and maintain creaminess without adding calories.

Using full-fat yogurt and increasing calories unexpectedly.

Why it happens People often grab whatever yogurt is on hand without checking fat content, which can double calories per serving.

Fix Always use zero-fat Greek yogurt to keep calories low and texture right for volume eating.

Not mixing protein powder thoroughly, causing chalkiness.

Why it happens Rushing the mixing step or adding powder all at once leads to dry clumps and gritty texture.

Fix Mix protein powder slowly into wet ingredients, using a whisk or hand mixer, until fully hydrated and smooth.

Pro Tips

Whisk protein powder slowly into yogurt to avoid clumps.
Protein powder tends to clump when added too quickly or in dry form. Slowly mixing it into the wet ingredients ensures proper hydration and smooth texture. This technique prevents chalkiness and improves mouthfeel in low-fat, low-calorie snacks.
Add xanthan gum gradually to control thickness.
Xanthan gum is a powerful thickener, so adding it bit by bit lets you achieve the perfect creamy consistency without making the snack gummy or sticky. This control is key for maintaining volume and texture in fat-free recipes.
Chill the snack before eating for best texture.
Cold temperatures firm up protein-based mixtures, enhancing creaminess and flavor. Eating it immediately can feel watery or loose. Chilling also helps flavors meld and the protein to set, improving overall satisfaction.

Variations

Chocolate Pumpkin Protein Snack

Swap vanilla protein powder for chocolate flavor and add 1 tablespoon unsweetened cocoa powder. This adds rich chocolate notes while keeping calories low and protein high. Expect a slightly thicker texture due to cocoa powder’s absorbency, but the flavor is indulgent and perfect for chocolate lovers.

Note: Mix cocoa powder with protein powder before adding to ensure even distribution and prevent clumping.

Pumpkin Spice Latte Protein Bowl

Add 1 teaspoon instant coffee granules and increase pumpkin pie spice to 1 teaspoon for a latte-inspired snack. This variation gives a caffeine boost and warm spice flavor, great for mornings or afternoon slumps. Macros stay similar, but flavor is more complex.

Note: Dissolve coffee granules in a tablespoon of warm water before mixing to avoid grainy texture.

Vegan Pumpkin Protein Snack

Use a plant-based protein powder and coconut yogurt alternative (light, unsweetened) instead of Greek yogurt. Texture will be slightly less creamy and protein content may be lower, but it’s a tasty vegan-friendly option. Add a pinch of guar gum as xanthan substitute if needed.

Note: Adjust sweetener amount as plant-based yogurts can be less tangy and sweeter than Greek yogurt.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This creamy pumpkin snack is best kept chilled and should not be left at room temperature for more than 1 hour due to the dairy content. Exposure to warmth can cause separation and spoilage, so keep it covered and refrigerated when not eating.

Refrigerator

Store in airtight containers for up to 3 days. The snack firms up and flavors meld when chilled. Texture remains creamy with occasional gentle stirring before serving. It tastes best cold but can be brought to room temperature briefly if preferred.

Freezer

Freeze the snack in airtight containers for up to 1 month. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator. Texture may become slightly icy or less creamy after freezing, so stir well before eating. Avoid refreezing to maintain quality.

Reheating

This snack is meant to be eaten cold or at room temperature. Reheating is not recommended as heat can cause separation of protein and dairy components, negatively affecting texture and flavor.

Make Ahead

Prepare the full mixture up to 2 days in advance and store in the fridge. Add ice cubes and blend fresh just before serving to restore volume and chill. Portion into containers for easy grab-and-go snacks throughout the week.

FAQ

Can I use flavored protein powder instead of vanilla?
Yes, you can substitute any flavored whey protein isolate, but be mindful of added sugars or calories in flavored powders. Vanilla works best for a neutral base, but chocolate or spice-flavored powders can add fun twists. Adjust sweetener accordingly to balance flavors.
Why is my snack watery after mixing?
Watery texture often happens if xanthan gum is omitted or not enough protein powder is used. Xanthan gum acts as a thickener to stabilize the mixture. Also, not chilling the snack before eating can make it feel loose. Try adding a small amount of xanthan gum and refrigerate before serving.
Can I replace Greek yogurt with cottage cheese?
Cottage cheese can be used but will change texture and flavor. It’s chunkier and less smooth, so blend well if you try it. Protein content is similar, but cottage cheese may add more sodium. Use low-fat or fat-free varieties to keep calories low.
How long does this snack keep in the fridge?
Stored in an airtight container, it keeps well for up to 3 days. Flavors improve as it chills, but texture may separate slightly. Stir gently before eating to restore creaminess.
Is this snack suitable for weight loss?
Absolutely! It’s high in protein to support muscle retention and satiety, low in calories, and volumized with fiber-rich pumpkin. Eating a large portion for under 180 calories helps you feel full and satisfied, making it easier to stick to a calorie deficit.
Can I add nuts or seeds for crunch?
You can add a small sprinkle of chopped nuts or seeds for texture, but keep in mind they add calories and fat. Use sparingly (1 teaspoon) to maintain the low-calorie profile.
What if I don’t have xanthan gum?
If you don’t have xanthan gum, you can omit it but expect a thinner texture that may separate faster. Alternatively, a small amount of chia seeds ground finely can help thicken, but will add some calories and change texture slightly.
Can I make this snack vegan?
Yes, by using plant-based protein powder and non-dairy yogurt alternatives like coconut or almond yogurt. Texture and protein content will differ, so adjust sweeteners and thickeners accordingly.
