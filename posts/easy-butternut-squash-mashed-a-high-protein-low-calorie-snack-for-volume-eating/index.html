Easy Butternut Squash Mashed: A High-Protein Low-Calorie Snack for Volume Eating

Chef
Published on October 15, 2026
10 min Prep Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty
4.7 (28 ratings)✓ Thanks for rating!

This creamy mashed butternut squash snack is surprisingly high in protein and low in calories, perfect for when you want a filling treat without guilt. Using egg whites to boost protein and volume makes it ideal for afternoon cravings or post-workout refueling.

Introduction

I’ve always struggled with feeling hungry on low-calorie diets, especially during my afternoon slump when I want something filling but not heavy. That’s why I started experimenting with mashed butternut squash as a snack base. It’s naturally low calorie, packed with fiber, and when combined with egg whites and zero-fat Greek yogurt, it turns into a creamy, protein-rich snack that feels like a treat but fits my macros perfectly. I tried making it several times before getting the texture right — my first batch was too watery because I skipped the xanthan gum, which helps thicken without adding calories or fat. I also learned that using a good quality zero-fat Greek yogurt keeps the mash tangy and smooth without any weird aftertaste. Now I eat this giant bowl almost every afternoon, and it keeps me full for hours without any guilt. Plus, it’s super easy to throw together, and the portion looks huge, so it really helps with volume eating when I’m cutting. If you hate tiny portions or boring protein bars, this is a great alternative that tastes way better and feels like comfort food.

What You'll Need

Approximately 1 cup volume per serving, 6 snack servings total

  • 2 cups cooked butternut squash, mashed
  • 3/4 cup liquid egg whites
  • 1/2 cup zero-fat Greek yogurt
  • 1 teaspoon xanthan gum (optional, for creaminess)
  • 1/4 teaspoon garlic powder
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • Stevia or monk fruit sweetener to taste (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start with cooked butternut squash that's been mashed until smooth. You want it to be free of lumps for the best texture. Using cooked squash adds natural sweetness and fiber, which helps with volume eating. Be sure to drain any excess water if you steamed it to avoid a watery final mash. The goal here is a creamy but thick base that holds together well once the protein and thickeners are added.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Smooth, lump-free mashed squash with no visible excess liquid.
Step 2
Slowly whisk in the liquid egg whites to the mashed squash. Egg whites add fluffiness and a big protein boost without calories or fat. Whisking them in gradually helps prevent clumps and ensures a uniform mixture. This also helps the egg whites hydrate properly, which is key to avoiding chalkiness. If you rush this step, the texture can become grainy or separate.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Uniform pale orange mixture with no streaks of egg whites.
Step 3
Fold in the zero-fat Greek yogurt carefully. This adds creaminess and a slight tang that balances the natural sweetness of the squash. Use a spoon or spatula to mix gently so you maintain some airiness. The yogurt also helps prevent the protein powder or egg whites from tasting chalky by adding acidity and moisture.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Creamy, smooth texture with no separation.
Step 4
Add the xanthan gum if using. This step is optional but highly recommended for a thick, creamy texture without adding calories or fat. Sprinkle it evenly over the mixture and whisk quickly to avoid clumps. Xanthan gum acts as a hydrocolloid that mimics fat’s mouthfeel, so your snack feels indulgent without the calories.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Mixture thickens slightly and feels velvety to the touch.
Step 5
Season with garlic powder, salt, and pepper to taste. These simple seasonings bring out the natural flavors and add savory depth without extra calories. Adjust slowly and taste as you go to avoid overpowering the squash’s delicate flavor.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Balanced savory aroma and taste without any one spice dominating.
Step 6
If you want a slightly sweet version, add a pinch of stevia or monk fruit sweetener. These zero-calorie sweeteners avoid the aftertaste common in low-fat dairy mixtures when overused. Start with a small amount and adjust based on your preference.
⏱ Time: 30 seconds
✓ Doneness: Subtle sweetness that complements but doesn’t overpower the squash.
Step 7
Chill the mash in the refrigerator for at least 30 minutes before serving. This step firms up the texture, allowing the xanthan gum and proteins to bind fully. Eating it cold also enhances the creamy mouthfeel and keeps the snack refreshing.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Snack is thickened and holds shape when scooped.
Step 8
Before serving, give the mash a gentle stir to restore fluffiness. This also breaks up any slight gel formation from the xanthan gum. Portion into bowls or meal prep containers, making sure each serving is about 1 cup for volume eating satisfaction.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Fluffy, creamy texture with no watery separation.
Step 9
Enjoy immediately or pack for later. This snack is great cold or room temperature, perfect for meal prep or a quick protein hit anytime. If you reheat, do so gently and avoid microwaving too long to prevent separation.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Snack retains texture and flavor after reheating or chilling.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping xanthan gum leading to watery texture.

Why it happens People often underestimate the importance of thickeners in low-fat, high-water recipes and assume the mash will thicken on its own.

Fix Add a small amount of xanthan gum (about 1 tsp) and whisk it in thoroughly to bind moisture and proteins, ensuring a creamy, thick texture that holds well.

Using full-fat yogurt and increasing calories unintentionally.

Why it happens Some assume any Greek yogurt works, not realizing full-fat versions add significant calories and fat, defeating the low-calorie goal.

Fix Always choose 0% fat Greek yogurt with no added sugars to keep calories low and maintain the snack’s light texture.

Over-sweetening with stevia causing bitterness.

Why it happens Zero-calorie sweeteners can taste bitter if used excessively, and people sometimes add too much to mask flavors.

Fix Add sweeteners sparingly, taste as you go, and balance with natural squash sweetness and seasoning.

Not draining cooked squash well causing runny mash.

Why it happens Water from steaming or boiling squash dilutes the mash, making it too thin and less satisfying.

Fix After cooking, press squash gently with a paper towel or drain in a fine mesh to remove excess water before mashing.

Rushing egg white incorporation causing clumps.

Why it happens Adding egg whites too fast can cause uneven hydration and grainy texture.

Fix Whisk egg whites in slowly and steadily, ensuring a smooth, uniform mixture.

Pro Tips

Whisk egg whites gradually into the squash to avoid clumps.
Gradual incorporation allows the egg whites to hydrate and disperse evenly, preventing grainy or chalky textures. This technique ensures a smooth, fluffy mash that holds together well without separation.
Use xanthan gum sparingly but don’t skip it if you want creaminess.
Xanthan gum thickens the mixture by binding water and proteins, mimicking fat’s mouthfeel without adding calories. Skipping it often leads to watery or runny texture, which reduces snack satisfaction.
Chill the mash before serving to firm up texture.
Refrigeration allows proteins and hydrocolloids to set, creating a denser, creamier texture. Eating it cold enhances mouthfeel and helps maintain volume, making the snack more filling.
Avoid over-sweetening with zero-calorie sweeteners.
Too much stevia or monk fruit can cause bitterness or an artificial aftertaste. Adding sweeteners gradually and tasting as you go keeps flavors balanced and natural.
Drain cooked squash well to prevent watery mash.
Excess moisture dilutes the protein and thickener effect, resulting in a runny final product. Proper draining ensures the mash is thick and creamy.
Use high-quality zero-fat Greek yogurt without stabilizers.
Some yogurts contain additives that affect texture or flavor. Pure Greek yogurt provides natural acidity and creaminess that improves protein powder hydration and prevents chalkiness.
Stir gently before serving to restore fluffiness.
Refrigeration can cause slight gel formation from xanthan gum. A gentle stir breaks this up, giving the mash a light, airy texture that feels indulgent.

Variations

Savory Herb Butternut Mash

Add fresh chopped herbs like rosemary and thyme plus a pinch of smoked paprika for a savory twist. This variation keeps calories low but adds aromatic depth and a slightly earthy flavor. Protein and texture remain the same, making it perfect for a snack or side dish.

Note: Fresh herbs release moisture, so reduce added liquids slightly to maintain thickness.

Sweet Cinnamon Maple Butternut Mash

Mix in cinnamon and a splash of sugar-free maple syrup for a dessert-like snack. This version is still low calorie but offers a comforting sweetness. Protein stays high thanks to egg whites and Greek yogurt, while the texture remains creamy and fluffy.

Note: Add sweetener gradually to avoid overpowering the natural squash flavor.

Spicy Chipotle Butternut Mash

Incorporate chipotle powder and a squeeze of lime for a smoky, spicy snack. This variation adds bold flavor without extra calories and keeps the protein content intact. Great for those who like a bit of heat.

Note: Adjust spice level carefully to avoid bitterness or overpowering the squash.

Pumpkin Spice Protein Mash

Swap butternut squash with pumpkin puree and add pumpkin pie spices for a seasonal treat. Protein and calories remain similar, and the mash stays creamy with the same technique. A cozy snack for fall afternoons.

Note: Pumpkin has more water, so drain or reduce liquid slightly to maintain texture.

Vegan Protein Butternut Mash

Replace egg whites with aquafaba whipped to soft peaks and use plant-based yogurt for a vegan version. Protein content will be slightly lower but still decent, and the texture remains airy and creamy with xanthan gum helping bind everything.

Note: Aquafaba whips differently than egg whites; whip to soft peaks and fold gently to preserve volume.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Due to the zero-fat dairy and egg whites, this snack should not be kept at room temperature for more than 1 hour. Cover with plastic wrap or store in an airtight container to prevent drying out. Warm temperatures can cause separation and volume loss.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container for up to 3 days. Chilling firms the texture and enhances creaminess. The mash may thicken further; stir gently before serving to restore fluffiness. It tastes best cold or at room temperature.

Freezer

Freeze in airtight containers for up to 1 month. Thaw overnight in the fridge and stir well before eating. Freezing may cause slight texture changes and some watery separation, so consume soon after thawing.

Reheating

If reheating, warm gently in short intervals in the microwave or on low stovetop heat. Avoid overheating to prevent protein separation or curdling. Add a splash of unsweetened almond milk if needed to loosen texture.

Make Ahead

Prepare the mash up to 2 days ahead and store chilled. Add xanthan gum during mixing to maintain texture over time. Portion into containers for easy grab-and-go snacks. Stir before serving to refresh texture.

FAQ

Can I substitute powdered egg whites for liquid egg whites?
Powdered egg whites can work but often absorb liquids differently and may cause a chalkier texture in low-fat recipes. If using powder, rehydrate carefully according to package instructions and whisk thoroughly to avoid clumps. Liquid egg whites are preferred for smoother, fluffier results.
How do I avoid a chalky taste from protein powders in this recipe?
Use a high-quality, unflavored or mildly flavored whey isolate with minimal additives. Mixing protein powder with acidic zero-fat Greek yogurt helps mask chalkiness. Also, add xanthan gum to improve mouthfeel and hydrate protein properly by whisking slowly.
Is this snack suitable for meal prep?
Absolutely! It stores well in the fridge for up to 3 days and can be portioned into containers for easy snacking. Just stir before eating to restore fluffiness. Avoid freezing if possible for best texture, but if you do, thaw slowly overnight.
Can I eat this while cutting or on a calorie deficit?
Yes! This snack is designed to be high in protein and low in calories, helping you feel full and maintain muscle during a calorie deficit. The large portion size also helps satisfy hunger without overeating.
What if I don’t have xanthan gum?
You can omit it but expect a thinner, less creamy texture that may separate more easily. Alternatively, try a small amount of chia seeds soaked and blended, but this will change the texture and slightly increase calories.
How do I count calories accurately for this snack?
Measure ingredients precisely, especially the protein sources and yogurt. Use nutrition labels from your specific brands and track in apps like MyFitnessPal. Remember that cooking methods like steaming squash don’t add calories but draining excess water is important.
Can I add other vegetables to the mash?
Yes, low-calorie veggies like cauliflower or zucchini can be steamed and mashed with the squash to increase volume and fiber. Adjust seasonings accordingly and monitor moisture to keep texture creamy.
Will this snack keep me full for long?
Thanks to its high protein and fiber content, this mash provides lasting satiety. The volume eating effect also helps stretch your stomach, signaling fullness and reducing cravings between meals.
