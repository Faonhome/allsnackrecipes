Easy 10-Minute High-Protein Low-Cal Snack with Quick Butternut Squash

Chef
Published on September 3, 2026
10 min Prep Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty
4.7 (39 ratings)✓ Thanks for rating!

This quick butternut squash snack blends creamy roasted squash with high-protein cottage cheese for a filling, low-calorie treat. The natural sweetness of squash pairs perfectly with protein-packed dairy, making it great for afternoon hunger or post-workout refuel.

Introduction

I’ve always struggled with feeling hungry on low-calorie days, especially when I’m cutting or trying to fit in enough protein without overeating. That’s why I love this quick butternut squash snack — it feels like a giant bowl of comfort but packs a serious protein punch without blowing my calories. The natural sweetness of roasted butternut squash makes it feel indulgent, but when mixed with cottage cheese and a scoop of protein powder, it turns into a creamy, filling snack that lasts for hours. One trick I learned was to add a little xanthan gum and egg whites to keep the texture fluffy and thick without adding fat. Plus, the cinnamon and vanilla make it taste like dessert, but it’s totally guilt-free. I tried making this without the xanthan gum once and it turned out watery and sad, so that little addition really helps. I usually prep a batch on Sunday night and keep it in the fridge for quick afternoon snacks or post-workout refueling. It’s way more satisfying than those tiny protein bars, and I love that I can eat a huge portion for under 150 calories. This snack is perfect if you want to feel full and energized without any weird ingredients or added sugar. Plus, it fits easily into MyFitnessPal and my gym bag for on-the-go munching.

What You'll Need

Approximately 1 cup per serving, 6 snack servings, about 2 cups volume per serving

  • 2 cups cooked butternut squash, mashed and cooled
  • 1 cup low-fat cottage cheese (1-2% fat)
  • 1/2 cup egg whites, lightly beaten
  • 1 scoop vanilla whey protein isolate powder
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
  • 1 tbsp powdered erythritol (or preferred zero-calorie sweetener)
  • 1/4 tsp xanthan gum (optional, for thickening)
  • 1 tbsp water or unsweetened almond milk (to adjust consistency)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by mashing your cooked butternut squash until smooth. This creates a creamy base that adds natural sweetness and volume without calories. Make sure the squash is fully cooled to avoid curdling the dairy in the next steps. If the squash is too watery, drain excess liquid to prevent a runny texture. A smooth, thick puree ensures the snack feels indulgent yet light.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: The squash should be smooth with no large chunks and slightly thick, not watery.
Step 2
In a mixing bowl, combine the low-fat cottage cheese and egg whites. Whisk gently until just combined. The egg whites add fluffiness and extra protein without fat, while the cottage cheese brings creaminess and a subtle tang. Avoid overmixing to keep the texture light. This step is key to balancing creaminess with volume in a low-calorie snack.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture should be creamy and slightly airy, not dense or lumpy.
Step 3
Add the vanilla whey protein isolate powder, cinnamon, vanilla extract, and erythritol to the cottage cheese mixture. Fold gently to combine. Protein powder boosts protein content without adding fat or many calories, but be careful to mix it evenly to avoid chalkiness. The cinnamon and vanilla add warmth and sweetness naturally.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: The mixture should be smooth and evenly colored with no visible powder clumps.
Step 4
Fold the mashed butternut squash into the protein and cottage cheese mixture. Use a spatula to gently combine, preserving the airiness from the egg whites. This layering technique keeps the snack light but filling, maximizing volume without extra calories. Mixing thoroughly ensures balanced flavor in every bite.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: The mixture should be uniform with a creamy orange hue and fluffy texture.
Step 5
If using xanthan gum, sprinkle it evenly over the mixture and stir quickly but gently to thicken. This hydrocolloid mimics creaminess and prevents watery separation common in low-fat dairy snacks. Don’t add too much or it can become gummy. This step helps maintain a luscious texture without adding calories or fat.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: The mixture thickens slightly and holds its shape better when scooped.
Step 6
Adjust the consistency by adding water or unsweetened almond milk one tablespoon at a time if the mixture feels too thick. This keeps the snack creamy and easy to eat without adding calories. Be cautious not to add too much liquid to avoid a runny texture that feels less satisfying.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: The final mixture should be creamy but scoopable, not watery or dry.
Step 7
Divide the mixture evenly into six serving bowls or containers. Portion control is important to keep calories in check but the volume will make you feel like you’re eating a lot. Using clear containers helps you appreciate the generous portion size, which is great for meal prep motivation.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Each container holds about 1 cup of fluffy, creamy snack.
Step 8
Chill the servings in the fridge for at least 30 minutes before eating. Chilling firms the texture, allowing flavors to meld and protein powder to hydrate fully. This step prevents chalkiness and improves mouthfeel. If rushed, the snack might feel gritty or too loose.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Snack is firm but creamy, with no gritty texture.
Step 9
Before serving, give the snack a gentle stir to fluff it up again. This reintroduces air and makes it feel fresh. Enjoy cold or at room temperature depending on preference. The snack holds up well for several hours, making it perfect for on-the-go snacking or a post-workout treat.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Snack looks light and fluffy, ready to eat.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping xanthan gum and ending up with a watery snack.

Why it happens People often underestimate how watery low-fat dairy and vegetable mixtures can be, expecting them to thicken naturally. Without a binder, the snack separates and loses volume, making it less satisfying.

Fix Add a small amount (about 1/4 teaspoon) of xanthan gum to the mixture and stir well. This binds water and proteins, creating a thick, creamy texture that holds up well in the fridge.

Using full-fat cottage cheese and increasing calories unintentionally.

Why it happens Some assume full-fat dairy is necessary for creaminess and don’t check labels, which bumps calories and fat beyond snack goals.

Fix Choose low-fat (1-2%) cottage cheese to keep calories down but maintain creaminess. This keeps the snack high-protein and low-calorie as intended.

Mixing protein powder into warm ingredients causing clumps.

Why it happens Protein powders hydrate faster and unevenly in warm liquids, leading to clumping and chalky texture.

Fix Always mix protein powder into chilled or room temperature ingredients slowly, whisking well to ensure smooth incorporation.

Pro Tips

Use chilled ingredients for mixing protein powder to prevent clumps.
Protein powder tends to clump when mixed with warm or room temperature liquids, especially in low-fat mixtures. Using chilled cottage cheese and egg whites slows hydration, allowing the powder to disperse evenly and preventing gritty texture. This results in a smoother, creamier snack that feels indulgent without extra calories.
Add xanthan gum gradually and mix thoroughly to avoid gummy texture.
Xanthan gum thickens quickly even in small amounts. Adding it slowly and mixing well prevents over-thickening and gummy spots. Proper dispersion creates a silky mouthfeel that mimics fat’s creaminess, which is essential in fat-free, high-protein snacks. Ignoring this can ruin texture and make the snack unpleasant.
Let the snack chill for at least 30 minutes before eating.
Chilling allows protein powder to fully hydrate and flavors to meld, improving texture and reducing chalkiness. It also firms the mixture, making it more satisfying to eat. Eating immediately can result in a gritty or runny snack that feels less filling, so patience pays off.

Variations

Pumpkin Spice Protein Bowl

Swap butternut squash for pureed pumpkin and add nutmeg and allspice for a cozy fall flavor. This variation keeps protein and calories similar but offers a different spice profile. Use the same technique for fluffiness and texture.

Note: Pumpkin puree is slightly wetter than squash, so reduce added liquid slightly to maintain thickness.

Savory Herb Butternut Bowl

Skip the sweeteners and cinnamon; add fresh herbs like thyme and rosemary with a pinch of salt and pepper. Use unflavored protein powder or omit it for a savory twist. This makes a great low-calorie, high-protein snack for those avoiding sweet flavors.

Note: Without sweeteners, balance acidity with a splash of lemon juice for brightness.

Vegan Protein Butternut Snack

Replace cottage cheese and egg whites with silken tofu and a plant-based protein powder. Add a pinch of turmeric for color and anti-inflammatory benefits. This variation lowers saturated fat but may have a softer texture.

Note: Blend tofu thoroughly to avoid graininess and adjust xanthan gum to maintain thickness.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This snack should not be left at room temperature for more than 1 hour due to dairy and egg whites. Keep it covered to prevent drying out and bacterial growth. Water separation may occur if left warm, so refrigerate promptly.

Refrigerator

Store in airtight containers for up to 4 days. The snack firms up when chilled, improving texture and flavor melding. Volume holds well, but stirring before eating refreshes fluffiness. Best enjoyed cold or slightly chilled.

Freezer

Freeze in sealed containers for up to 1 month. Thaw overnight in the fridge. Texture may become slightly watery or icy due to high water content; stir well after thawing to restore creaminess. Avoid refreezing.

Reheating

This snack is best eaten cold or at room temperature. Reheating is not recommended as heat can cause separation and loss of fluffiness. If desired, warm gently in short bursts and stir, adding a splash of almond milk if needed.

Make Ahead

Prepare the mixture up to step 7 and store in airtight containers. Add xanthan gum and adjust liquid fresh before chilling to prevent over-thickening. Ideal for meal prep Sundays to have ready-to-eat snacks all week.

FAQ

Can I substitute whey protein isolate with plant-based protein powder?
Yes, you can swap whey isolate for a plant-based protein powder, but expect a slight change in texture and flavor. Plant proteins sometimes absorb liquids differently and may require adjusting the amount of xanthan gum or liquid to maintain creaminess. Also, some plant proteins have a stronger taste, so consider unflavored or vanilla varieties to keep the snack balanced.
How do I prevent the snack from tasting chalky?
To avoid chalkiness, mix protein powder into chilled dairy slowly and whisk thoroughly. Chilling the snack for at least 30 minutes allows the powder to hydrate fully and smooth out. Using acidic dairy like cottage cheese also helps mask chalky notes. Avoid rushing or mixing with warm ingredients.
Is this snack suitable for cutting phases?
Absolutely! This snack is designed to be low in calories but high in protein and volume, making it perfect for cutting. It keeps you full longer, reduces cravings, and fits easily into calorie-controlled meal plans while providing muscle-supporting protein.
Can I use frozen butternut squash?
Yes, frozen butternut squash works well as long as it’s fully thawed and drained of excess water before mashing. This prevents a watery snack and helps maintain the right texture. Fresh or roasted squash can also be used interchangeably.
What if I don’t have xanthan gum?
Xanthan gum helps thicken and stabilize the snack but isn’t absolutely required. Without it, the snack may be slightly runnier and separate over time. You can try substituting a small amount of chia seeds ground finely or gelatin for thickening, but texture will vary.
How long can I store the snack in the fridge?
Stored properly in airtight containers, this snack keeps fresh and tasty for up to 4 days in the refrigerator. The texture may firm up, so stir gently before eating to restore fluffiness.
Can I add nuts or seeds for extra crunch?
You can add a small amount of chopped nuts or seeds for texture, but keep in mind they add calories and fat. Use sparingly (about 1 tsp per serving) to maintain the low-calorie profile.
Is this snack good for post-workout recovery?
Yes, it’s a great post-workout snack since it delivers fast-digesting protein and carbs with low fat, helping muscle recovery and replenishing glycogen without feeling heavy.
