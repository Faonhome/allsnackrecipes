Easy Make-Ahead Low-Calorie High-Protein Brussels Sprouts Snack Boxes

Chef
Published on October 12, 2026
10 min Prep Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty
4.7 (60 ratings)✓ Thanks for rating!

These roasted Brussels sprouts snack boxes pack a surprising protein punch thanks to a sprinkle of fat-free cheese and a side of egg whites. Low in calories but high in volume, they’re perfect for a filling afternoon snack or meal prep when you want to stay full without guilt.

Introduction

I’ve always struggled with feeling hungry on low-calorie days, especially when I’m trying to cut without losing energy. Small snacks just don’t cut it, and protein bars often feel tiny and chalky. That’s why I started making these Brussels sprouts snack boxes. They’re huge in volume, thanks to the naturally bulky sprouts and whipped egg whites, but still low in calories and packed with protein from the fat-free cheese and egg whites. The trick I learned was to roast the sprouts just right to keep them crispy and toss them with a bit of lemon for brightness. I also whip the egg whites lightly and fold them in to add fluff and satiety without adding fat. I once tried this with a different yogurt base and ended up with a watery mess, but switching to egg whites and cheese made all the difference. My friends always ask how I eat so much and still stay on track, and this snack is my secret. It’s perfect for afternoon cravings or meal prep on Sundays when I want something filling but guilt-free.

What You'll Need

6 snack servings, approximately 1.5 cups volume per serving

  • 600 grams fresh Brussels sprouts, trimmed and halved
  • 150 grams fat-free shredded cheese
  • 4 large egg whites, lightly whipped
  • 1 teaspoon olive oil (optional, minimal for roasting)
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1 tablespoon fresh lemon juice
  • Stevia or monk fruit sweetener, optional, to balance bitterness

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by trimming and halving the Brussels sprouts. Toss them lightly with a teaspoon of olive oil, salt, pepper, garlic powder, and smoked paprika. The oil is optional but helps the sprouts roast to a crisp without drying out. Spread them evenly on a baking sheet. Roasting brings out their natural sweetness and reduces bitterness, which makes the snack more enjoyable. Avoid overcrowding the pan to ensure even roasting and crisp edges.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Sprouts should be golden brown on edges and tender inside but still have a slight crunch.
Step 2
While the sprouts roast, lightly whip the egg whites until they form soft peaks. This adds volume and a fluffy texture to the snack, making it more filling without extra calories. Be careful not to overwhip, or they’ll become dry and rubbery. The whipped whites will fold in easily with the other ingredients, helping to bind the snack and provide a protein boost.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Egg whites should hold soft peaks and look glossy, not dry or grainy.
Step 3
Once the Brussels sprouts are roasted and cooled slightly, transfer them to a large mixing bowl. Add the fat-free shredded cheese and fold in gently. The cheese melts slightly from the warmth, adding creaminess and protein without fat. This combination keeps the snack satisfying and prevents dryness common in low-fat recipes.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cheese should be evenly distributed and slightly softened but not melted into a liquid.
Step 4
Gently fold the whipped egg whites into the Brussels sprouts and cheese mixture. This step is key for volume eating: the air in the egg whites creates a fluffy texture that tricks your stomach into feeling fuller. Folding gently keeps the air bubbles intact, so don’t stir vigorously or you’ll lose that lightness.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture should look airy and light with no large clumps.
Step 5
Add a tablespoon of fresh lemon juice to brighten the flavor and counteract any lingering bitterness from the sprouts. If you find the sprouts too bitter, a pinch of stevia or monk fruit sweetener can balance the taste without adding calories. Mix gently to combine all flavors.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: The mixture should have a fresh, tangy aroma and balanced flavor.
Step 6
Divide the mixture evenly into six snack containers or bowls. This portion size gives you a large volume of food for under 200 calories, perfect for meal prep or quick snacks. The generous serving size helps you feel satisfied and less likely to snack on less healthy options later.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Each container should be filled about 1.5 cups with the fluffy Brussels sprouts mixture.
Step 7
Store the snack boxes in the refrigerator if not eating immediately. The chilled temperature helps the egg whites firm up slightly, improving texture. When ready to eat, you can enjoy cold or let sit at room temperature for a few minutes to take the chill off.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Snack should hold shape and not release excess water when chilled.
Step 8
For reheating, if desired, warm gently in the microwave for 20-30 seconds to avoid drying out the egg whites or making the cheese rubbery. Adding a splash of unsweetened almond milk can help maintain creaminess. Avoid overheating which can cause separation or toughness.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Snack should be warm but still moist and fluffy.
Step 9
Enjoy your snack with a side of fresh veggies or a small handful of berries for extra fiber and antioxidants. The combination of protein, fiber, and volume eating keeps you full and energized, perfect for between meals or post-workout refueling.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Snack feels satisfying and filling without heaviness.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping whipping egg whites and mixing directly causes dense texture.

Why it happens Some assume mixing all ingredients is enough, but without whipped egg whites, the snack loses volume and fluffiness, making it less filling and less enjoyable.

Fix Always whip egg whites separately until soft peaks form before folding gently into the mixture. This step traps air and creates a light, fluffy texture essential for volume eating.

Using full-fat cheese or too much oil increases calories significantly.

Why it happens People often add fats for flavor or texture without realizing how many calories they add, which can sabotage a low-calorie snack plan.

Fix Stick to fat-free cheese and minimal oil (1 teaspoon or less). You can skip oil entirely and roast sprouts with a non-stick pan or parchment paper to keep calories low.

Pro Tips

Whip egg whites just until soft peaks form for maximum fluffiness.
Overwhipping egg whites causes them to become dry and grainy, which ruins the light texture needed for volume eating snacks. Soft peaks hold air bubbles that increase volume and create a fluffy mouthfeel, making the snack feel more substantial without added calories.
Roast Brussels sprouts at a high temperature to get crispy edges.
High heat caramelizes the natural sugars in Brussels sprouts, reducing bitterness and adding depth of flavor. Crispy edges provide a satisfying crunch that contrasts nicely with the soft whipped egg whites and melted cheese, enhancing texture and overall enjoyment.
Use fat-free cheese to keep calories low but maintain creaminess.
Fat-free cheese melts better than expected and adds savory flavor without fat calories. It helps bind the mixture and prevents dryness common in low-fat recipes. Using full-fat cheese would increase calories and fat, defeating the low-calorie goal.

Variations

Spicy Buffalo Brussels Sprouts Snack

Add a teaspoon of hot sauce and a pinch of cayenne to the roasted sprouts before mixing with egg whites and cheese. This gives the snack a spicy kick while keeping calories low. Protein and volume remain the same, but flavor is bolder and great for spice lovers.

Note: Adjust seasoning carefully to avoid overpowering the delicate egg white fluffiness; fold in hot sauce gently.

Lemon Herb Brussels with Cottage Cheese

Swap fat-free shredded cheese for low-fat cottage cheese for a creamier texture and slightly different protein profile. Add fresh herbs like dill or parsley with lemon juice for a fresh twist. Calories stay low, and protein remains high.

Note: Cottage cheese adds moisture, so reduce lemon juice slightly to prevent watery texture.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This snack is best consumed within 1 hour at room temperature due to the egg whites and dairy content. If left out longer, moisture separation may occur and texture will degrade. Keep covered to avoid contamination.

Refrigerator

Store in airtight containers for up to 3 days. Chilling firms up the egg whites and cheese, improving texture. The snack tastes great cold or at room temperature after a few minutes out of the fridge.

Freezer

Not recommended to freeze as the high water content in sprouts and egg whites can cause icy texture and separation upon thawing.

Reheating

Warm gently in microwave for 20-30 seconds with a splash of unsweetened almond milk to restore creaminess. Avoid overheating which can toughen egg whites and cause separation.

Make Ahead

Prepare the roasted Brussels sprouts and whip egg whites separately up to 2 days ahead. Store sprouts and egg whites chilled separately. Fold together just before eating or packing for best volume and texture.

FAQ

Can I substitute the egg whites with Greek yogurt?
Greek yogurt can add creaminess and protein but won’t provide the same volume and fluffiness as whipped egg whites. If you use yogurt, expect a denser texture and slightly higher calories depending on fat content. For best volume eating results, stick with egg whites.
How do I avoid the protein powder taste if I add it?
If you choose to add unflavored whey protein isolate, mix it with acidic ingredients like lemon juice or a splash of vinegar to reduce chalkiness. Also, blend protein powder with a small amount of cold liquid first before folding into the mixture to prevent clumping.
Is this snack suitable for meal prep?
Yes! It stores well in the fridge for up to 3 days and holds its volume and flavor. Keep egg whites and sprouts mixed together to maintain texture. Avoid freezing to prevent texture loss.
Can I eat this snack while cutting?
Absolutely. This snack is designed for cutting phases with low calories and high protein to keep you full and preserve muscle. The large portion size helps manage hunger without guilt.
What if my mixture is too watery?
Watery texture usually means the egg whites weren’t whipped enough or the sprouts released too much moisture. Make sure to dry sprouts well before roasting and whip egg whites to soft peaks before folding gently.
Can I add other vegetables?
Sure! Low-calorie veggies like celery or cucumber can be added for extra volume and crunch. Just keep portions moderate to maintain protein focus and calorie limits.
How do I measure calories accurately?
Weigh your ingredients precisely and use nutrition labels or apps like MyFitnessPal. Pay special attention to cheese and oil quantities, as small changes can affect calorie counts significantly.
Will this snack keep me full for long?
Yes, the high protein content combined with fiber-rich Brussels sprouts and volume from whipped egg whites promotes fullness for several hours, reducing the need for frequent snacking.
