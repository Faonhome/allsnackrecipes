Easy Homemade Basil Snack Bowl Packed with Protein and Under 150 Calories

Chef
Published on July 3, 2026
10 min Prep Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty
4.5 (47 ratings)✓ Thanks for rating!

This snack bowl features fresh homemade basil blended into a creamy, high-protein base that’s low in calories but big on flavor. The secret? Using zero-fat Greek yogurt to keep it light yet filling. Perfect for a refreshing afternoon pick-me-up or post-workout boost!

Introduction

I’ve always struggled with feeling hungry on low-calorie days, especially when I’m cutting or trying to keep my energy up between meals. Small snacks just don’t cut it for me — I want something that feels like a real snack without wrecking my macros. That’s why I started making this homemade basil protein bowl. It’s a game-changer! I use zero-fat Greek yogurt as the base to keep it creamy and high in protein but low in calories. The fresh basil adds a bright, herbaceous flavor that makes it feel fancy but it’s so simple to whip up. One trick I learned was adding a little xanthan gum to thicken it up without any fat or calories, which keeps the texture perfect and not watery like some low-fat snacks can get. Plus, blending in egg whites gives it extra fluff and protein punch without heaviness. I usually make a big batch on Sundays for meal prep — it keeps well and is perfect for those afternoon slumps or right after the gym when I want something fresh and filling. My friends always ask how I eat so much and still lose weight, and honestly, this bowl is part of the secret. It’s huge in volume, creamy, and satisfying for under 150 calories. You’ll love how it fills you up without feeling like a diet snack.

What You'll Need

Approximately 1 large bowl per serving, 6 snack servings total, about 2 cups volume per serving

  • 2 cups zero-fat Greek yogurt, chilled
  • 1/2 cup fresh basil leaves, packed
  • 1/2 cup diced cucumber, peeled
  • 1/4 cup egg whites, pasteurized
  • 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice
  • 1 tsp xanthan gum (optional, for thickness)
  • 1 packet stevia or monk fruit sweetener
  • Salt and black pepper to taste

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by washing and roughly chopping the fresh basil leaves and cucumber. The basil is the star here, so make sure it’s fresh and vibrant. Chopping the cucumber adds volume and crunch without adding many calories. Fresh veggies bulk up the snack and keep it light. Avoid over-blending at this stage to preserve some texture.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Basil is finely chopped but not pureed; cucumber pieces are small and even.
Step 2
In a blender, combine the zero-fat Greek yogurt, chopped basil, cucumber, egg whites, lemon juice, and sweetener. Blend on medium speed until smooth and creamy. The lemon juice brightens flavors and helps prevent the protein powder or yogurt from tasting chalky. Blending thoroughly ensures a silky texture.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is homogenous, creamy, with no visible lumps.
Step 3
If using xanthan gum, sprinkle it slowly over the blended mixture while running the blender on low. Xanthan gum thickens the snack without adding calories or fat, preventing watery separation common in low-fat dairy snacks. Add it gradually to avoid clumping.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture thickens noticeably and coats the sides of the blender.
Step 4
Taste the mixture and adjust salt and pepper to your liking. Salt enhances the basil’s aroma and balances the tartness from lemon juice. Be careful not to over-salt since the yogurt already has some sodium.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Balanced flavor with a fresh herbal note and mild tang.
Step 5
Pour the mixture into large bowls or meal prep containers. The volume should be generous, around 2 cups per serving, which helps with satiety. Portioning out now makes it easy to grab and go.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Smooth, thick snack evenly distributed in containers.
Step 6
Chill the bowls in the refrigerator for at least 30 minutes before serving. Chilling firms up the texture and melds the basil flavor throughout. If you eat it too soon, it might feel watery and less satisfying.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Snack is cool, thickened, and flavors are well blended.
Step 7
Before eating, give the bowl a gentle stir to refresh the texture. If it’s too thick, add a splash of unsweetened almond milk to loosen it. This keeps the snack creamy without adding calories.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Creamy, scoopable texture that’s not runny.
Step 8
For an extra boost, sprinkle some chopped fresh herbs or a few diced cucumbers on top for crunch and color. It’s a great way to add volume and freshness without calories.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Visually appealing garnish that complements the bowl.
Step 9
Enjoy immediately or pack it into your gym bag or office drawer for a refreshing, high-protein snack that keeps hunger at bay without the guilt. This bowl is perfect for volume eating when you want to feel full but stay on track.
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: Snack eaten fresh, feeling satisfying and light.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping xanthan gum and ending up with a watery, separated snack.

Why it happens People often assume yogurt alone will thicken the snack, but low-fat yogurt lacks fat to stabilize the texture, causing water separation.

Fix Add about 1 tsp xanthan gum gradually while blending to thicken and stabilize. This prevents watery separation and keeps the snack creamy and satisfying.

Using full-fat yogurt which increases calories and reduces volume.

Why it happens Some think full-fat yogurt tastes better and is healthier, but it adds unwanted calories and reduces the portion size you can eat guilt-free.

Fix Always use zero-fat Greek yogurt to maximize protein and volume while keeping calories low.

Over-blending basil and cucumber until mushy, losing texture.

Why it happens It’s tempting to blend everything smooth, but this removes the refreshing crunch and volume that make the snack feel substantial.

Fix Pulse basil and cucumber just enough to combine but keep small pieces for crunch and volume.

Not chilling the snack long enough, resulting in thin texture.

Why it happens Low-fat mixtures need time to firm up in the fridge; eating too soon means watery, less satisfying texture.

Fix Chill the snack for at least 30 minutes before eating to allow flavors to meld and texture to thicken.

Using too much sweetener causing an artificial aftertaste.

Why it happens Zero-calorie sweeteners can taste bitter if overused, overpowering the fresh basil and yogurt flavors.

Fix Add sweetener gradually and taste as you go, aiming just to balance tartness without sweetness dominating.

Pro Tips

Add xanthan gum slowly while blending to avoid clumps and achieve perfect creaminess.
Xanthan gum is a powerful thickener; adding it all at once causes lumps that ruin texture. Slowly sprinkling it while blending disperses it evenly, mimicking fat's smooth mouthfeel without calories. This step is crucial for a creamy, non-watery snack.
Use chilled ingredients to help the yogurt mixture thicken faster and taste fresher.
Cold temperatures help thicken the yogurt and egg white mixture naturally, improving texture without the need for extra thickeners. It also prevents separation and keeps the basil flavor bright and fresh, making the snack more enjoyable.
Blend the basil and cucumber just enough to keep some texture for a refreshing crunch.
Over-blending can create a mushy, less appealing texture. Leaving small bits of cucumber and basil adds volume and crunch, which enhances satiety and makes the snack feel more substantial despite low calories.
Add lemon juice last to brighten flavors and reduce any chalkiness from protein or yogurt.
Acidic ingredients like lemon juice balance the sometimes chalky or tangy taste of low-fat dairy and protein powders. Adding it last preserves its fresh aroma and ensures the snack tastes vibrant rather than flat.
Use pasteurized egg whites for safety and to avoid cooking during blending.
Raw egg whites can carry salmonella risk. Pasteurized liquid egg whites are safe to consume raw and blend well into cold mixtures, adding protein and fluff without altering flavor or texture.
Portion the snack into individual containers for easy grab-and-go convenience.
Pre-portioning helps control calories and makes it easier to stick to your macros. It also keeps the snack fresh longer and prevents overeating, which is key when managing calorie intake.
If the snack becomes watery after chilling, stir in a little more xanthan gum or strain excess liquid.
Low-fat dairy snacks can separate as they sit, making the texture less appealing. Xanthan gum stabilizes the mixture, but if separation occurs, a quick stir or straining helps restore creaminess and volume.

Variations

Spicy Basil Protein Bowl

Add a pinch of cayenne pepper and a dash of smoked paprika for a spicy twist. This variation keeps calories low but adds a warming kick that pairs surprisingly well with fresh basil. Expect a slightly bolder flavor and the same creamy texture.

Note: Spices won’t affect texture but add complexity. Adjust spice level to taste to avoid overpowering the basil.

Cucumber Mint Protein Snack

Swap half the basil for fresh mint leaves and add extra diced cucumber for a cooling, refreshing snack. This version is lighter in herb flavor but still high in protein and low in calories, perfect for hot days.

Note: Mint is softer than basil; pulse gently to avoid turning the mixture too green or mushy.

Lemon Basil Protein Smoothie Bowl

Blend the mixture with ice cubes and a splash of unsweetened almond milk for a smoothie bowl texture. This variation is great for summer mornings and keeps protein high with a refreshing lemony basil flavor.

Note: Add ice slowly to avoid thinning the mixture too much; adjust xanthan gum accordingly.

Vegan Basil Protein Bowl

Replace Greek yogurt and egg whites with silken tofu and vegan protein powder. This keeps protein high and calories low while offering a similar creamy texture. Basil flavor shines through with a plant-based twist.

Note: Silken tofu blends smoothly but may need extra xanthan gum for thickness.

Herbed Basil and Chive Protein Dip

Add chopped fresh chives and garlic powder to create a savory dip perfect for dipping raw veggies. This variation is still high protein and low calorie but leans savory, great for parties or snacks.

Note: Garlic powder intensifies flavor; add gradually to avoid bitterness.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This snack should not be left at room temperature for more than 30 minutes due to the dairy and egg whites. Cover with plastic wrap or a lid to prevent drying out if left briefly.

Refrigerator

Store in airtight containers for up to 3 days. The snack firms up and flavors meld when chilled. Texture stays creamy, though slight separation can occur—just stir before eating. Best served cold or at room temp.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as the high-water content causes icy texture and separation. If freezing, use airtight freezer-safe containers and thaw overnight in the fridge, stirring well before eating.

Reheating

This snack is best eaten cold or at room temperature. Reheating can cause separation and loss of creaminess due to egg whites and xanthan gum. If needed, warm gently and stir in a splash of almond milk.

Make Ahead

Prepare the mixture up to 2 days in advance and store in the fridge. Add xanthan gum and fresh herbs last to keep texture fresh. Portion into containers for easy grab-and-go snacks throughout the week.

FAQ

Can I substitute the zero-fat Greek yogurt with regular yogurt?
Regular yogurt has more fat and calories, which will increase the snack’s calorie count and reduce volume. The texture may also be less thick and creamy. If you use regular yogurt, expect a richer but less diet-friendly snack.
Why is xanthan gum important in this recipe?
Xanthan gum acts as a thickener and stabilizer, preventing watery separation common in low-fat dairy snacks. It mimics the creamy mouthfeel of fat without adding calories, keeping the snack satisfying and smooth.
Can I use fresh herbs other than basil?
Yes! Mint, parsley, or chives work well and add different flavor profiles. Just adjust quantities to taste since some herbs are stronger. Basil remains classic, but feel free to experiment.
How long does this snack keep in the fridge?
Stored in airtight containers, it keeps well for up to 3 days. Flavors meld nicely, and the texture remains creamy with a quick stir before eating.
Is it safe to eat raw egg whites in this recipe?
Use pasteurized liquid egg whites to avoid any risk of salmonella. These are safe to consume raw and blend well without cooking.
Can I add protein powder to boost protein further?
Yes, a neutral or vanilla whey isolate powder can be added. Blend it with the yogurt and liquids to avoid chalkiness. Adjust sweetener accordingly.
What if my snack turns out watery?
This usually means not enough xanthan gum or not chilling long enough. Stir in a bit more xanthan gum and chill again. Avoid over-blending watery ingredients.
Is this snack suitable for meal prep?
Absolutely! It stores well in the fridge for several days and is perfect for quick, high-protein snacks during busy weeks.
