Easy One Pot Peach Cobbler Recipe with a Protein Boost Under 200 Calories

Published on August 5, 2026
10 min Prep Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty
4.5 (20 ratings)✓ Thanks for rating!

This one pot peach cobbler adds a surprising protein punch with cottage cheese and egg whites, keeping it light and low calorie. It’s perfect for a guilt-free dessert or snack that fills you up without weighing you down, especially during cutting or meal prep days.

Introduction

I’ve always struggled with feeling hungry on low-calorie days, especially when I just want a big, satisfying snack that won’t ruin my macros. This one pot peach cobbler became my go-to because it’s huge in volume but still under 200 calories per serving. What makes it special? The combo of cottage cheese and egg whites gives it a creamy, fluffy texture without any added fat. Plus, I add xanthan gum to keep it from getting watery, which was a problem in my first batch. The peaches add natural sweetness and juiciness, making it taste like a treat instead of a diet snack. I usually whip this up on Sundays for the week, and it keeps me full through those afternoon cravings. My friends always ask how I manage to eat so much and still lose weight — this cobbler is my secret weapon! It’s like eating dessert but with a protein punch that actually fuels my workouts and recovery.

What You'll Need

Approximately 1 cup per serving, 6 servings total, perfect for meal prep containers

  • 2 cups peeled and sliced fresh peaches
  • 1 cup zero-fat Greek yogurt
  • 3/4 cup low-fat cottage cheese, blended smooth
  • 4 large egg whites
  • 1 scoop vanilla whey protein isolate powder
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 tbsp monk fruit sweetener
  • 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 tsp xanthan gum (optional, for thickness)
  • 1 tsp lemon juice

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by blending the low-fat cottage cheese until smooth to avoid lumps in your cobbler. This step is crucial because cottage cheese can be grainy, and a smooth base helps create a creamy texture without adding fat. Use a food processor or high-speed blender for best results. If you skip this, you’ll end up with a less pleasant texture that feels chunky rather than fluffy.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Smooth, creamy cottage cheese mixture with no visible curds
Step 2
In a large bowl, whisk the egg whites until soft peaks form. This adds volume and fluffiness to the cobbler without extra calories. Be careful not to overbeat or the whites will become dry and crumbly, which will ruin the texture. The lightness from the egg whites balances the density of cottage cheese and yogurt.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Egg whites hold soft peaks and look glossy
Step 3
Gently fold the blended cottage cheese, Greek yogurt, protein powder, vanilla extract, monk fruit sweetener, cinnamon, and lemon juice into the whipped egg whites. Folding carefully preserves the air whipped into the egg whites, keeping the mixture light. Stirring too vigorously will deflate the mixture and make the cobbler dense.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is homogenous, light, and fluffy without streaks
Step 4
If using, sprinkle xanthan gum evenly over the mixture and whisk quickly to avoid clumps. Xanthan gum acts as a thickener and stabilizer, preventing watery separation since this recipe has zero fat. Without it, the cobbler might become runny after chilling.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Mixture slightly thickens and feels smooth without lumps
Step 5
Fold in the fresh peach slices gently to keep their shape intact. This adds juicy bursts and natural sweetness. Avoid overmixing here to prevent the peaches from breaking down and turning the mixture watery.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Peach slices are evenly distributed without being crushed
Step 6
Divide the mixture evenly into six serving bowls or meal prep containers. Portion control is key to keeping calories in check while still enjoying a generous serving size. Each portion is about 1 cup, which looks huge compared to typical protein snacks.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Containers filled evenly with a fluffy, creamy mixture
Step 7
Refrigerate the cobbler for at least 2 hours before eating. Chilling firms up the texture as the protein and xanthan gum set, improving creaminess and mouthfeel. Eating it immediately can feel watery and less satisfying.
⏱ Time: 120 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cobbler is firm but still creamy and holds its shape
Step 8
Before serving, optionally sprinkle a pinch of cinnamon or a few fresh peach slices on top for extra flavor and presentation. This adds a fresh, inviting aroma and a pop of color without adding calories.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Cobbler looks appealing and smells fragrant
Step 9
Enjoy chilled as a post-workout snack, afternoon pick-me-up, or light dessert. The high protein content helps muscle recovery and keeps you full longer, while the low calories let you indulge without guilt. Perfect for volume eating on a deficit!
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: Satisfied appetite and no hunger for hours

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the blending of cottage cheese and ending up with a lumpy texture.

Why it happens Many assume cottage cheese can be used as-is, but its curds can create an unpleasant mouthfeel in creamy desserts.

Fix Always blend cottage cheese until smooth before mixing with other ingredients. This ensures a creamy, uniform texture that mimics fat without calories.

Overwhipping egg whites until dry and crumbly.

Why it happens It’s easy to get carried away when whipping egg whites, thinking more volume is better.

Fix Stop whipping once soft peaks form. This retains moisture and creates a light, fluffy texture essential for this cobbler.

Not using xanthan gum, resulting in watery separation after chilling.

Why it happens Zero-fat recipes lack natural thickeners, so liquid separates if stabilizers are omitted.

Fix Add a small amount of xanthan gum and whisk well to stabilize the mixture and maintain creaminess.

Using flavored or sweetened Greek yogurt, adding hidden calories.

Why it happens Flavored yogurts often contain sugars that increase calories and affect macros.

Fix Stick to plain zero-fat Greek yogurt and sweeten with monk fruit or lemon juice to control calories.

Folding peach slices too aggressively, causing them to break down and release excess liquid.

Why it happens Peaches are delicate and can become mushy if mixed roughly.

Fix Fold peaches gently and just enough to incorporate them evenly without crushing.

Pro Tips

Blend the cottage cheese until completely smooth before mixing to avoid grainy texture.
Cottage cheese naturally has curds that can feel unpleasant in a dessert. Blending creates a creamy base that mimics the richness of fat without the calories, improving mouthfeel and overall satisfaction.
Whip egg whites to soft peaks for optimal volume and moisture retention.
Soft peaks trap air to lighten the mixture but still hold moisture. Overbeating dries out egg whites, causing a crumbly texture, while underbeating won’t add enough volume, making the cobbler dense.
Add xanthan gum slowly and whisk well to prevent clumping.
Xanthan gum thickens and stabilizes without calories but can form lumps if added too quickly. Proper dispersion ensures a smooth, creamy texture that holds up during chilling.
Chill the cobbler for at least 2 hours before eating for best texture.
Chilling allows proteins and xanthan gum to set, firming the cobbler into a creamy, scoopable dessert. Eating immediately can feel watery and less satisfying.
Use fresh, ripe peaches for natural sweetness and juiciness.
Ripe peaches add flavor and moisture without added sugars, enhancing fullness and taste. Frozen or canned peaches often contain added sugars or syrup, increasing calories.
Fold ingredients gently to preserve the air whipped into egg whites.
Vigorous mixing deflates the egg whites, reducing volume and fluffiness. Gentle folding maintains a light texture that makes the snack feel more substantial.
Measure sweeteners carefully to avoid aftertaste.
Monk fruit and other zero-calorie sweeteners can have a bitter or metallic aftertaste if overused. Using the right amount balances sweetness without overpowering natural peach flavor.

Variations

Berry Peach Cobbler

Add 1/2 cup mixed berries (blueberries, raspberries) along with peaches for a colorful twist. The berries add antioxidants and a slight tartness that complements the sweet peaches. This variation increases fiber slightly but keeps calories similar.

Note: Berries can add extra moisture, so consider reducing lemon juice slightly to maintain thickness.

Cinnamon Spice Peach Cobbler

Increase cinnamon to 1 tsp and add a pinch of nutmeg and ground ginger for a warm, spiced flavor. This adds complexity without calories and enhances the dessert feel.

Note: Spices don’t affect texture but boost aroma and taste, making it perfect for colder months.

Vegan Protein Peach Cobbler

Use plant-based protein powder and swap egg whites for aquafaba (chickpea water) whipped to soft peaks. Replace Greek yogurt with coconut yogurt (light version) to keep it creamy. Expect slightly different texture but still high protein and low calorie.

Note: Aquafaba whips differently than egg whites; whip longer and fold gently to maintain volume.

No Xanthan Gum Version

Omit xanthan gum and add 1 tbsp chia seeds soaked in 2 tbsp water to thicken naturally. This adds fiber and omega-3s but may alter texture slightly, making it less creamy and more gel-like.

Note: Chia seeds absorb liquid slowly; soak at least 10 minutes before folding into the mixture.

Tropical Peach Cobbler

Add 1/4 cup diced pineapple and a splash of coconut extract to the base. This variation brings tropical flavors and keeps the snack refreshing and light, great for summer.

Note: Pineapple adds moisture; reduce lemon juice slightly to keep the cobbler from getting too loose.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This cobbler is best kept refrigerated and should not be left out at room temperature for more than 2 hours due to the dairy and egg whites. If left out longer, it might separate or lose volume and freshness.

Refrigerator

Store in airtight containers for up to 4 days. The cobbler firms up nicely when chilled and tastes best cold or slightly chilled. Texture remains creamy and fluffy, though volume may reduce slightly after a day.

Freezer

Freeze in airtight containers for up to 1 month. Thaw overnight in the fridge. Note that freezing may cause slight texture changes, making it less fluffy and slightly icy. Stir gently after thawing to improve texture.

Reheating

This snack is best enjoyed cold. Reheating is not recommended as it can cause separation of egg whites and loss of fluffiness. If desired, let it come to room temperature for 10-15 minutes before eating.

Make Ahead

Prepare the cobbler base and blend cottage cheese in advance, store separately from peaches. Assemble and fold in peaches just before serving or packing for meal prep to maintain freshness and prevent sogginess. Ideal for prepping 1-2 days ahead.

FAQ

Can I use flavored protein powder instead of vanilla?
Yes, but be mindful that flavored powders may contain added sugars or calories that affect your macros. Vanilla is preferred for its neutral flavor that blends well with peaches. If using flavored powder, adjust sweetener amounts accordingly to avoid overpowering the cobbler.
What if I don’t have xanthan gum? Can I skip it?
You can omit xanthan gum, but expect the cobbler to be a bit runnier and less creamy after chilling. A good alternative is soaked chia seeds, which thicken naturally but may change texture slightly. Xanthan gum is best for a stable, creamy finish without added calories.
How long does this cobbler keep in the fridge?
Stored in an airtight container, it stays fresh for up to 4 days. The texture may firm up but remains creamy and delicious. After this, dairy and egg whites can start to degrade, so it’s best eaten within this timeframe.
Can I use canned peaches instead of fresh?
Fresh peaches are best to keep calories low and avoid added sugars. If using canned, choose ones packed in water or juice without added sugars. Drain well to reduce excess liquid that can make the cobbler watery.
Is this recipe suitable for post-workout recovery?
Absolutely! With over 20 grams of protein and low calories, it supports muscle repair while keeping energy intake controlled. The carbs from peaches help replenish glycogen, making it a balanced post-workout snack.
Can I substitute egg whites with whole eggs?
Using whole eggs adds fat and calories, which changes the low-calorie profile. Egg whites are preferred for volume and protein without fat. If substituting, reduce other fat sources and recalculate macros accordingly.
Why does my cobbler sometimes turn watery after chilling?
Watery separation often happens if xanthan gum is skipped or not mixed well, or if peaches release too much juice. Make sure to add xanthan gum evenly and fold peaches gently. Using firm, ripe peaches helps reduce excess liquid.
Can I add other fruits to this cobbler?
Yes, berries, pineapple, or mango can be added for variety. Keep in mind some fruits have higher sugar and water content, which may affect texture and calories. Adjust sweeteners and thickeners as needed.
