Easy 10-Minute High-Protein Low-Calorie Grilled Sweet Potatoes Snack

These grilled sweet potatoes are a surprisingly high-protein, low-calorie snack that fills you up without guilt. The secret? Pairing them with a dollop of zero-fat Greek yogurt and a sprinkle of cinnamon for volume eating goodness. Perfect for an afternoon energy boost or post-workout refuel!

Introduction

I’ve always struggled with feeling hungry on low-calorie days, especially when cutting. Tiny snack portions just don’t cut it for me—I need volume. That’s why I love this grilled sweet potatoes recipe. It’s amazing how something so simple can feel like a feast. The trick is pairing the naturally sweet, fiber-rich potatoes with creamy zero-fat Greek yogurt, cinnamon, and a touch of vanilla. This combo bulks up the snack without adding calories, so you get a giant bowl that fills your stomach and your protein needs. I tried making this with regular yogurt once and it was too watery, but switching to thick zero-fat Greek yogurt made all the difference. Plus, grilling the potatoes caramelizes their natural sugars, giving them a rich flavor that feels indulgent but is totally guilt-free. I usually whip this up in the afternoon slump or right after a workout—it satisfies me for hours without any crash. My friends always ask how I eat so much and stay lean, and this snack is my secret weapon. It’s easy, quick, and perfect for meal prep too.

What You'll Need

Approximately 1 large bowl per serving, 6 snack servings, about 1.5 cups volume per serving

  • 3 medium sweet potatoes, washed and sliced into 1/2 inch rounds
  • 1 cup zero-fat Greek yogurt, chilled
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice
  • 1/2 tsp vanilla extract
  • Stevia or monk fruit sweetener, to taste
  • Fresh mint leaves for garnish (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your grill or grill pan to medium-high heat. Slice the sweet potatoes into 1/2 inch rounds to ensure they cook evenly and quickly. This thickness is key because thinner slices can fall apart or burn, while thicker ones take too long and dry out. Brush the slices lightly with water or a mist of cooking spray to prevent sticking without adding fat. Grill the slices for about 4-5 minutes on each side until you see nice grill marks and the potatoes are tender but still hold their shape. Avoid flipping too often as this can cause them to break apart and lose moisture.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Sweet potatoes should be tender when pierced with a fork and have visible grill marks without charring.
Step 2
While the sweet potatoes grill, prepare the protein-rich yogurt topping. In a bowl, mix 1 cup of zero-fat Greek yogurt with lemon juice, vanilla extract, and your preferred zero-calorie sweetener like stevia or monk fruit. The acidity from lemon juice helps cut through the yogurt’s tang and adds brightness, while vanilla enhances sweetness naturally. Whisk until smooth and creamy. This step is important because it builds volume and creaminess without adding fat, keeping the snack low-calorie yet satisfying. Chill the mixture if you want it extra refreshing.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: The yogurt mixture should be smooth, slightly thickened, and well-flavored without any graininess or separation.
Step 3
Once the sweet potatoes are grilled, arrange them in a large bowl or on individual plates. Dollop the prepared Greek yogurt mixture generously over the warm potatoes. The contrast between warm, caramelized sweet potatoes and cool, creamy yogurt is what makes this snack so enjoyable. The yogurt adds protein and moisture, creating a luscious texture without fat. Sprinkle ground cinnamon evenly on top for a warm, aromatic finish that also helps regulate blood sugar spikes. Cinnamon also complements the natural sweetness of the potatoes beautifully.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Snack should look inviting with a creamy white topping and a dusting of cinnamon, ready to eat immediately.
Step 4
Garnish with fresh mint leaves if desired for a pop of color and refreshing flavor. Mint also aids digestion and adds a fresh aroma that balances the sweetness. This is optional but elevates the snack aesthetically and taste-wise. Serve immediately for best texture and flavor. If making ahead, keep components separate and combine just before eating to avoid sogginess.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Fresh mint leaves look vibrant and add a fresh green contrast.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using full-fat yogurt instead of zero-fat Greek yogurt.

Why it happens People often assume full-fat yogurt is healthier or more filling, but it adds unnecessary calories and fat, defeating the purpose of a low-calorie snack.

Fix Always check labels and choose thick zero-fat Greek yogurt to keep protein high and calories low. This maintains the creamy texture without added fat.

Skipping lemon juice in the yogurt mix.

Why it happens Lemon juice might seem like an odd addition, so it’s often forgotten. Without it, yogurt can taste too tangy or protein powders can become chalky.

Fix Add a tablespoon of fresh lemon juice to balance flavors and improve mouthfeel in the yogurt topping.

Grilling sweet potatoes too thin or thick.

Why it happens Inconsistent slicing leads to uneven cooking—thin slices burn, thick slices stay raw inside.

Fix Use a mandoline or sharp knife to slice evenly about 1/2 inch thick for perfect grilling.

Pro Tips

Use a grill pan or outdoor grill for even caramelization without extra oil.
Grilling sweet potatoes without oil can be tricky as they tend to stick. A well-heated grill pan or outdoor grill ensures quick searing and caramelization, locking in flavor while keeping calories low. Avoid flipping too frequently to prevent breakage and drying out.
Whisk yogurt with lemon juice and vanilla before adding to sweet potatoes.
This step enhances flavor and texture by reducing tanginess and preventing chalkiness from protein powders. The acidity also helps the yogurt stay thick and creamy, which is essential in low-fat, high-protein snacks to avoid watery textures.
Slice sweet potatoes uniformly for even cooking and texture.
Even thickness ensures all pieces cook through simultaneously, preventing some from being underdone or mushy. This consistency is key for a pleasant mouthfeel and to maintain volume eating benefits.

Variations

Berry-Topped Grilled Sweet Potato Protein Snack

Swap the cinnamon for fresh berries like blueberries or raspberries on top of the yogurt. This adds antioxidants and a fresh tartness that pairs well with the sweet potatoes. Macros stay similar, with added fiber and vitamins.

Note: Add berries just before serving to maintain their texture and prevent wateriness in the yogurt.

Spiced Pumpkin Protein Grilled Sweet Potato Bowl

Mix pumpkin puree with the Greek yogurt, along with pumpkin pie spice, for a seasonal twist. This increases fiber and adds a creamy texture, keeping protein high while adding festive flavor.

Note: Use canned pumpkin puree without added sugars; adjust sweetener to taste.

Savory Herbed Grilled Sweet Potato Snack

Skip the sweeteners and cinnamon, and instead top grilled sweet potatoes with Greek yogurt mixed with fresh herbs like dill and chives. This savory version is great for a mid-day snack or light lunch.

Note: Ensure herbs are finely chopped and yogurt is well mixed to distribute flavors evenly.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This snack is best eaten fresh. At room temperature, it can keep for up to 1 hour if covered, but the yogurt may start to separate and the sweet potatoes lose texture.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container for up to 3 days. Keep yogurt and sweet potatoes separate if possible to prevent sogginess. The snack tastes best chilled or at room temperature after a few minutes out of the fridge.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as the texture of grilled sweet potatoes and yogurt deteriorates significantly, becoming watery and icy upon thawing.

Reheating

Reheat grilled sweet potatoes gently in a toaster oven or skillet to restore some texture before adding cold yogurt topping. Avoid microwaving yogurt to prevent separation.

Make Ahead

Grill sweet potatoes and prepare yogurt topping 1-2 days ahead. Store separately in airtight containers and combine just before eating to maintain volume and texture.

FAQ

Can I substitute the Greek yogurt with cottage cheese for this snack?
Yes, low-fat cottage cheese can be used as a protein source, but it has a different texture and flavor. It won’t be as creamy as Greek yogurt and may make the snack less smooth. Adjust seasoning and sweetness accordingly.
How do I prevent the protein powder or yogurt from tasting chalky in this recipe?
Adding a bit of lemon juice or vanilla extract helps mask chalkiness. Also, whisking the yogurt mixture thoroughly and chilling it before serving improves texture and flavor. Avoid overusing protein powder to keep it smooth.
Is this snack suitable for post-workout recovery?
Absolutely! It provides a good balance of protein and carbs to replenish muscles and glycogen stores. The sweet potatoes offer complex carbs, while Greek yogurt supplies high-quality protein.
Can I prepare this snack in advance for meal prep?
Yes, but keep the grilled sweet potatoes and yogurt topping separate until ready to eat to prevent sogginess. Both components keep well refrigerated for up to 3 days.
What if I don’t have a grill or grill pan?
You can roast the sweet potato slices in the oven at 425°F (220°C) for about 20 minutes, flipping halfway. This method also caramelizes the potatoes nicely without added oil.
How do I count calories accurately with this recipe?
Use a kitchen scale to measure sweet potatoes and yogurt precisely. Check nutrition labels for your specific brands, especially for Greek yogurt and sweeteners, to track macros accurately.
Can I add nut butters or seeds to increase fat content?
While you can, adding nut butters or seeds will increase calories significantly and may not fit a low-calorie snack goal. Use sparingly if you want to maintain the calorie count under 200.
What’s the best way to reheat grilled sweet potatoes without drying them out?
Reheat gently in a toaster oven or skillet over low heat to warm through without drying. Avoid microwaving as it can make potatoes mushy.
