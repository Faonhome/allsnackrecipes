Easy Volume-Eating High-Protein Cucumber Snack for Under 150 Calories

Chef
Published on June 20, 2026
10 min Prep Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty
4.8 (58 ratings)✓ Thanks for rating!

I love this snack because it’s crunchy, super filling, and packs a solid protein punch without tipping the calorie scale. The trick? Mixing crisp cucumber with creamy zero-fat Greek yogurt and whipped egg whites for fluffiness. Perfect for an afternoon pick-me-up or post-workout refuel!

Introduction

I’ve always struggled with feeling hungry on low-calorie diets, especially when I’m trying to cut but still want to eat something satisfying. Tiny snacks just don’t cut it for me, and I hate feeling like I’m starving between meals. That’s why I came up with this simple cucumber snack that’s not only super high in protein but also gives me a big volume of food to munch on without the guilt. It’s way more filling than those tiny protein bars and has no weird additives. The secret is whipping the egg whites to add fluff and mixing in zero-fat Greek yogurt for creaminess without calories. I also add xanthan gum sometimes to keep the texture thick and avoid watery separation, which I learned the hard way after a failed batch! Plus, the fresh dill and lemon juice give it a bright, fresh flavor that makes snacking feel like a treat. I usually eat this in the afternoon slump or right after a workout, and it keeps me full for hours. My friends even ask how I eat so much and still lose weight! It’s perfect for meal prep too since it holds up well in the fridge and tastes even better chilled. If you’re tired of tiny, boring snacks that don’t satisfy, give this one a try—you’ll love how much you can eat without breaking your calorie bank.

What You'll Need

Approximately 2 cups volume per serving, 6 snack servings

  • 2 large cucumbers, peeled and diced
  • 1 cup zero-fat Greek yogurt
  • 1/2 cup liquid egg whites, whipped until fluffy
  • 1 scoop unflavored whey protein isolate powder
  • 1 tablespoon chopped fresh dill
  • 1 teaspoon lemon juice
  • Stevia or monk fruit sweetener, to taste
  • 1/4 teaspoon xanthan gum (optional, for thickness)
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by peeling and dicing the cucumbers into small, bite-sized pieces. This is important because smaller pieces increase the volume you get to eat and help distribute the flavors better. Avoid leaving the cucumber chunks too large or uneven, as that can make the snack less enjoyable to eat and reduce the perceived volume. The peeling step also removes any bitterness from the skin, keeping the flavor fresh and light.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Uniformly diced cucumber pieces, crisp and fresh-looking
Step 2
Whip the liquid egg whites in a clean bowl until they form soft peaks. This adds volume and fluffiness without any fat or calories. Be careful not to overwhip or the egg whites will become grainy and dry, which can affect the texture of the final snack. Whipping introduces air, which makes the snack feel larger and more satisfying, perfect for volume eating.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Soft, glossy peaks that hold their shape but still bend slightly
Step 3
In a separate bowl, mix the zero-fat Greek yogurt with the whey protein isolate powder. Stir gently to avoid clumps and ensure the protein powder hydrates fully. Using unflavored whey isolate helps keep calories low and avoids any chalkiness that can happen with concentrate or flavored powders. If you notice any chalkiness, adding a splash of cold water or lemon juice can help smooth the texture.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Smooth creamy mixture with no visible lumps or powder residue
Step 4
Combine the whipped egg whites gently with the yogurt-protein mixture. Use a folding motion to keep as much air as possible in the mixture. This step is key to maintaining the fluffiness and volume of the snack. Overmixing will deflate the air bubbles, resulting in a denser texture that feels less satisfying and lower volume.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Light, airy mixture that looks creamy but fluffy
Step 5
Add the diced cucumbers to the creamy mixture along with fresh dill, lemon juice, salt, and pepper. Stir gently to combine everything evenly. The fresh dill and lemon juice brighten the flavor and balance the creamy protein base. Season to taste but keep salt moderate to avoid water retention.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Evenly coated cucumber pieces with visible herbs and creamy texture
Step 6
If you want a thicker texture that holds well, sprinkle xanthan gum evenly over the mixture and fold in gently. Xanthan gum acts as a zero-calorie thickener that prevents watery separation common in low-fat dairy snacks. Be careful to add just a small amount (about 1/4 teaspoon) because too much will make the snack gummy and unpleasant.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Slightly thicker mixture that clings well to cucumber pieces without running
Step 7
Taste the snack and adjust sweetness with stevia or monk fruit sweetener if desired. Adding a touch of sweetness balances the savory flavors and enhances overall satisfaction without adding calories. Avoid sugar or honey to keep the snack low-calorie and macros-friendly.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Balanced sweet-savory flavor without any bitterness or aftertaste
Step 8
Transfer the mixture into serving bowls or airtight containers for meal prep. Portion out about 2 cups per serving to feel like you’re eating a giant bowl of food, which helps with satiety and sticking to your calorie goals. The large portion size tricks your brain into feeling satisfied with less calorie density.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Evenly portioned servings with visible creamy cucumber mixture
Step 9
Chill the snack in the refrigerator for at least 30 minutes before eating. Chilling firms up the texture and allows flavors to meld. If you eat it too soon, it might feel a bit watery or loose. The cold temperature also enhances the refreshing quality of the cucumber and yogurt combo.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Firm, chilled snack with cohesive texture and bright flavor

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping xanthan gum and ending up with watery, separated snack.

Why it happens Many assume zero-fat dairy and protein powder will naturally blend well, but without a thickener, the mixture separates quickly due to lack of fat.

Fix Add a small amount (about 1/4 teaspoon) of xanthan gum to the mixture and fold gently. This stabilizes the texture and prevents watery separation, keeping the snack creamy and enjoyable.

Overwhipping egg whites causing dryness and grainy texture.

Why it happens It’s easy to think more whipping means more volume, but overwhipping squeezes out moisture and damages the structure.

Fix Stop whipping when soft peaks form. The egg whites should be glossy and hold shape but still be tender. This ensures fluffiness and smooth texture.

Using full-fat or 2% Greek yogurt, increasing calories and altering texture.

Why it happens People often grab whatever yogurt is on hand, not realizing fat content impacts calorie count and mouthfeel.

Fix Always use zero-fat Greek yogurt for this recipe to keep calories low and texture light. It also blends better with protein powder and whipped egg whites.

Adding too much sweetener causing artificial aftertaste.

Why it happens Trying to mask protein powder flavor with excess sweetener can backfire, making the snack taste bitter or chemical.

Fix Add sweetener gradually, tasting as you go. Use natural options like stevia or monk fruit in small amounts for balanced flavor.

Eating the snack immediately without chilling, resulting in watery texture.

Why it happens Low-fat mixtures need time to set and thicken, but impatience leads to a loose, unappealing consistency.

Fix Refrigerate the snack for at least 30 minutes before eating to allow flavors to meld and texture to firm up.

Pro Tips

Whip egg whites just until soft peaks form for best volume and texture.
Soft peaks hold maximum air without drying out, which keeps the snack fluffy and light. Overwhipping causes dryness and graininess, ruining the creamy mouthfeel. The air bubbles from whipping make the snack feel larger and more satisfying without adding calories.
Use unflavored whey isolate to avoid chalkiness and excess carbs.
Whey isolate has fewer fillers and carbs than concentrate, making it ideal for low-calorie snacks. Unflavored powder lets you control sweetness and flavor without artificial additives. Proper hydration in yogurt prevents lumps and chalky texture.
Add xanthan gum sparingly to prevent watery separation in low-fat mixtures.
Xanthan gum thickens the snack and mimics fat’s creamy texture without calories. Too much turns the snack gummy and unpleasant. A small amount stabilizes the mixture and keeps it cohesive, especially important when mixing protein powder, yogurt, and egg whites.
Dice cucumbers small and evenly to maximize volume and flavor distribution.
Smaller pieces increase the volume you can eat and make the snack easier to eat. Uneven chunks can reduce perceived volume and make the texture inconsistent. Peeling removes bitterness and enhances freshness.
Chill the snack for at least 30 minutes before eating to firm texture and meld flavors.
Chilling helps the mixture thicken and flavors develop. Eating immediately might result in watery or loose texture. Cold temperature enhances the refreshing quality of cucumber and yogurt.
Use fresh herbs like dill and lemon juice to brighten flavor and mask protein powder notes.
Acidic and fresh flavors balance the creamy base and reduce any chalkiness or blandness from protein powder. It makes the snack taste vibrant and less like a typical protein shake.
Measure sweeteners carefully to avoid overpowering the snack with artificial aftertaste.
Natural sweeteners like stevia can have a bitter aftertaste if overused. Adding just enough to balance savory flavors improves enjoyment without masking freshness or adding calories.

Variations

Spicy Cucumber Protein Snack

Add a pinch of cayenne pepper and chopped jalapeño to the base recipe for a spicy kick. This variation adds a metabolism-boosting element and fresh heat that pairs well with creamy yogurt. The macros remain similar, with minimal calorie increase from spices.

Note: Spices won’t affect texture but add flavor complexity. Adjust spice level carefully to avoid overpowering the delicate cucumber flavor.

Cucumber and Edamame Protein Bowl

Swap half the egg whites with shelled edamame for a plant-based protein boost. This adds fiber and a slightly nutty flavor while keeping calories low. Texture will be chunkier and more substantial.

Note: Use cooked and cooled edamame. Fold gently to preserve the fluffy texture from egg whites.

Lemon Herb Cucumber with Cottage Cheese

Replace Greek yogurt with low-fat cottage cheese for a different texture and slightly higher protein content. The lemon and dill keep it fresh and bright. Expect a chunkier texture with more chew.

Note: Mix cottage cheese thoroughly to smooth lumps before combining with other ingredients.

Sweet Cucumber and Berry Protein Snack

Add a handful of fresh berries and a touch more stevia for a sweet, fruity twist. This variation adds antioxidants and natural sweetness while keeping calories low. Texture becomes creamier with juicy bursts from berries.

Note: Add berries last to avoid crushing and maintain fresh texture.

Cucumber Protein Dip with Celery Sticks

Thicken the base recipe slightly with extra xanthan gum to create a dip consistency. Serve with celery sticks or bell pepper slices for a crunchy, high-protein snack. Great for sharing or meal prep.

Note: Adjust xanthan gum carefully to avoid gummy texture. Chill well before serving.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This snack is best eaten chilled and should not be left out at room temperature for more than 1 hour due to the dairy and egg whites. Keep covered to prevent drying out or absorbing odors.

Refrigerator

Store in airtight containers for up to 3 days. The snack firms up in the fridge and flavors meld nicely. Volume and texture hold well, but some separation may occur—just stir gently before eating. It tastes best cold.

Freezer

Not recommended to freeze this snack as the high water content in cucumber and dairy causes icy texture and loss of fluffiness upon thawing.

Reheating

This snack is meant to be eaten cold. Reheating will cause separation and loss of texture. If needed, serve at room temperature but avoid heating.

Make Ahead

Prepare the creamy protein base and diced cucumber separately 1-2 days ahead. Whip egg whites fresh before folding in to maintain maximum fluffiness. Combine all components just before serving or packing for best texture.

FAQ

Can I substitute the whey protein powder with plant-based protein?
Yes, you can use a plant-based protein powder, but be aware some have a grittier texture or stronger flavor. Choose an unflavored, smooth powder and mix well. You might need to adjust sweetener or xanthan gum slightly to maintain creamy texture.
Why does my snack turn watery after mixing?
Watery texture usually happens due to lack of thickener like xanthan gum or because the yogurt is too watery. Also, if you stir too vigorously after whipping egg whites, you can deflate the air bubbles, releasing water. Use thick zero-fat Greek yogurt and add a small amount of xanthan gum for best results.
Is it safe to eat raw whipped egg whites?
Using pasteurized liquid egg whites is safe to consume raw as they have been heat-treated to eliminate bacteria. Avoid raw egg whites from fresh eggs unless you’re confident in their safety. Pasteurized whites are convenient and safe for this recipe.
How long does this snack keep its volume after chilling?
The snack maintains good volume for up to 3 days refrigerated if stored properly. The whipped egg whites may slowly deflate over time, so gently fold the mixture before serving to restore fluffiness.
Can I add other vegetables instead of cucumber?
Cucumber’s high water content and mild flavor make it ideal for volume eating. You can try celery or bell peppers for crunch, but they may alter flavor and texture. Adjust seasoning accordingly.
How do I avoid chalkiness from protein powder?
Use a high-quality unflavored whey isolate and mix it thoroughly with yogurt before folding in egg whites. Adding a splash of lemon juice or cold water helps smooth texture. Avoid overmixing after adding egg whites.
Can I make this snack vegan?
This recipe relies on dairy and egg whites for protein and texture, so it’s not fully vegan. To adapt, use plant-based yogurt and protein powder, and replace egg whites with aquafaba whipped to soft peaks, though texture and flavor will differ.
Is this snack suitable for meal prep?
Absolutely! It stores well in the fridge for up to 3 days and is great for grab-and-go. Keep components separate if possible and fold together before eating to maintain fluffiness.
