Easy Cornbread Sheet Pan Recipe Perfect for High Protein, Low Calorie Snacking

Chef
Published on June 13, 2026
10 min Prep Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This cornbread sheet pan recipe is a great way to enjoy a high-protein, low-calorie snack that fills you up without the guilt. Using egg whites and a touch of Greek yogurt keeps it moist and protein-packed. Perfect for afternoon cravings or meal prep!

Introduction

I've always struggled with feeling hungry on low-calorie diets, especially when cutting. Small snacks just don’t cut it, and I hate feeling like I’m starving between meals. That’s why I love this cornbread sheet pan recipe — it gives me a huge portion that actually fills me up without loading on calories. Unlike store-bought protein bars that are tiny and often full of weird ingredients, this recipe uses simple, wholesome stuff like cornmeal and egg whites to keep protein high and calories low. The trick is using zero-fat Greek yogurt and egg whites to add moisture and protein without fat, plus a little xanthan gum to keep the texture fluffy and not crumbly. I also bulk it up with chopped bell peppers and scallions for crunch and volume, so it feels like I'm eating way more food than I really am. It’s perfect for afternoon snacks when I’m battling the 3pm slump or for meal prep Sundays when I want something grab-and-go. I did have a batch that was too watery once, but adjusting the yogurt and almond milk ratio fixed that right up. My friends always ask how I manage to eat so much and still lose weight, and this recipe is a big part of that secret. Honestly, I find myself reaching for this giant cornbread slice while binge-watching Netflix, and it’s such a satisfying way to snack without guilt.

What You'll Need

6 generous servings, approximately 1/6 of a full sheet pan per serving, about 1.5 cups volume each

  • 1 cup cornmeal, dry
  • 1/2 cup oat flour, dry
  • 1/2 cup zero-fat Greek yogurt, cold
  • 4 large egg whites, room temperature
  • 1/4 cup unsweetened almond milk, cold
  • 1 tbsp erythritol sweetener
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp xanthan gum (optional, for texture)
  • 1/2 cup finely chopped bell peppers (for volume and crunch)
  • 1/4 cup chopped scallions

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by preheating your oven to 375°F (190°C) and lining a sheet pan with parchment paper. This ensures even cooking and easy cleanup. Mixing dry ingredients first — cornmeal, oat flour, baking powder, salt, and xanthan gum — helps distribute the leavening agents evenly, which is crucial for that light texture. Avoid skipping the xanthan gum if you want a moist, cohesive crumb; it acts as a binder since there’s no fat in this recipe. Mix these dry ingredients thoroughly to prevent clumps, which can cause uneven rising or gritty texture.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are evenly combined with no lumps or streaks of baking powder
Step 2
In a separate bowl, whisk the egg whites until frothy but not stiff. Then add the zero-fat Greek yogurt, cold almond milk, and erythritol sweetener. Mixing these wet ingredients cold helps keep the batter thick and prevents it from becoming watery. Combining the wet ingredients gently with the dry ensures the protein powder and yogurt hydrate properly, preventing chalkiness. Be careful not to overmix once combined; overmixing can activate gluten in the oat flour, making the cornbread tough.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Wet ingredients are well blended and smooth, with no separation
Step 3
Fold the wet ingredients into the dry mixture gently until just combined. Then fold in the chopped bell peppers and scallions carefully to distribute evenly without deflating the batter. This layering adds crunch and volume without extra calories. Avoid overmixing here to keep the cornbread tender. The batter should be thick but spreadable. If it feels too runny, add a teaspoon more oat flour to help it hold shape during baking.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is uniformly mixed with visible veggies dispersed throughout
Step 4
Pour the batter evenly onto the prepared sheet pan and spread it out with a spatula to about 1-inch thickness. The even thickness ensures the cornbread cooks through without drying out edges. If the batter is too thick to spread, wet your spatula slightly with water to smooth the surface. This step also helps achieve a nice crust while keeping the inside moist and fluffy.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is spread evenly with a smooth top surface across the pan
Step 5
Bake in the preheated oven for 15-18 minutes, checking at 15 minutes for doneness. The cornbread is done when the edges start to turn golden and a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs. Avoid overbaking, which dries out the cornbread and reduces the pleasant moist texture that keeps it filling without added fat.
⏱ Time: 15-18 minutes
✓ Doneness: Golden edges and clean toothpick test confirm perfect bake
Step 6
Remove the sheet pan from the oven and let the cornbread cool for at least 10 minutes before slicing. Cooling firms up the structure since this recipe has no fat to hold moisture, preventing it from falling apart when cut. If you slice too soon, the cornbread can crumble or be gummy. Use a sharp serrated knife for clean cuts.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cornbread is firm to the touch and holds shape when sliced
Step 7
Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 4 days. The cornbread will firm up and taste even better chilled or at room temperature. To reheat, microwave briefly or warm in a toaster oven to revive the soft texture. Avoid reheating multiple times as this can dry it out.
⏱ Time: Storage ongoing
✓ Doneness: Cornbread maintains moist texture after reheating
Step 8
For meal prep, portion the cornbread into individual servings right after cutting and keep them separated with parchment paper to prevent sticking. This makes grab-and-go snacking easy and keeps portions consistent for accurate calorie counting.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Individual slices neatly separated and ready for storage
Step 9
Enjoy your cornbread snack plain or with a light spread of zero-fat Greek yogurt or a sprinkle of cinnamon for extra flavor without adding calories. This adds variety without compromising the low-calorie, high-protein profile.
⏱ Time: Optional
✓ Doneness: Snack is flavorful and satisfying without added fat

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping xanthan gum causing crumbly texture.

Why it happens People often think xanthan gum is optional, but without fat, the batter lacks structure and falls apart easily.

Fix Add 1 teaspoon xanthan gum to dry ingredients to bind moisture and improve texture without adding calories.

Using full-fat yogurt instead of zero-fat.

Why it happens Assuming any yogurt works leads to higher calories and changes in texture.

Fix Always choose zero-fat Greek yogurt for moisture and protein without extra fat or calories.

Overmixing batter after combining wet and dry.

Why it happens Eager to get a smooth batter, but this activates gluten making it tough.

Fix Mix just until combined, folding gently to keep the cornbread tender.

Not chilling wet ingredients causing runny batter.

Why it happens Warm yogurt or milk speeds protein powder hydration, making batter watery and chalky.

Fix Use cold yogurt and almond milk straight from fridge to keep batter thick and creamy.

Cutting cornbread too soon while warm.

Why it happens Eager to eat leads to crumbling and gummy slices.

Fix Let cornbread cool completely for at least 10 minutes to firm up before slicing.

Pro Tips

Whisk egg whites separately until frothy before adding to batter.
Whipping egg whites incorporates air, which lightens the batter and improves texture, especially important in low-fat recipes. Without this step, the cornbread can be dense and dry.
Use cold wet ingredients to keep batter thick.
Cold yogurt and almond milk slow protein powder hydration, preventing a chalky, watery batter. This helps maintain creaminess and prevents separation during baking.
Don’t overmix the batter once wet and dry ingredients combine.
Overmixing activates gluten in oat flour, causing toughness. Gentle folding preserves tenderness and fluffiness.
Add xanthan gum as a binder for moisture and structure.
Xanthan gum mimics fat’s texture, preventing crumbly cornbread despite zero-fat ingredients. It stabilizes batter and improves mouthfeel.
Chop vegetables finely to add crunch without extra calories.
Finely chopped bell peppers and scallions add volume and texture, making the snack feel larger and more satisfying without adding fat or many calories.
Cool cornbread completely before slicing.
Cooling firms the structure since there’s no fat to hold moisture, preventing crumbling and sogginess when cutting.
Store slices separated by parchment paper.
Prevents sticking and keeps portions consistent for easy grab-and-go snacking and accurate calorie tracking.

Variations

Spicy Jalapeño Cornbread

Add 1-2 finely chopped jalapeños and a pinch of cayenne pepper for a spicy kick. This variation adds flavor complexity without extra calories, keeping protein high. Expect a slightly more moist texture due to the peppers’ water content.

Note: Because jalapeños add moisture, you might need to reduce almond milk by 1-2 tablespoons to keep batter consistency.

Cheesy Herb Cornbread (Low-Fat Cheese)

Incorporate 1/4 cup fat-free shredded cheese and 1 tsp mixed dried herbs. This boosts flavor and protein slightly, with minimal calories. Texture will be creamier but still light due to zero-fat ingredients.

Note: Add cheese with dry ingredients and reduce xanthan gum to 1/2 tsp to avoid gummy texture.

Pumpkin Spice Cornbread

Swap almond milk for 1/4 cup pumpkin puree and add cinnamon, nutmeg, and allspice. This adds fiber and flavor without adding fat. Protein remains high with no calorie spike.

Note: Pumpkin puree adds moisture; reduce yogurt slightly to keep batter from becoming too wet.

Vegan Protein Cornbread

Replace egg whites with aquafaba (chickpea water) whipped to stiff peaks and use plant-based Greek-style yogurt. Protein content drops slightly but still remains substantial. Texture may be less firm but still satisfying.

Note: Aquafaba requires longer whipping and gentle folding to maintain airiness.

Berry-Infused Cornbread

Fold in 1/2 cup fresh blueberries or raspberries for natural sweetness and antioxidants. This adds volume and flavor with minimal calories. Expect a moist crumb and slight tartness.

Note: Add berries last and fold gently to avoid crushing them and turning batter purple.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This cornbread is best eaten fresh or stored briefly at room temperature for up to 2 hours covered tightly to prevent drying. Because it contains zero-fat dairy and egg whites, it can separate or become watery if left out longer.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container for up to 4 days. The cornbread firms up when chilled but retains moisture and flavor. It tastes great cold or warmed slightly in the microwave for 20-30 seconds.

Freezer

Freeze individual slices wrapped tightly in plastic wrap and placed in a freezer-safe bag for up to 2 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge and warm gently to restore softness. Avoid refreezing to maintain texture.

Reheating

Microwave for 20-30 seconds or warm in a toaster oven at 300°F for 5 minutes. Avoid overheating as this can dry out the cornbread and cause separation of ingredients.

Make Ahead

Prepare the batter up to 1 day ahead and keep refrigerated. Spread on the sheet pan and bake fresh to maintain fluffiness and texture. Alternatively, bake and slice ahead, then store slices separated by parchment paper for easy grab-and-go snacking.

FAQ

Can I substitute whole eggs for egg whites in this recipe?
Using whole eggs will increase the fat and calorie content, which may affect the low-calorie goal. The cornbread will be richer and more moist but less suitable if you want a high-protein, low-fat snack. If you do substitute, reduce or omit the Greek yogurt to balance moisture.
What’s the best protein powder to use here?
A whey isolate protein powder is ideal because it has minimal carbs and fats, ensuring low calories. Avoid blends with added maltodextrin or fillers that increase calories. Choose an unflavored or mild vanilla powder to avoid overpowering the cornbread flavor.
How do I prevent the batter from becoming watery?
Use cold wet ingredients and measure liquids carefully. Adding xanthan gum helps bind moisture. If batter is too runny, add a tablespoon of oat flour gradually until it thickens to a spreadable consistency.
Can I make this gluten-free?
Yes! Use certified gluten-free oat flour and ensure your baking powder and other ingredients are gluten-free. This recipe naturally contains no wheat flour, so it’s already close to gluten-free.
How long does this snack keep its fullness effect?
Thanks to high protein and fiber from oat flour and veggies, this cornbread keeps you full for 2-3 hours, making it perfect for mid-afternoon hunger or post-workout recovery.
Can I add sweetener if I want a sweeter snack?
Yes, erythritol or stevia work well to add sweetness without calories or aftertaste. Adjust to taste, but start with small amounts to avoid overpowering the natural corn flavor.
Is it okay to eat this snack cold?
Absolutely! Many find it tastes even better chilled, as the flavors meld and the texture firms up nicely. Just warm slightly if you prefer a softer bite.
Can I double the recipe for meal prep?
Yes, doubling works great. Just use a larger sheet pan or bake in batches to ensure even cooking. Keep portions separated with parchment paper for easy storage.
