Viral TikTok No Bake Chocolate Protein Bites with Oats & Almond Butter for a High-Protein Low-Calorie Snack

Cynthia Sullivan
Published on June 4, 2026
10 min Prep Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These no-bake chocolate protein bites are packed with oats and almond butter, giving you a satisfying high-protein, low-calorie snack that feels indulgent but won’t wreck your macros. Perfect for a quick energy boost or post-workout treat!

What You'll Need

Approximately 6 protein bites, each about 2 tablespoons in size, perfect for a quick snack

  • 1 cup rolled oats, dry
  • 1/2 cup almond butter, natural and unsweetened
  • 1/4 cup chocolate protein powder (whey isolate preferred)
  • 2 tbsp unsweetened almond milk, cold
  • 1 tbsp sugar-free maple syrup or erythritol syrup
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1/4 tsp sea salt
  • Optional: 1 tbsp chia seeds for extra texture and fiber

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by combining the rolled oats and chocolate protein powder in a large mixing bowl. Mixing the dry ingredients first ensures an even distribution of protein throughout your bites. This step is important because it prevents clumps of protein powder, which can cause a chalky texture. Use a fork or whisk to break up any lumps and incorporate air, making your bites lighter. Avoid adding the wet ingredients too soon, or the oats might clump and become hard to mix evenly.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: The dry mixture should be uniform in color and texture with no visible clumps of protein powder.
Step 2
In a separate small bowl, whisk together the almond butter, cold almond milk, sugar-free maple syrup, vanilla extract, and sea salt. The cold almond milk helps hydrate the protein powder later and keeps the mixture from becoming too sticky. Whisking these wet ingredients well ensures they blend smoothly, which will give your bites a creamy texture without adding fat. If you rush this step, the almond butter might not mix fully, resulting in uneven bites.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: The mixture should be smooth, slightly runny but thick, with no lumps of almond butter.
Step 3
Pour the wet mixture into the bowl with the dry oats and protein powder. Use a sturdy spoon or spatula to fold everything together gently. Folding rather than stirring vigorously keeps some air in the mixture, which helps maintain a lighter texture. You want the oats and protein powder fully coated but not soggy. If the mixture feels too dry, add almond milk a teaspoon at a time; too much will make the bites mushy and hard to form.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: The mixture should come together into a thick dough that holds shape when pressed.
Step 4
If using, sprinkle in the chia seeds and fold them in evenly. Chia seeds add a bit of crunch and fiber, which helps with satiety. They also absorb moisture and help bind the bites. Make sure to distribute them well to avoid clumps. This step is optional but recommended for extra texture and nutritional boost.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Chia seeds should be evenly spread throughout the mixture.
Step 5
Line a small tray or plate with parchment paper. Using your hands or a small cookie scoop, form the mixture into bite-sized balls, about 2 tablespoons each. Press firmly to compact the mixture so the bites hold together. The firmness depends on how well the wet and dry ingredients combined; if they’re crumbly, add a splash more almond milk and mix again.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bites should be firm, hold their shape, and not crumble when handled.
Step 6
Place the formed bites in the refrigerator for at least 30 minutes to firm up. Chilling helps the almond butter and protein powder bind together, improving texture and making the bites less sticky. If you skip this step, the bites may fall apart or feel too soft. The cold also reduces any residual chalkiness from the protein powder.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bites should feel firm to the touch and slightly set.
Step 7
Once chilled, transfer the bites to an airtight container. Store in the refrigerator and enjoy within a week. Keeping them cold maintains their shape and texture. If left out too long, especially in warm environments, they can soften and lose their firmness.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Bites remain firm and do not stick excessively to each other.
Step 8
Before eating, let the bites sit at room temperature for 5 minutes if you prefer a softer texture. This makes them less dense and easier to chew without compromising their structure. If you want a firmer bite, eat them straight from the fridge. This flexibility lets you enjoy them how you like best.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bites feel pleasantly soft or firm depending on preference.
Step 9
For an extra touch, you can roll the bites in a dusting of cocoa powder or finely chopped nuts before chilling. This adds a nice finish and a bit of extra flavor or crunch. Just be mindful that nuts add calories, so keep the coating light if you’re watching macros closely.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bites have an even, light coating that enhances appearance and taste.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the wet ingredient mixing step, causing clumpy, chalky bites.

Why it happens People often combine all ingredients at once, assuming the powders and nut butters will blend easily. This leads to uneven hydration and a chalky mouthfeel.

Fix Always whisk wet ingredients separately and then fold into dry ingredients slowly. Use cold almond milk to hydrate protein powder properly and prevent clumps.

Using too much almond butter, making the bites calorie-dense and greasy.

Why it happens Almond butter is delicious and tempting to add generously, but it’s high in calories and fat, which defeats the low-calorie goal.

Fix Measure almond butter carefully and stick to the recipe amount. This keeps calories in check and ensures the bites are not overly rich or heavy.

Not chilling the bites, resulting in soft, sticky texture that falls apart.

Why it happens Impatience or lack of fridge space leads to skipping the chilling step, which is crucial for texture development.

Fix Plan ahead and refrigerate bites for at least 30 minutes. This step firms the protein and fat, giving the bites structure and better mouthfeel.

Pro Tips

Always mix protein powder with cold liquid before adding to dry ingredients.
Cold liquids help protein powders hydrate properly without clumping or creating a chalky texture. Warm or room temperature liquids can cause the powder to bind unevenly, resulting in gritty bites. Proper hydration also improves mouthfeel and flavor.
Press the mixture firmly into balls to ensure they hold together well.
Compact bites prevent crumbling and improve texture. The pressure activates the binding properties of almond butter and chia seeds, creating a cohesive snack. Loose bites tend to fall apart and are harder to eat, reducing enjoyment and portability.
Chill the bites for at least 30 minutes before eating or storing.
Refrigeration firms up the bites by allowing fats and protein to set, improving texture and flavor. Skipping chilling can lead to sticky, soft bites that don’t hold shape, making them messy and less satisfying.

Variations

Peanut Butter Chocolate Protein Bites

Swap almond butter for natural peanut butter for a familiar, nutty flavor. Peanut butter has a slightly higher fat content, so reduce the amount slightly to keep calories low. These bites will be a bit richer and have a classic peanut-chocolate combo that’s always a hit.

Note: Peanut butter tends to be thicker; you might need a splash more almond milk to get the right consistency.

Vegan No Bake Protein Bites

Use a plant-based protein powder like pea or brown rice isolate and replace almond butter with sunflower seed butter for a nut-free vegan option. The texture will be slightly different but still creamy and satisfying. Adjust sweetener to taste as some plant proteins are less sweet.

Note: Plant proteins often absorb more liquid, so add almond milk gradually to avoid a dry mixture.

Berry Infused Protein Bites

Add 2 tbsp freeze-dried berry powder or finely chopped freeze-dried berries for a fruity twist. This adds antioxidants and a natural tartness that balances the chocolate. The bites remain low-calorie but gain a fresh flavor dimension.

Note: Freeze-dried berries don’t add moisture, so no changes needed to liquid quantities.

Mocha Protein Bites

Stir in 1 tsp instant espresso powder with the dry ingredients for a coffee-chocolate flavor. This is perfect for a morning or afternoon pick-me-up. The caffeine adds a little energy boost without extra calories.

Note: Espresso powder dissolves quickly; mix it well with protein powder to avoid gritty spots.

Cinnamon Spice Protein Bites

Add 1 tsp ground cinnamon and a pinch of nutmeg to the dry mix for a warm, cozy flavor. These bites taste like a treat but are still low-calorie and high-protein. Great for fall or winter snacking.

Note: Spices don’t affect texture, so no changes needed to liquids or binders.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

These protein bites should not be kept at room temperature for more than 2 hours, especially in warm conditions, as the almond butter can soften and the texture may become sticky. Keep them covered in a cool place if eating soon.

Refrigerator

Store the bites in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 7 days. Chilling preserves their firmness and flavor. They taste great cold but can be left out for a few minutes before eating if you prefer a softer texture.

Freezer

Freeze the bites for up to 2 months in a sealed container or freezer bag. To thaw, place in the fridge overnight or at room temperature for 30 minutes. Freezing may slightly change texture, making them a bit denser.

Reheating

These bites are best eaten cold or at room temperature. Reheating is not recommended as it can cause separation of almond butter and protein powder, ruining texture.

Make Ahead

Prepare the mixture and form the bites up to 2 days in advance. Store them in the fridge and chill fully before eating. For weekly meal prep, make a larger batch and freeze half for later to maintain freshness.

FAQ

Can I substitute peanut butter for almond butter in this recipe?
Yes, peanut butter can be used as a substitute and will give a classic peanut-chocolate flavor. Just be mindful that peanut butter is slightly higher in fat, so you might want to reduce the amount a bit to keep calories low. Also, peanut butter is thicker, so you may need to add a splash more almond milk to get the right consistency.
What if my protein bites are too dry and crumbly?
If your bites are dry, it usually means there’s not enough liquid to hydrate the protein powder and bind the oats. Try adding almond milk one teaspoon at a time until the mixture holds together when pressed. Also, make sure to mix the wet ingredients well before combining with dry to avoid dry spots.
Can I use a plant-based protein powder instead of whey?
Absolutely! Plant-based isolates like pea or brown rice protein work well but tend to absorb more liquid. You might need to add a bit more almond milk to achieve the right texture. Keep in mind some plant proteins have a stronger flavor, so adjust sweetener to taste.
How long do these protein bites last in the fridge?
Stored in an airtight container, these bites stay fresh and firm for up to 7 days. They may soften slightly over time but should still hold together well. If you want to keep them longer, freezing is a good option.
Can I skip the sugar-free syrup?
You can omit the syrup if you prefer, but the bites will be less sweet and might taste a bit bland depending on your protein powder’s flavor. To keep sweetness without calories, you could add a pinch of stevia or monk fruit sweetener instead.
Why do I need to chill the bites before eating?
Chilling firms up the mixture by allowing the fats and protein to set, which improves texture and makes the bites less sticky. Without chilling, the bites can be soft and crumbly, making them harder to eat and less enjoyable.
Can I add other mix-ins like nuts or dried fruit?
You can, but keep in mind nuts and dried fruit add calories quickly. Use small amounts to keep the snack low-calorie. Also, dried fruit can add moisture or stickiness, so adjust almond milk accordingly to maintain bite consistency.
Is this recipe suitable for meal prep?
Definitely! These bites store well in the fridge and freezer, making them perfect for meal prep. You can make a batch on Sunday and have ready-to-eat snacks all week. Just keep them chilled for best texture.
