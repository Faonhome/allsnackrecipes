Easy Make-Ahead Grilled Butternut Squash Boxes for High-Protein Low-Calorie Snacking

10 min Prep Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty
4.5 (46 ratings)✓ Thanks for rating!

This grilled butternut squash snack is perfect for meal prep—low in calories but packed with fiber and volume to keep you full without guilt. I love pairing it with a dollop of zero-fat Greek yogurt for a protein boost. Great for afternoon cravings or post-workout fuel!

Introduction

I’ve always struggled with feeling hungry on low-calorie days, especially when I’m cutting or trying to eat clean. Small snacks just didn’t cut it—I needed something that gave me volume without packing on the calories. That’s where this grilled butternut squash snack comes in. It’s one of my favorite go-to’s because it feels like I’m eating a huge bowl but I’m only taking in about 150 calories per serving. The trick is grilling the squash to bring out its natural sweetness and pairing it with a protein-rich dip made from zero-fat Greek yogurt and whipped egg whites. This combo keeps me full for hours and satisfies my craving for something savory and creamy without the fat. I’ve tried making dips with just yogurt before, but it was too runny, so I started adding a little xanthan gum to thicken it up without adding calories or fat. Plus, the smoked paprika adds a nice smoky kick that makes this snack feel special. I usually prep a batch on Sundays and keep them in containers for quick snacks during the week—perfect for work, post-gym, or even Netflix binges when I want something tasty but guilt-free. Honestly, a friend once asked me how I eat so much squash and still lose weight—this is the secret!

What You'll Need

6 snack servings, approximately 1 cup grilled squash plus 2 tbsp protein dip per serving

  • 1 medium butternut squash, peeled and sliced into 1/2 inch thick rounds
  • 1 tsp olive oil (optional, for grilling)
  • 1 cup zero-fat Greek yogurt
  • 3 large egg whites, lightly whipped
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • Fresh parsley, chopped (for garnish)
  • 1 tsp xanthan gum (optional, to thicken yogurt dip)
  • Stevia or monk fruit sweetener, to taste (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by peeling the butternut squash and slicing it into uniform 1/2 inch thick rounds. This thickness ensures the squash cooks evenly on the grill without becoming mushy. If the slices are too thin, they’ll dry out; too thick and they won’t soften enough. Use a sharp knife and be careful—squash skin can be tough. Evenly sized slices help with consistent grilling and portion control.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Slices should be uniform and about 1/2 inch thick, smooth on the cut edges.
Step 2
Preheat your grill or grill pan to medium heat. Lightly brush the squash slices with a teaspoon of olive oil to prevent sticking and enhance caramelization, but keep it minimal to stay low-calorie. Grill the slices for 3-4 minutes per side until tender and grill marks appear. Don’t rush this step—proper grilling brings out the natural sugars and flavor without adding calories.
⏱ Time: 8 minutes
✓ Doneness: Golden grill marks on both sides and squash is tender when pierced with a fork.
Step 3
While the squash grills, prepare the protein dip by combining zero-fat Greek yogurt with lightly whipped egg whites. Whipping the egg whites adds volume and fluffiness without fat, making the dip feel creamy and substantial. Add a teaspoon of xanthan gum slowly while whisking to thicken the mixture and avoid watery separation. This step prevents the dip from being runny and watery, which is common with low-fat dairy.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dip should be thick, airy, and hold soft peaks without separating.
Step 4
Season the dip with smoked paprika, salt, pepper, and a touch of stevia or monk fruit sweetener if you want a slight hint of sweetness to balance the smoky flavor. Mix well to combine. The spices add depth and flavor without adding calories, making the snack more satisfying and less bland.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dip is evenly seasoned with a smooth, creamy texture.
Step 5
Plate the grilled butternut squash slices in a large bowl or meal prep containers. Spoon about 2 tablespoons of the protein dip over or beside the squash. The dip adds a creamy texture and a protein punch that complements the naturally sweet and tender squash perfectly.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Each serving has visible grilled squash and a dollop of thick dip.
Step 6
Garnish with fresh chopped parsley for a pop of color and freshness. Parsley also adds a subtle herbal note that brightens the dish without adding calories. This finishing touch makes the snack look more appetizing and inviting.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Bright green parsley scattered evenly on top.
Step 7
Let the snack cool to room temperature before covering if storing for meal prep. This prevents condensation and sogginess in the containers. Proper cooling helps maintain the texture of the grilled squash and the dip’s consistency.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Squash and dip are cool but not cold, no visible moisture inside container.
Step 8
Store the snack in airtight containers in the fridge for up to 4 days. The grilled squash holds up well, and the protein dip thickens slightly when chilled, making it even creamier. Just give it a quick stir before eating to restore fluffiness.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Snack is chilled and firm but creamy.
Step 9
When ready to eat, enjoy cold or let the squash warm slightly to room temperature. Avoid reheating the dip as the egg whites and xanthan gum may separate and lose texture. This snack is best enjoyed fresh or chilled for maximum flavor and texture.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Squash is tender and dip is fluffy and creamy.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping xanthan gum and ending up with watery dip.

Why it happens Many assume yogurt alone will thicken the dip, but zero-fat yogurt is often runny and separates when mixed with egg whites.

Fix Add 1 tsp xanthan gum slowly while whisking to thicken and stabilize the dip, preventing watery texture.

Using too much oil on squash, increasing calories.

Why it happens People often over-brush squash thinking it needs oil to grill properly, unaware it adds unnecessary calories.

Fix Use just 1 tsp total for all slices, brushed lightly to prevent sticking but keep calories low.

Not whipping egg whites leading to dense dip.

Why it happens Some mix egg whites directly without whipping, missing out on volume and fluffiness.

Fix Whip egg whites to soft peaks before folding into yogurt for airy texture and added volume.

Grilling squash slices unevenly thick causing inconsistent cooking.

Why it happens Without uniform slicing, some pieces cook too fast or stay hard.

Fix Slice all pieces about 1/2 inch thick using a sharp knife for even grilling.

Reheating dip causing separation and loss of texture.

Why it happens Heat breaks down egg whites and xanthan gum structure.

Fix Eat dip cold or at room temperature; do not microwave or heat.

Pro Tips

Use a sharp knife and peel the squash carefully to get even slices.
Even slices ensure uniform grilling and prevent some pieces from drying out or being undercooked. Uneven thickness can lead to inconsistent texture and flavor, making the snack less enjoyable.
Lightly brush squash with oil to prevent sticking but keep it minimal.
A tiny amount of oil helps with caramelization and prevents the squash from sticking to the grill, but too much adds unnecessary calories. This balance enhances flavor without compromising the low-calorie goal.
Whip egg whites separately before folding into yogurt for fluffiness.
Whipping egg whites traps air, increasing volume and creating a light, creamy dip texture. This technique adds protein and volume without fat or calories, making the snack more satisfying.
Add xanthan gum slowly while whisking to avoid clumps.
Xanthan gum thickens the dip and prevents watery separation but can form clumps if added too fast. Gradual incorporation ensures smooth texture and better mouthfeel.
Season the dip with spices to boost flavor without calories.
Low-calorie snacks can taste bland; spices add complexity and make the snack more enjoyable, helping you stick to your diet without feeling deprived.
Cool grilled squash before storing to prevent sogginess.
Storing warm squash traps moisture and causes soggy texture. Cooling preserves firmness and makes meal prep containers more appealing.
Avoid reheating the dip to maintain texture.
Heat can cause egg whites and xanthan gum to break down, resulting in separation and loss of creaminess. The dip tastes best chilled or at room temperature.

Variations

Spicy Chipotle Grilled Butternut Squash Snack

Add chipotle powder and a squeeze of lime to the dip for a smoky, spicy twist. The heat pairs well with sweet squash and adds complexity. Macros stay similar, but the flavor is bolder, perfect for spice lovers.

Note: Use chipotle powder sparingly to avoid overpowering; lime juice can thin dip slightly so adjust xanthan gum accordingly.

Herbed Protein Dip with Fresh Dill and Chives

Mix fresh dill and chives into the yogurt-egg white dip for a fresh, garden flavor. This variation adds zero calories but enhances taste and freshness, making the snack feel more gourmet.

Note: Chop herbs finely and fold gently to preserve dip fluffiness.

Vegan Version with Silken Tofu and Nutritional Yeast

Replace yogurt and egg whites with blended silken tofu and nutritional yeast for a plant-based protein dip. The texture is creamy but slightly denser; expect slightly different mouthfeel and flavor.

Note: Blend tofu until very smooth and add xanthan gum to thicken; chill before serving.

Add Roasted Red Pepper Puree for Sweetness and Color

Fold in pureed roasted red peppers to the dip for a sweet, colorful variation. This adds natural sweetness and vitamins without many calories, making the snack more vibrant and flavorful.

Note: Puree peppers finely and strain to avoid watery dip; adjust xanthan gum as needed.

Serve with Steamed Edamame for Extra Protein

Add a side of steamed edamame pods to increase protein content and add a satisfying bite. This combo boosts protein by 7-8 grams per serving with minimal calorie increase.

Note: Steam edamame just before serving to keep texture firm and fresh.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This snack should not be left at room temperature for more than 2 hours due to the egg whites and yogurt. Keep covered if out briefly. Warm temps may cause water separation and loss of dip texture.

Refrigerator

Store in airtight containers for up to 4 days. The dip thickens and firms up when chilled, and the grilled squash stays tender. The snack tastes best cold or at room temperature.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as the high water content in squash and yogurt causes icy texture and separation upon thawing.

Reheating

Avoid reheating the dip; it will separate and lose creaminess. Squash can be warmed briefly, but best enjoyed at room temp or chilled.

Make Ahead

Grill squash and prepare dip 1-2 days ahead. Store separately if possible to keep dip fresh. Mix just before eating to maintain volume and texture.

FAQ

Can I substitute the zero-fat Greek yogurt with regular yogurt?
You can, but regular yogurt has more fat and calories, which changes the macro balance and texture. The dip may be creamier but less low-calorie. For best results, stick to zero-fat Greek yogurt to keep protein high and calories low.
How do I avoid the dip tasting chalky from protein or egg whites?
Whip egg whites well to add air and volume, and add xanthan gum slowly while whisking to prevent watery separation. Also, use fresh yogurt and season well with spices to mask any chalkiness.
Can I make this snack vegan?
Yes! Use silken tofu blended with nutritional yeast instead of yogurt and egg whites. The texture will be a bit different—denser and less fluffy—but still creamy and protein-rich.
How long does this snack keep in the fridge?
Stored in airtight containers, it keeps well for up to 4 days. The dip thickens when chilled but remains tasty. Give the dip a quick stir before eating to restore fluffiness.
Is the olive oil necessary for grilling?
Not strictly, but a light brush prevents sticking and helps caramelize the squash for better flavor. Use minimal oil to keep calories low.
Can I use pre-cut butternut squash?
Yes, but check for added preservatives or oils that increase calories. Freshly sliced squash gives better texture and flavor.
What if I don’t have xanthan gum?
You can skip it, but the dip may be runnier and separate faster. Alternatively, a small amount of chia seeds can thicken, but they change texture and appearance.
When is the best time to eat this snack?
It’s great for post-workout recovery due to the protein, or as a filling afternoon snack to curb hunger without excess calories.
