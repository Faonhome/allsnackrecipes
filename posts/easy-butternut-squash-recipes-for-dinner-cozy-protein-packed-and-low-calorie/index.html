Easy Butternut Squash Recipes For Dinner: Cozy, Protein-Packed, and Low-Calorie

Published on October 3, 2026
10 min Prep Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty
4.6 (49 ratings)✓ Thanks for rating!

These easy butternut squash dinners are surprisingly high in protein and low in calories, perfect for when you want a filling meal without the guilt. Using roasted squash with lean chicken and a touch of spices, they’re great for weeknight dinners or meal prep.

Introduction

I've always struggled with feeling hungry on low-calorie diets, especially when I want to eat enough food to feel satisfied. Butternut squash is a game changer for me because it’s naturally sweet, low in calories, and super filling thanks to its fiber and water content. Pairing it with lean chicken and zero-fat Greek yogurt boosts the protein without adding unwanted fats. I love roasting the squash with a little smoked paprika and thyme to bring out its cozy flavors. One trick I’ve learned is to add the yogurt just before serving to keep the creamy texture without watering down the dish. This recipe gives me a huge portion that feels like a real meal, not a tiny snack, and it keeps me full well into the evening. I’ve tried other squash recipes that ended up watery or bland, but roasting and layering in fresh veggies really makes this one stand out. Plus, it’s super easy to prep for busy weeknights or meal prep Sundays. If you want a comforting dinner that fits your macros and tastes delicious, you’ll love this.

What You'll Need

Approximately 1 large bowl per serving, 6 servings total, about 2 cups volume per serving

  • 4 cups peeled and cubed butternut squash
  • 1 cup cooked shredded chicken breast
  • 1/2 cup diced bell peppers (red or yellow)
  • 1/2 cup chopped spinach
  • 1/4 cup chopped red onion
  • 1/2 cup zero-fat Greek yogurt
  • 1 tsp olive oil (optional, for roasting)
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • 1 tsp chopped fresh thyme or rosemary

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 400°F (200°C). Toss the peeled and cubed butternut squash with olive oil (if using), smoked paprika, fresh thyme, salt, and pepper. Spread the cubes evenly on a baking sheet lined with parchment paper. Roasting the squash caramelizes its natural sugars, enhancing flavor without added calories. Make sure the cubes are in a single layer to roast evenly and avoid steaming, which can make them mushy.
⏱ Time: 25-30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Squash should be tender and lightly browned on edges when pierced with a fork.
Step 2
While the squash roasts, prepare your vegetables by dicing the bell peppers, chopping the spinach, and finely chopping the red onion. These add crunch and volume without many calories, making the dish more filling. Keep the veggies raw for texture contrast, which helps prevent the bowl from feeling dense or heavy.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Veggies should be fresh, crisp, and evenly chopped.
Step 3
In a large mixing bowl, combine the cooked shredded chicken breast with the chopped vegetables. Chicken provides the high-quality protein that keeps you full and supports muscle repair. Mixing the veggies in now allows their flavors to mingle with the chicken before adding the warm squash.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken and veggies are evenly distributed.
Step 4
Once the squash is roasted and slightly cooled, add it to the chicken and vegetable mixture. The warmth of the squash will gently wilt the spinach and soften the onions just a bit, creating a nice texture balance. Avoid adding the yogurt at this stage to prevent curdling or watery separation.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Ingredients are combined but the squash is still warm, not hot.
Step 5
Gently fold in the zero-fat Greek yogurt just before serving or packing for lunch. This keeps the yogurt creamy without breaking down or separating. The yogurt adds protein and a tangy creaminess that balances the sweetness of the squash and spices.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Yogurt is evenly mixed without watery liquid pooling.
Step 6
Taste and adjust seasoning with a little more salt, pepper, or smoked paprika if needed. This final step ensures the flavors pop and the dish feels satisfying without extra calories from sauces or dressings.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Balanced, flavorful taste with no bland spots.
Step 7
Divide the mixture into six portions for meal prep or immediate serving. Portioning helps keep calories in check and makes it easy to grab a filling, protein-rich meal anytime. Use airtight containers if prepping ahead.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Evenly sized portions, approximately 2 cups each.
Step 8
Store leftovers in the refrigerator and reheat gently in the microwave or on the stovetop. Add a splash of water or almond milk if the mixture seems dry after reheating to maintain creaminess without extra fat.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Warm throughout without drying out or separating.
Step 9
Enjoy your hearty but low-calorie dinner bowl! This recipe works great as a post-workout meal or a satisfying option during cutting phases when you need volume but want to keep calories low.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Feeling full and satisfied without heaviness.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the binder or thickener, resulting in watery texture.

Why it happens People often assume zero-fat yogurt or lean chicken alone will hold the dish together, but without proper technique, the mixture can separate or become watery.

Fix Make sure to roast the squash well to reduce moisture and add yogurt only at the end, folding gently. This keeps the texture creamy and prevents watery separation.

Pro Tips

Don’t skip roasting the squash; it’s key for flavor.
Roasting caramelizes the natural sugars in butternut squash, creating a deeper, richer taste that boiling or steaming can’t match. This method also evaporates excess moisture, preventing the dish from becoming watery when mixed with yogurt and chicken.
Add Greek yogurt last to keep the texture creamy.
Mixing yogurt into hot ingredients too early can cause it to separate or curdle. Adding it just before serving preserves its creamy texture and prevents watery separation, which is especially important in low-fat recipes without much fat to stabilize the mixture.
Use fresh herbs and spices to enhance flavor without calories.
Low-calorie meals can sometimes taste bland, but fresh herbs and spices add complexity and satisfaction without adding fat or sugar. This helps you enjoy your meal fully and reduces cravings for higher-calorie comfort foods.

Variations

Spicy Chipotle Butternut Squash Bowl

Add chipotle powder and a squeeze of lime for a smoky, spicy twist. Swap chicken for turkey breast for a leaner protein option. Expect a slightly bolder flavor and similar texture, with macros staying low-calorie and protein-rich.

Note: Adjust spice levels gradually to avoid overpowering the natural sweetness of the squash.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Due to the yogurt and chicken, this snack should not be left at room temperature for more than 2 hours. Cover it loosely if serving shortly after prep to prevent drying out.

Refrigerator

Store in airtight containers for up to 4 days. The dish tastes great chilled or gently reheated; the protein firms up nicely, and the squash retains its texture. Stir before serving to recombine any separated yogurt.

Freezer

Freeze for up to 1 month in freezer-safe containers. Thaw overnight in the fridge and reheat gently, adding a splash of almond milk if needed to restore creaminess. Avoid freezing yogurt-heavy portions for too long as texture may degrade.

Reheating

Reheat in a microwave or on the stovetop at low power to avoid curdling the yogurt. Add a small amount of water or unsweetened almond milk to loosen the texture if it seems dry.

Make Ahead

Roast the squash and cook the chicken up to 2 days ahead. Store veggies chopped separately and combine with yogurt just before eating to maintain freshness and texture.

FAQ

Can I substitute the chicken with a plant-based protein?
Absolutely! Firm tofu or tempeh can be used instead of chicken for a vegetarian option. Just make sure to press tofu well to remove excess moisture, and pan-sear it to add flavor and texture. Protein content might vary slightly, so adjust portions accordingly.
How do I prevent the yogurt from separating in the dish?
Add yogurt at the very end, after the squash has cooled slightly. Mixing it into hot ingredients can cause curdling or watery separation. Stir gently to keep the creamy texture intact.
Is this recipe suitable for meal prep?
Yes! It stores well in airtight containers for up to 4 days. Keep the yogurt separate if you want to maintain the freshest texture and add it right before eating.
Can I use frozen butternut squash?
You can, but fresh roasting yields better texture and flavor. If using frozen, thaw completely and pat dry before roasting to avoid excess moisture, which can make the dish watery.
How many calories and protein does one serving have?
Each serving is approximately 180 calories with 22 grams of protein, making it a great low-calorie, high-protein dinner option.
Can I add other vegetables?
Definitely! Feel free to add zucchini, mushrooms, or cauliflower for extra volume and nutrients. Just adjust cooking times accordingly to keep textures balanced.
What if I don’t have smoked paprika?
Regular paprika or a pinch of cumin can be good substitutes. The smoky flavor adds depth, but the recipe still works well without it.
Is olive oil necessary for roasting?
It’s optional. A small amount helps with caramelization and flavor, but you can omit it to keep calories even lower. Just watch the squash closely to prevent drying out.
