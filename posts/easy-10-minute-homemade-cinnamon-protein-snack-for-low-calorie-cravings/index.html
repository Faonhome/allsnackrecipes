Easy 10-Minute Homemade Cinnamon Protein Snack for Low-Calorie Cravings

Chef
Published on October 30, 2026
10 min Prep Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty
4.6 (42 ratings)✓ Thanks for rating!

This homemade cinnamon protein snack is a cozy, guilt-free treat packed with 20g of protein and under 150 calories. Using zero-fat Greek yogurt and a sprinkle of warming cinnamon, it’s perfect for a post-workout boost or a satisfying afternoon pick-me-up.

Introduction

I’ve always struggled with feeling hungry on low-calorie days, especially when I’m trying to cut but still want to eat a decent amount of food. Tiny snacks just don’t cut it, and I hate feeling like I’m starving between meals. That’s why I came up with this homemade cinnamon protein snack — it’s like a giant, creamy bowl of comfort that fills you up without wrecking your calories. I love that it’s packed with zero-fat Greek yogurt and whey isolate for a serious protein boost, but still feels like a treat thanks to the warm cinnamon and vanilla. What makes this recipe better than store-bought protein bars or powders mixed with water is the volume and texture — I bulk it up with a little xanthan gum and ice cubes to make it fluffy and thick without adding calories. Plus, the cinnamon adds flavor without sugar, so it feels indulgent but stays light. I’ve tried versions without xanthan gum and they turned out watery, so that’s my secret to keeping it creamy. I usually make a batch on Sundays for the week or whip it up quickly after workouts when I need something filling but light. It’s perfect for meal prep, afternoon slumps, or anytime you want a snack that feels like a real meal but is under 150 calories. My friends always ask how I eat so much and still lose weight — this is one of the reasons!

What You'll Need

Approximately 1 large bowl per serving, 6 snack servings, about 1.5 cups volume per serving

  • 2 cups zero-fat Greek yogurt, cold
  • 1 scoop vanilla whey protein isolate powder
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1 tablespoon powdered erythritol (or preferred zero-calorie sweetener)
  • 1/4 teaspoon xanthan gum (optional, for creaminess and thickening)
  • 1/2 teaspoon pure vanilla extract
  • Ice cubes (optional, for fluffiness)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by chilling your zero-fat Greek yogurt in the fridge so it’s cold and thick. Cold yogurt helps the mixture stay creamy and prevents separation when you mix in protein powder. Use a whisk or electric mixer to smooth out any lumps before adding other ingredients. This step is key because cold yogurt hydrates the protein powder better and keeps the texture silky instead of gritty.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Smooth, lump-free yogurt that looks thick and creamy
Step 2
Add the vanilla whey protein isolate powder and powdered erythritol to the yogurt. Stir gently but thoroughly to combine. Protein isolate hydrates quickly and dissolves better in cold dairy, so take your time to avoid chalkiness. If you rush or mix too aggressively, the texture can get grainy. Use a folding motion to keep it light and creamy.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Evenly mixed powder with no visible clumps or chalkiness
Step 3
Sprinkle in the ground cinnamon and add the pure vanilla extract. Cinnamon not only adds flavor but also helps suppress appetite naturally. Mix gently to incorporate the spices evenly without overworking the yogurt, which can cause it to thin out. The vanilla extract enhances the sweetness without extra calories.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Uniformly speckled mixture with cinnamon and a subtle aroma of vanilla
Step 4
If using, sprinkle the xanthan gum evenly over the surface and whisk briskly to prevent clumping. Xanthan gum is a zero-calorie thickener that mimics fat’s creaminess and prevents watery separation in low-fat snacks. It also helps the snack hold volume longer. Don’t skip this if you want a smooth, fluffy texture.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture thickens slightly and feels smooth rather than runny
Step 5
Optionally, add a few ice cubes and whisk vigorously to create a fluffy, mousse-like texture. The ice adds volume and chills the snack further, making it feel more indulgent and filling without adding calories. Be careful not to over-whisk or the mixture might become too watery as the ice melts.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Light, airy texture with a slight froth on top
Step 6
Taste the mixture and adjust sweetness with more erythritol if needed. Remember, zero-calorie sweeteners can have an aftertaste if overused, so add sparingly. The cinnamon and vanilla should balance the flavor nicely, giving a natural sweetness without sugar.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Balanced sweet-spiced flavor with no bitterness or aftertaste
Step 7
Divide the snack into six equal portions in airtight containers or bowls. Portion control is important to keep calories in check but the volume will still feel generous. This recipe yields about 1.5 cups per serving, which is a satisfying snack size that won’t leave you hungry.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Evenly portioned servings with a consistent creamy texture
Step 8
Refrigerate the portions for at least 30 minutes before eating. Chilling firms up the texture and helps the flavors meld. Eating it cold also enhances the creamy mouthfeel and reduces any potential chalkiness from the protein powder.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Snack is firm but creamy, not watery or separated
Step 9
Before serving, give each portion a gentle stir to fluff it up again. This snack is best eaten cold straight from the fridge, perfect for a quick, high-protein low-calorie boost that feels like a treat but won’t derail your goals.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Light, creamy texture ready to enjoy

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping xanthan gum and ending up with watery, separated snack.

Why it happens People often underestimate the need for a thickener in fat-free dairy snacks, assuming yogurt alone will hold the texture. Without xanthan gum, the mixture separates as protein and water separate.

Fix Add a small amount (about 1/4 teaspoon per batch) of xanthan gum evenly sprinkled and whisked in to stabilize and thicken the snack. This prevents watery separation and improves mouthfeel.

Mixing protein powder too vigorously causing chalky texture.

Why it happens Rushing the mixing process or using warm liquids causes protein powder to clump and not hydrate properly, creating a gritty mouthfeel.

Fix Mix protein powder gently into cold yogurt using folding motions or a whisk to ensure smooth hydration and avoid chalkiness.

Using full-fat yogurt and increasing calories unknowingly.

Why it happens Some assume any Greek yogurt is low-calorie, but full-fat versions add significant calories and fat, undermining the low-calorie goal.

Fix Always choose zero-fat Greek yogurt and check nutrition labels carefully to keep calories low.

Pro Tips

Whisk protein powder gently into cold yogurt to avoid chalkiness.
Protein powders, especially isolates, can clump and taste chalky if mixed too aggressively or in warm liquids. Mixing gently in cold yogurt allows the powder to hydrate fully and evenly, creating a smooth texture without gritty bits.
Use xanthan gum sparingly to thicken and stabilize the snack.
Xanthan gum mimics the mouthfeel of fat and keeps low-fat mixtures creamy and stable. Without it, watery separation occurs quickly, ruining texture. Too much xanthan gum, however, makes the snack gummy and unpleasant.
Chill the snack before eating for best texture and flavor.
Cold temperatures firm the yogurt and protein mixture, enhancing creaminess and reducing any chalky taste. It also helps the flavors meld, making the cinnamon and vanilla more pronounced.

Variations

Pumpkin Spice Cinnamon Protein Snack

Add 1/4 cup canned pumpkin puree and a pinch of nutmeg and cloves to the base recipe for a seasonal twist. This adds fiber and moisture, slightly increasing calories but boosting volume and flavor. Expect a creamier texture and richer taste that still stays under 180 calories per serving.

Note: Add pumpkin puree after mixing protein powder and yogurt, then whisk in spices. The extra moisture may require a tiny bit more xanthan gum to maintain thickness.

Chocolate Cinnamon Protein Bowl

Mix in 1 tablespoon unsweetened cocoa powder and swap vanilla protein powder for chocolate flavor. The cocoa adds antioxidants and a rich taste that pairs well with cinnamon. Calories remain low, with a slight increase from cocoa powder. Texture stays creamy and thick.

Note: Add cocoa powder with cinnamon and erythritol for even flavor distribution. Use high-quality cocoa for best results.

Vegan Cinnamon Protein Snack

Replace whey isolate with a plant-based pea or rice protein isolate and Greek yogurt with unsweetened almond yogurt. This lowers protein slightly but keeps calories low and texture creamy. Flavor is slightly earthier but still delicious with cinnamon and vanilla.

Note: Plant proteins hydrate differently; mix slowly and chill longer to improve texture. Xanthan gum remains important.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This snack should not be left out at room temperature for more than 1 hour due to the dairy content. Keep it covered to prevent drying or contamination, but it's best eaten chilled.

Refrigerator

Store in airtight containers for up to 4 days. The snack firms up and flavors meld when chilled, maintaining a creamy texture. Stir gently before eating to fluff up the texture.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as the high water content and dairy can cause icy texture and separation upon thawing. If frozen, thaw slowly in the fridge and stir well before serving.

Reheating

This snack is best eaten cold and should not be reheated. Heating can cause separation and loss of creamy texture.

Make Ahead

Prepare the full batch and portion into containers to refrigerate. Add ice cubes fresh before eating if you want extra fluffiness. Xanthan gum helps maintain texture over storage.

FAQ

Can I substitute the whey protein isolate with plant-based protein?
Yes, you can use plant-based protein isolates like pea or rice protein, but expect a slightly different texture and flavor. Plant proteins hydrate differently and may require longer chilling or additional xanthan gum to achieve similar creaminess.
Why does my snack turn watery after mixing?
Watery texture usually happens if you skip xanthan gum or overmix, causing separation of water from protein and yogurt. Adding a small amount of xanthan gum and mixing gently helps maintain a thick, creamy texture.
How do I avoid chalkiness from protein powder?
Mix protein powder gently into cold yogurt and avoid using warm liquids. Whisk slowly to hydrate the powder fully without clumping. Using whey isolate also reduces chalkiness compared to concentrate.
Is this snack suitable for cutting or weight loss?
Absolutely! It’s high in protein and low in calories, which supports satiety and muscle maintenance while keeping calorie intake low. The volume and creamy texture help prevent hunger between meals.
Can I add sweeteners other than erythritol?
Yes, you can use stevia or monk fruit sweeteners, but add them sparingly to avoid aftertaste. Powdered forms blend better in cold mixtures and avoid graininess.
How long can I store this snack in the fridge?
Store in airtight containers for up to 4 days. The texture and flavor hold up well when chilled, but stir gently before serving to restore fluffiness.
Can I make this snack ahead for meal prep?
Definitely. It’s perfect for meal prep. Prepare the full batch, portion it out, and refrigerate. Add ice cubes fresh before eating if you want extra fluffiness.
What if I don’t have xanthan gum?
Without xanthan gum, the snack will be thinner and may separate faster. You can try adding a small amount of chia seeds soaked in water as a natural thickener, but texture will differ.
