Make-Ahead Slow Cooker Tomato Basil Soup: High Protein, Low Calorie Comfort

Published on July 21, 2026
10 min Prep Time
240 min Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This slow cooker tomato basil soup is a cozy, protein-packed delight that’s light on calories but big on flavor. Using cottage cheese and fresh basil, it’s perfect for meal prep or a comforting snack when you want something filling but guilt-free.

Introduction

I’ve always struggled when cutting calories because small portions leave me hungry and cranky. That’s why I love this slow cooker tomato basil soup—it’s a giant, comforting bowl that actually fills me up without blowing my calorie budget. Unlike store-bought soups that are either too salty or loaded with cream, this recipe uses low-fat cottage cheese to add protein and creaminess without the calories. The trick is blending the cottage cheese in to keep it silky smooth but still high in protein. I also add a pinch of xanthan gum to thicken it just right, so it feels indulgent but stays light. Plus, cooking it slow lets the flavors deepen, and the fresh basil adds a bright, herbal note that makes the soup feel fresh and satisfying. I usually make a big batch on Sundays, and it’s my go-to afternoon snack when I’m craving something warm and filling. It’s amazing how much volume you get for under 150 calories a cup! Honestly, I had a batch that was too watery before I added the xanthan gum, so don’t skip that if you want the best texture. This soup feels like a treat but fits perfectly into my macros, and my friends always ask how I eat so much and still lose weight. It’s all about smart ingredients and volume eating!

What You'll Need

Approximately 1.5 cups of soup per serving, 6 servings total

  • 6 cups fresh tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 cup low-fat cottage cheese
  • 1 large onion, diced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 cups low-sodium vegetable broth
  • 1/4 cup fresh basil leaves, chopped
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • 1 tsp xanthan gum (optional, for thickening)
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • 1 tbsp olive oil (optional, minimal for texture)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by prepping all your veggies: chop the tomatoes, dice the onion, and mince the garlic. This step is key because slow cooking gently breaks down the tomatoes and onions, releasing their natural sweetness and deepening the flavor. Make sure the pieces are relatively uniform to cook evenly. Avoid large chunks that won’t soften well during the slow cook. This even texture helps the final blend be smooth and creamy without needing extra fat.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Tomatoes and onions look soft and broken down, with a fragrant, sweet aroma
Step 2
Add the chopped tomatoes, onion, garlic, vegetable broth, oregano, salt, and pepper to your slow cooker. Stir everything together well. Cooking low and slow for 4 hours allows the flavors to meld beautifully without needing oil or cream to boost richness. Resist the urge to open the lid frequently, as this drops the temperature and extends cooking time. The broth should cover the vegetables completely for even cooking.
⏱ Time: 4 hours
✓ Doneness: Soup is bubbling gently and the vegetables are very soft
Step 3
Once cooked, carefully transfer about half the soup to a blender along with the cottage cheese and lemon juice. Blending in cottage cheese adds a creamy texture and a hefty protein boost while keeping calories low. Blend until silky smooth. If you skip blending, the cottage cheese won’t fully incorporate, leading to a grainy texture. Use a high-speed blender or work in batches to avoid overfilling.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Soup is smooth and creamy without lumps, with a rich but light texture
Step 4
Return the blended soup to the slow cooker and stir in the fresh chopped basil. Add xanthan gum if using, sprinkling it evenly while whisking quickly to prevent clumps. This step thickens the soup naturally without fat, giving it that satisfying mouthfeel. Avoid adding xanthan gum dry without mixing well, or you’ll get slimy clumps. Let the soup cook on low an additional 15 minutes to activate the thickener.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Soup thickens slightly, coating the back of a spoon but still pouring easily
Step 5
Taste and adjust seasoning with salt, pepper, or more lemon juice to brighten flavors. The lemon juice cuts through the richness and enhances the tomato’s natural acidity, balancing the soup. If it tastes flat, a little acid goes a long way. This finishing step is important because slow cooking can mellow flavors too much.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Balanced, fresh flavor with a slight tang and herbaceous notes
Step 6
Optionally, drizzle in a teaspoon of olive oil for a silky finish if you want a touch of healthy fat without adding many calories. This step is optional but helps with mouthfeel and richness, especially if you prefer a creamier soup. Use sparingly to keep calories low.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Oil disperses evenly, adding a subtle sheen to the surface
Step 7
Serve hot with a sprinkle of fresh basil or cracked black pepper on top. Portion out about 1.5 cups per serving to get a filling snack that’s under 150 calories but packs over 20 grams of protein. The volume eating aspect means you get a large bowl that satisfies hunger and helps with satiety during calorie deficit phases.
⏱ Time: Immediate
✓ Doneness: Soup is steaming hot with vibrant color and fresh herb aroma
Step 8
To store leftovers, cool soup completely before transferring to airtight containers. This prevents condensation and preserves texture and flavor. When reheating, warm gently on the stove or microwave, stirring occasionally to prevent separation. Avoid overheating which can cause the protein to curdle or the xanthan gum to break down.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes reheating
✓ Doneness: Soup is hot and creamy without separation or graininess
Step 9
For meal prep, divide soup into 6 containers for grab-and-go high protein low calorie snacks. This recipe holds well refrigerated for up to 4 days, making it perfect for busy weeks. Just reheat as needed and enjoy a comforting, filling snack anytime.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Soup maintains creamy texture and fresh flavor after storage

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Soup turns out watery and thin.

Why it happens Often caused by skipping xanthan gum or not blending the cottage cheese properly, which leaves the soup lacking body and creaminess.

Fix Be sure to blend cottage cheese fully into the soup and add xanthan gum gradually while whisking. This thickens the soup naturally without fat. Also, cook long enough for water to reduce slightly.

Chalky or grainy texture in the soup.

Why it happens Happens when protein powder or cottage cheese isn’t blended well, or if the soup is overheated causing curdling.

Fix Use a high-speed blender and blend in batches. Reheat gently and avoid boiling after adding dairy. Adding lemon juice at blending helps prevent chalkiness by balancing pH.

Soup tastes bland or flat.

Why it happens Not enough seasoning or lemon juice, or using underripe tomatoes with low natural acidity.

Fix Adjust salt, pepper, and lemon juice at the end. Use ripe tomatoes and fresh basil for bright flavor. Slow cook long enough to develop sweetness.

Xanthan gum clumps in the soup.

Why it happens Adding xanthan gum too quickly or dry without whisking causes clumping.

Fix Sprinkle xanthan gum slowly while whisking vigorously to evenly distribute it. Alternatively, dissolve it in a small amount of cold broth before adding.

Using full-fat cottage cheese or adding too much oil, increasing calories.

Why it happens People often don’t check labels or add more oil for texture, which defeats the low-calorie goal.

Fix Always choose fat-free or low-fat cottage cheese and limit oil to 1 tsp or less. This keeps calories low while maintaining creaminess.

Pro Tips

Blend the cottage cheese with the soup in batches for the smoothest texture.
Blending in smaller amounts ensures the cottage cheese fully incorporates without lumps or graininess. A high-speed blender helps achieve a silky consistency that mimics cream without adding fat. Skipping this can result in a chalky or curdled texture.
Add xanthan gum slowly while whisking to avoid clumps.
Xanthan gum thickens the soup without calories but can clump if added too quickly or dry. Whisking it in gradually distributes it evenly, creating a creamy mouthfeel that replaces fat’s texture. Clumps ruin the smoothness and make the soup unpleasant.
Use fresh ripe tomatoes for best flavor and natural sweetness.
Ripe tomatoes have more natural sugars and acidity, which balance the savory and creamy elements of the soup. They also provide more volume and water content, enhancing satiety. Using underripe or canned tomatoes with additives can dull the flavor.
Cook the soup on low heat for at least 4 hours to develop deep flavor.
Slow cooking breaks down the tomatoes and onions gently, releasing natural sweetness and blending flavors without needing added sugar or fat. Rushing this step results in a flat, less satisfying soup.
Add lemon juice at the blending stage to brighten the flavor.
Lemon juice cuts through the creaminess and acidity of the tomatoes, balancing the soup’s flavor profile. Adding it too early can cause bitterness or dull the fresh basil’s aroma.
Store leftovers in airtight containers and reheat gently to preserve texture.
Low-fat, protein-rich soups can separate or become grainy if overheated. Gentle reheating preserves the creamy texture and flavor, making your meal prep last longer and taste fresh.
Use minimal olive oil only if you want a silkier mouthfeel without many added calories.
Olive oil adds richness and helps the soup feel indulgent but adds calories quickly. Use sparingly to keep the snack low calorie while improving texture and flavor.

Variations

Vegan Version with Silken Tofu

Swap cottage cheese for silken tofu blended into the soup for a vegan-friendly protein boost. The texture stays creamy and protein content remains high, though slightly less than dairy. Use nutritional yeast for a cheesy flavor. Macros shift to slightly lower protein and fat-free.

Note: Silken tofu blends smoothly but may need extra lemon juice or nutritional yeast for flavor depth. Xanthan gum helps maintain thickness.

Spicy Kick with Red Pepper Flakes

Add 1/2 tsp red pepper flakes during slow cooking for a warming spicy note. This variation adds zero calories but heightens flavor complexity, making the soup more exciting. Protein and calories remain unchanged.

Note: Add spices early to infuse flavor fully. Adjust heat level to taste.

Adding White Beans for Extra Fiber

Blend in 1/2 cup cooked white beans for additional fiber and creaminess. This increases volume and keeps calories low while adding about 3-4g more protein per serving. The texture becomes thicker and heartier.

Note: Puree beans thoroughly with soup to avoid graininess. May need slightly more broth.

Greek Yogurt Swap for Cottage Cheese

Use 1 cup 0% Greek yogurt instead of cottage cheese for a tangier flavor and smooth texture. Protein remains high but expect a slightly different mouthfeel. Yogurt adds probiotics and creaminess without fat.

Note: Add yogurt off-heat to prevent curdling and stir gently.

Roasted Tomato Basil Soup

Roast tomatoes and garlic before adding to slow cooker for a deeper, caramelized flavor. This adds complexity without calories but requires extra prep time. Protein and macros stay the same.

Note: Roasting concentrates flavor; reduce broth slightly to maintain consistency.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This soup should not be left at room temperature for more than 2 hours due to its dairy content. Keep covered and refrigerate promptly to maintain safety and texture. Warm temperatures can cause separation and loss of volume.

Refrigerator

Store in airtight containers for up to 4 days. The soup thickens slightly when chilled but reheats smoothly. Flavor often improves after a day as ingredients meld. Reheat gently to preserve creamy texture.

Freezer

Freeze in airtight containers for up to 2 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge. Texture may become slightly grainy after freezing but can be smoothed out with gentle blending or stirring upon reheating.

Reheating

Reheat on low heat on the stove or in short microwave bursts, stirring frequently. Avoid boiling to prevent curdling of cottage cheese and breakdown of xanthan gum. Add a splash of vegetable broth or water if too thick.

Make Ahead

Prepare the soup base and slow cook up to 2 days ahead. Blend in cottage cheese and basil fresh before serving to preserve texture and flavor. Store components separately if possible to keep volume and freshness for meal prep.

FAQ

Can I use protein powder instead of cottage cheese in this soup?
You can, but it’s tricky. Protein powder can become chalky or grainy when heated, especially without fat. If you try it, add protein powder after cooking and blend off heat with some cold broth or almond milk to prevent clumping. Cottage cheese works better for creaminess and texture.
Is this soup suitable for cutting or weight loss diets?
Absolutely! It’s low calorie but high in protein and volume, which helps keep you full and supports muscle retention. The large portion size tricks your brain into feeling satisfied without excess calories.
How do I prevent the soup from separating when reheating?
Reheat gently on low heat and stir often. Avoid boiling the soup, which causes dairy to curdle and xanthan gum to break down. Adding a splash of broth or water can help maintain smooth texture.
Can I make this soup in a regular pot instead of a slow cooker?
Yes, just simmer gently for about 45-60 minutes until tomatoes and onions are soft. Stir occasionally to prevent sticking. The slow cooker just makes it easier to develop deeper flavors with less attention.
What if I don’t have xanthan gum? Can I skip it?
You can skip it but the soup will be thinner and less creamy. To thicken naturally, simmer uncovered to reduce liquid or add a small amount of pureed white beans or cooked cauliflower.
How do I count calories accurately for this soup?
Measure your ingredients carefully and use nutrition info from your specific cottage cheese and broth brands. Portion the soup into equal servings to keep track. Avoid adding extra oils or toppings that increase calories.
Can I add other vegetables to this soup?
Yes! Low-calorie veggies like zucchini or celery blend well and add volume without many calories. Just adjust broth amounts to keep the right consistency.
How long does this soup keep in the fridge?
Stored in airtight containers, it stays fresh up to 4 days. The flavor often improves after a day as ingredients meld. Always smell and check before eating.
