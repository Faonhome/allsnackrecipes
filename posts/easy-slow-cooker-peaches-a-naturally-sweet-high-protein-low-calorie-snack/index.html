Easy Slow Cooker Peaches: A Naturally Sweet High-Protein Low-Calorie Snack

Chef
Published on August 29, 2026
10 min Prep Time
240 min Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty
4.9 (53 ratings)✓ Thanks for rating!

These slow cooker peaches are a juicy, naturally sweet snack that pairs beautifully with a dollop of zero-fat Greek yogurt for a high-protein, low-calorie treat. The slow cooking intensifies the peach flavor without added sugar, perfect for a guilt-free afternoon boost or post-workout snack.

Introduction

I’ve always struggled with feeling hungry on low-calorie days, especially when cutting. Small snacks just don’t cut it for me — I want something that fills my stomach without wrecking my macros. That’s why I love this slow cooker peaches recipe. It’s juicy and satisfying, but with almost no calories and a ton of natural sweetness. The slow cooking really brings out the peaches’ flavor, and pairing them with zero-fat Greek yogurt adds that creamy protein punch I crave. Plus, the volume is impressive — you get a generous bowl that feels like a treat but is totally guilt-free. I used to make the mistake of rushing the cooking time and ending up with mushy fruit, but letting the peaches cook low and slow keeps them tender yet intact. The trick to keeping the snack low-calorie but creamy is the Greek yogurt; it’s thick without any fat, so no greasy mouthfeel here. I’ve been eating this every afternoon lately, especially when I need a little something after the gym or during that 3pm energy crash. It’s easy to prep on Sundays and lasts all week, which makes meal prep a breeze. Friends keep asking how I eat so much peachy goodness and still stay on track — it’s the volume and protein combo that does the trick!

What You'll Need

Approximately 1/2 cup cooked peaches per serving, 6 snack servings

  • 6 large fresh peaches, halved and pitted
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 teaspoon pure vanilla extract
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 1 cup zero-fat Greek yogurt (for serving)
  • Stevia or monk fruit sweetener, to taste (optional)
  • 1/2 cup water

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by washing and halving your peaches, removing the pits carefully. This is important because leaving the pits in can add bitterness if they crack during cooking. Arrange the peach halves in the slow cooker with the cut side facing up; this helps them hold their shape better as they cook. Sprinkle the ground cinnamon evenly over the peaches to add warm spice without extra calories. Pour the lemon juice over everything to brighten the flavors and prevent browning. Adding water creates the perfect moisture environment, ensuring the peaches cook evenly without drying out. Avoid skipping the lemon juice because it keeps the peaches vibrant and fresh-tasting.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Peaches arranged evenly, cinnamon and lemon juice coating visible, water pooled gently at the bottom
Step 2
Set your slow cooker on low heat and cook the peaches for about 4 hours. This slow, gentle cooking allows the fruit to soften perfectly while concentrating their natural sugars, giving you that rich peach flavor without added sweeteners. Resist the urge to open the lid too often—every peek releases heat and extends cooking time. If you’re in a hurry, you can cook on high for 2 hours, but the texture won’t be as tender. The goal is peaches that are soft but still hold their shape, not mushy or falling apart.
⏱ Time: 240 minutes
✓ Doneness: Peaches are tender to the touch but still hold their shape, aroma is sweet and inviting
Step 3
Once cooked, carefully remove the peaches with a slotted spoon into a bowl. The cooking liquid will be syrupy and fragrant—feel free to drizzle a little over the peaches for extra flavor. If you want a touch of sweetness, stir in a small amount of stevia or monk fruit sweetener into the liquid before serving. This keeps added calories minimal while enhancing the natural sweetness of the peaches. Avoid using sugar or honey here to keep the snack low-calorie.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Peaches glistening with syrup, liquid slightly thickened and sweet-smelling
Step 4
Serve the warm peaches topped with a generous scoop (about 1/4 cup) of zero-fat Greek yogurt. The yogurt adds a creamy texture and a solid protein boost that balances the sweetness. Stir gently to combine if you like a creamier bowl or keep separate for texture contrast. The cool yogurt against warm peaches is delightful and helps keep the snack refreshing. If you want extra volume, add a sprinkle of cinnamon or a few fresh berries on top without adding many calories.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Creamy yogurt topping contrasts with warm peaches, cinnamon dusting visible
Step 5
For meal prep, divide the peaches and yogurt into six airtight containers. Store in the refrigerator for up to 4 days. The peaches will continue to absorb some of the yogurt’s creaminess, making the snack even better the next day. When ready to eat, you can enjoy it cold or warm it slightly in the microwave for 20 seconds to bring out the peach aroma. Avoid overheating as it can cause the yogurt to separate and lose its creamy texture.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Snacks portioned evenly in containers, ready for fridge storage
Step 6
If you want to add a little texture, sprinkle a tablespoon of air-popped popcorn or a few sliced almonds on top just before serving. This adds crunch but keep it minimal to stay low-calorie. The contrast between soft peaches, creamy yogurt, and crunchy topping makes the snack feel indulgent without extra fat or sugar. Be mindful not to add too much as nuts and popcorn can quickly increase calorie count.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Crunchy topping visible, snack looks inviting and balanced
Step 7
To keep the peaches from becoming watery after refrigeration, drain any excess liquid before serving. This prevents the snack from feeling soggy and keeps the texture pleasant. If you prefer a saucier snack, stir the liquid back in. The liquid is flavorful but can dilute the yogurt’s creaminess if too much is added.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Peaches appear plump but not swimming in liquid
Step 8
Experiment with spices like nutmeg or ginger for a twist on the classic cinnamon flavor. Adding a pinch of these spices before cooking can enhance the natural sweetness without calories and add a cozy aroma perfect for cooler days. Start with a small amount to avoid overpowering the peaches.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Spices evenly sprinkled and blended with peaches
Step 9
For a protein boost without yogurt, try folding in whipped egg whites sweetened lightly with stevia once the peaches are cooled. This creates a fluffy, mousse-like texture that’s still low-calorie and high-protein. Whip the egg whites to soft peaks and fold gently to avoid deflating. This technique adds volume and creaminess without fat.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Egg whites form soft peaks and blend smoothly with peaches

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using overripe peaches that become mushy.

Why it happens People often grab the softest peaches thinking they’ll cook faster, but they break down too much in the slow cooker.

Fix Choose peaches that are ripe but still firm to the touch. This ensures they hold their shape during the 4-hour cooking process.

Skipping lemon juice and ending with dull, brown peaches.

Why it happens Many underestimate the role of acidity in preserving fruit color.

Fix Always toss peaches in fresh lemon juice before cooking to keep them bright and fresh-looking.

Adding too much sweetener and increasing calories.

Why it happens It’s tempting to add sugar for sweetness, but peaches naturally sweeten during slow cooking.

Fix Use natural zero-calorie sweeteners sparingly or skip them. Taste before adding any sweetener.

Overcooking on high heat causing mushy texture.

Why it happens Rushing the slow cooker can ruin the peach texture.

Fix Stick to low heat for 4 hours for tender but intact peaches.

Reheating the snack too hot and breaking yogurt texture.

Why it happens High heat causes yogurt to separate and become watery.

Fix Warm peaches gently, add yogurt after heating, or enjoy cold.

Pro Tips

Don’t skip the lemon juice when prepping peaches.
Lemon juice’s acidity prevents enzymatic browning, keeping peaches bright and fresh-looking. It also balances the sweetness and helps maintain texture during slow cooking. Without it, peaches might turn dull and mushy.
Use zero-fat Greek yogurt for creaminess and protein.
Zero-fat Greek yogurt provides a thick, creamy texture and high protein with minimal calories. Its acidity helps prevent chalkiness if combined with protein powders and balances the sweetness of the peaches.
Cook peaches low and slow in the slow cooker.
Slow cooking at low temperature concentrates natural sugars and softens peaches gently without turning them to mush. High heat can break down fruit too fast, losing texture and flavor.
Avoid adding too much liquid to the slow cooker.
Excess liquid dilutes flavor and causes the peaches to steam rather than roast gently. Just a little water is enough to keep moisture balanced and produce a syrupy cooking liquid.
Store snack portions in airtight containers for meal prep.
Airtight containers preserve freshness and prevent the peaches from absorbing fridge odors. Proper storage keeps the snack tasting fresh and maintains texture for up to 4 days.
Add crunchy toppings just before serving.
Crunchy nuts or popcorn add textural contrast but will get soggy if added too early. Adding them last keeps the snack enjoyable and low-calorie.
If reheating, warm gently and avoid overheating the yogurt.
High heat causes yogurt to separate and lose creaminess. Warm peaches separately or microwave briefly, then add yogurt after heating for best texture.

Variations

Vanilla Protein Boost

Add a scoop of vanilla whey isolate protein powder to the Greek yogurt topping for an extra 20g of protein. Mix the protein powder into the yogurt before serving for a creamy, rich flavor. This increases protein content without adding fat or many calories.

Note: Use cold yogurt to prevent protein powder chalkiness. Stir gently to combine and avoid clumps.

Spiced Ginger Peach

Add 1/2 teaspoon ground ginger and a pinch of nutmeg to the peaches before slow cooking for a warm, spicy twist. This variation adds depth of flavor without calories and pairs well with plain Greek yogurt.

Note: Distribute spices evenly over peaches to avoid overpowering any single bite.

Berry Peach Medley

Mix in 1/2 cup fresh or frozen blueberries or raspberries in the last 30 minutes of cooking. The berries add antioxidants, color, and a tangy contrast while keeping calories low and protein high when paired with yogurt.

Note: Add berries late to prevent overcooking and preserve their texture.

Egg White Fluff Peach Bowl

Fold in whipped egg whites sweetened with stevia into cooled peaches for a fluffy, mousse-like texture that’s light and high in protein. This adds volume and creaminess without fat, making the snack feel indulgent.

Note: Whip egg whites to soft peaks and fold gently to maintain fluffiness.

Coconut Yogurt Alternative

For a dairy-free option, use unsweetened high-protein coconut yogurt instead of Greek yogurt. This changes the flavor profile slightly and adds healthy fats but keeps the snack low-calorie and protein-rich.

Note: Choose brands with added pea or rice protein to maintain protein content.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This snack is best kept refrigerated and should not be left at room temperature for more than 2 hours due to the dairy content. If left out, the yogurt may separate and the peaches can ferment, affecting flavor and safety.

Refrigerator

Store in airtight containers for up to 4 days. The snack tastes great cold or slightly warmed. The peaches will soften further and absorb yogurt creaminess, making the texture more uniform. Stir before eating if liquid separates.

Freezer

Freeze peaches (without yogurt) in freezer-safe containers for up to 2 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge. Yogurt is best added fresh after thawing to maintain creaminess. Freezing may cause texture changes, making peaches softer.

Reheating

Warm peaches gently in the microwave for 20-30 seconds. Add Greek yogurt after reheating to prevent separation. Avoid overheating as yogurt will curdle and lose its creamy texture.

Make Ahead

Cook peaches in the slow cooker up to 2 days ahead. Store peaches and yogurt separately to maintain texture. Assemble just before eating for best volume and freshness. Perfect for easy meal prep and grab-and-go snacks.

FAQ

Can I use frozen peaches instead of fresh for this slow cooker recipe?
Yes, frozen peaches can be used, but they tend to release more water during cooking, which might make the final dish a bit more watery. To compensate, reduce added water slightly and drain excess liquid before serving. Texture may be softer compared to fresh peaches.
Is this snack suitable for cutting or weight loss?
Absolutely! This snack is low in calories but high in protein and volume, which helps keep you full. The slow cooker peaches provide natural sweetness without added sugar, and the zero-fat Greek yogurt adds a creamy protein boost, making it perfect for cutting phases.
Can I substitute the Greek yogurt with another protein source?
You can try using unsweetened high-protein plant-based yogurts or fold in whipped egg whites for volume and protein. Keep in mind that texture and flavor will vary, and some alternatives may add fat or calories, so adjust portions accordingly.
How do I prevent the snack from becoming watery after refrigeration?
Drain any excess liquid that collects in the container before eating. Storing peaches and yogurt separately can also help maintain texture. Stir gently before serving to redistribute moisture evenly.
Can I add sweetener to the peaches before cooking?
It’s usually unnecessary since slow cooking concentrates the peaches’ natural sugars. If you prefer it sweeter, add a small amount of zero-calorie sweetener like stevia or monk fruit after cooking to avoid altering the texture during cooking.
How long can I store the snack in the fridge?
Stored in airtight containers, the snack keeps well for up to 4 days. Beyond that, the texture and flavor may degrade, especially the yogurt which can separate or sour.
Is it safe to eat the snack cold after slow cooking?
Yes! Many people enjoy this snack cold straight from the fridge. The flavors meld nicely, and the texture is refreshing, especially on warm days.
What’s the best way to reheat the snack without ruining the yogurt?
Warm the peaches separately in the microwave for 20-30 seconds, then add the Greek yogurt after heating. Avoid microwaving the yogurt directly to prevent curdling and separation.
