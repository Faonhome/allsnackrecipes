Easy 10-Minute Simple Cucumber Recipes UK for High Protein Low Calorie Snacking

These simple cucumber snack recipes are perfect if you're after something light yet packed with protein. Using crisp cucumbers paired with creamy zero-fat Greek yogurt and a touch of protein powder, they keep calories low and volume high. Great for a quick afternoon pick-me-up or post-workout fuel!

What You'll Need

Approximately 1 large bowl per serving, 6 snack servings total, about 2 cups volume each

  • 2 large cucumbers, peeled and diced
  • 1 cup zero-fat Greek yogurt
  • 1 scoop vanilla whey protein isolate powder
  • 1 tablespoon fresh lemon juice
  • 1 teaspoon chopped fresh dill
  • 1 teaspoon xanthan gum (optional, for thickening)
  • Stevia or monk fruit sweetener to taste
  • Pinch of salt and black pepper

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by peeling and dicing the cucumbers into small, bite-sized pieces. This increases the surface area and adds volume without extra calories. Make sure to remove seeds if they’re watery to avoid excess liquid in the snack. The goal here is to have crisp, fresh cucumber chunks that provide crunch and hydration. Avoid chopping too finely or the texture will become mushy and less satisfying. The fresh cucumbers form the bulk of the snack, so their texture is key to that volume eating effect.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cucumbers should be uniformly diced and feel firm and crisp to the touch.
Step 2
In a mixing bowl, combine the zero-fat Greek yogurt with the vanilla whey protein isolate powder. Stir gently to avoid clumps. The whey isolate hydrates best when mixed slowly with cold yogurt, preventing chalkiness. Add the lemon juice and fresh dill for flavor, then sprinkle in xanthan gum gradually while whisking to thicken the mixture. This step is crucial because xanthan gum mimics creaminess in low-fat recipes without adding calories or fat. Let the mixture rest for 2-3 minutes to fully thicken before combining with cucumbers.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: The yogurt-protein mixture should be smooth, creamy, and slightly thickened without lumps.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping xanthan gum and ending up with a watery, unappealing snack.

Why it happens Many assume zero-fat yogurt alone will thicken the snack, but without a binder, water separates quickly, ruining texture.

Fix Add a small amount of xanthan gum gradually while whisking to thicken and stabilize the mixture, ensuring a creamy, cohesive snack.

Using full-fat yogurt unknowingly, increasing calories.

Why it happens Labels can be confusing, and some yogurts marketed as Greek are not zero-fat, which boosts calories unexpectedly.

Fix Check nutrition labels carefully and choose 0% fat Greek yogurt to keep calories low and macros on point.

Mixing protein powder too quickly causing lumps.

Why it happens Protein powders hydrate slowly and can clump if added all at once or stirred too aggressively.

Fix Add powder slowly to yogurt and whisk gently but thoroughly to ensure smooth incorporation.

Pro Tips

Add xanthan gum gradually while whisking to avoid clumps.
Xanthan gum thickens the mixture by binding water molecules, creating a creamy texture without fat. If added too quickly or in large amounts, it forms lumps that ruin the smoothness. Gradual incorporation ensures even hydration and a perfect consistency.
Use cold yogurt and protein powder to mix for best texture.
Cold ingredients help the protein powder hydrate slowly, preventing chalkiness or graininess. Warm mixtures can cause protein to clump or separate, leading to an unpleasant texture. Keeping everything cold keeps the snack smooth and enjoyable.
Peel and deseed cucumbers to reduce excess water in the snack.
Cucumber seeds and skin can release extra water, diluting the creamy base and making the snack watery. Removing these parts keeps the texture firm and prevents separation, which is important for volume eating snacks that rely on thick, satisfying mouthfeel.

Variations

Spicy Cucumber Protein Snack

Add a pinch of cayenne pepper and chopped fresh mint to the cucumber yogurt mix for a spicy twist. This variation adds a metabolism-boosting kick and refreshing notes. Macros remain similar with a slight flavor change that keeps the snack exciting.

Note: Spices don’t affect texture but add heat; balance with extra lemon juice if needed to keep flavors bright.

Berry-Infused Cucumber Protein Bowl

Mix in a handful of fresh blueberries or raspberries for antioxidants and natural sweetness. This adds minimal calories but boosts flavor complexity and nutrients. Expect a slightly looser texture due to berry juices.

Note: Add berries last to avoid breaking down and making the snack watery; serve immediately for best freshness.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Due to the zero-fat dairy and fresh cucumbers, this snack should not be left out at room temperature for more than 1 hour. Cover it tightly to prevent drying out. Water separation may occur if left warm, so best eaten fresh.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container for up to 3 days. The snack firms slightly when chilled, which some find preferable. Volume remains mostly intact, but stir gently before serving to reincorporate any liquid separation.

Freezer

Not recommended to freeze this snack as cucumbers become watery and yogurt texture degrades. Freezing can cause icy crystals and loss of creaminess.

Reheating

This snack is best enjoyed cold or at room temperature. Reheating can cause separation and loss of texture due to the dairy and xanthan gum.

Make Ahead

Prepare the yogurt-protein mixture and diced cucumbers separately up to 2 days ahead. Combine just before eating to maintain cucumber crunch and prevent sogginess. Keep xanthan gum-thickened yogurt chilled until ready.

FAQ

Can I substitute the whey protein isolate with plant-based protein?
Yes, you can use a plant-based protein powder, but texture and flavor may vary. Pea or rice proteins tend to be grittier and sometimes less creamy when mixed with yogurt. You might need to adjust xanthan gum slightly to achieve the same thickness.
How do I prevent the snack from becoming watery?
Removing cucumber seeds and peeling them helps reduce excess water. Also, xanthan gum thickens the yogurt-protein mixture and prevents water separation. Store the snack chilled and consume within a few days to maintain texture.
Is this snack suitable for cutting phases?
Absolutely! With around 140 calories and 20 grams of protein per serving, it’s ideal for cutting. The high volume keeps you full, and the low calorie count fits well into most deficit meal plans.
Can I add sweeteners to this snack?
Yes, adding stevia or monk fruit sweetener works well without calories or aftertaste. Add gradually and taste as you go to avoid over-sweetening.
How long does the fullness last after eating this snack?
Thanks to the high protein and volume from cucumbers, you can expect to feel satisfied for 2-3 hours, making it perfect for bridging meals or post-workout recovery.
Can I prepare this snack in bulk for meal prep?
You can prep components ahead, but combine cucumbers and yogurt mixture just before eating to keep the cucumbers crunchy and the texture fresh.
What if I don’t have xanthan gum?
You can omit it, but expect a thinner, more watery texture. To compensate, try adding a bit more protein powder or chilling longer to allow some thickening.
Is zero-fat Greek yogurt necessary?
Zero-fat yogurt keeps calories low and texture light. Using low-fat or full-fat yogurts will increase calories and change mouthfeel, making the snack richer but less low-calorie.
