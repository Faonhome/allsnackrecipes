Easy 10-Minute Basil Cheese Recipe for a High-Protein Low-Cal Snack

Published on July 14, 2026
10 min Prep Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This basil cheese snack is creamy, fresh, and packed with protein while staying low in calories. Using zero-fat cottage cheese and fresh basil, it’s perfect for a quick afternoon boost or post-workout refuel without any guilt.

Introduction

I’ve always struggled with feeling hungry on low-calorie diets, especially when I’m trying to cut but still want to eat a decent volume of food. Tiny portions just don’t cut it for me, and I hate feeling like I’m starving by mid-afternoon. That’s why I developed this basil cheese snack — it’s creamy, packed with protein, and feels like a real treat without blowing my calorie budget. Using zero-fat cottage cheese keeps it light, while fresh basil adds an herbal brightness that makes it feel fancy but fresh. One trick I learned is to add a tiny bit of xanthan gum to thicken the mixture without adding fat, which keeps the texture rich and satisfying. Plus, adding diced cucumber gives it a nice crunch and bulk without calories, so you get a big, filling portion that really hits the spot. I usually whip this up in about 10 minutes, and it’s become my go-to for meal prep or a quick snack after the gym. A friend even asked me how I eat so much cheese and still lose weight! This snack feels indulgent but is totally guilt-free.

What You'll Need

Approximately 1 cup basil cheese per serving, 6 snack servings, about 1 cup volume per serving

  • 2 cups low-fat cottage cheese (0% fat, well drained)
  • 1/4 cup fresh basil leaves, finely chopped
  • 1 tablespoon fresh lemon juice
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon xanthan gum (optional, for creaminess)
  • 1 teaspoon water (if using xanthan gum)
  • 1/2 cup diced cucumber (for volume and crunch)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by draining your low-fat cottage cheese well to prevent watery texture. Place it in a mixing bowl and stir gently to break up any large curds. This step is crucial because excess moisture can make the final snack runny and less creamy. If the mixture looks too wet, you can strain it through a fine sieve or cheesecloth for 10 minutes before mixing.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: The cottage cheese should be moist but not dripping or watery, with a creamy consistency.
Step 2
Chop fresh basil leaves finely and add them to the cottage cheese along with lemon juice, garlic powder, salt, and pepper. Stir well to combine. The lemon juice adds brightness and helps mask any chalkiness from the low-fat dairy, while the basil infuses fresh herbal flavor. Mix thoroughly to distribute the seasoning evenly.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: The mixture should look evenly green-speckled with basil and smell fresh and tangy.
Step 3
If using xanthan gum for extra creaminess, sprinkle it evenly over the mixture along with a teaspoon of water. Whisk vigorously or use a hand mixer on low speed to incorporate. Xanthan gum thickens the mixture without adding calories or fat, giving a luscious mouthfeel that mimics full-fat cheese. Be careful not to add too much or whisk too long, or it might become slimy.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: The mixture should thicken slightly, becoming creamy and smooth without lumps.
Step 4
Dice cucumber into small pieces and fold gently into the basil cheese mixture. This adds crunch and volume without calories, making the snack more satisfying. The water content in cucumber also helps keep the snack refreshing. Avoid overmixing, which can break down the cucumber and make the mixture watery.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Cucumber pieces should be evenly distributed and maintain their shape and crunch.
Step 5
Taste the mixture and adjust seasoning if needed, adding more salt, pepper, or lemon juice to balance flavors. This final step ensures the snack isn’t bland, which is common when using low-fat dairy. The lemon juice and herbs should shine through without overpowering the creamy base.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: The snack tastes fresh, savory, and well-seasoned with balanced acidity.
Step 6
Transfer the basil cheese to airtight containers or bowls and chill in the refrigerator for at least 30 minutes before serving. Chilling firms up the texture and lets flavors meld. If you skip chilling, the texture may be looser and flavors less developed.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: The snack should be cool and slightly firm to the touch, with flavors blended.
Step 7
Serve the basil cheese as a dip with sliced veggies or as a spread on low-calorie crackers. For a volume eating snack, pair with celery sticks or bell pepper strips to keep calories low but fullness high. This versatility makes it a great snack anytime.
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: The snack holds its shape well and pairs nicely with crunchy vegetables.
Step 8
For meal prep, portion the basil cheese into 6 equal servings and store in separate containers. This makes grab-and-go snacking easy and helps control portions. Keep refrigerated and consume within 3 days for best freshness.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Each portion looks uniform and fresh, with no excess liquid separation.
Step 9
Before eating leftovers, stir gently to reincorporate any slight separation. If the texture feels too thick, add a splash of cold water or unsweetened almond milk and stir to loosen. This refreshes the snack without adding calories or fat.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: The texture should be creamy and smooth again, ready to enjoy.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping draining of cottage cheese leading to watery snack.

Why it happens People often assume cottage cheese is ready to use straight from the container, but its excess liquid can make the snack too runny and unappetizing.

Fix Strain cottage cheese in a fine mesh sieve or cheesecloth for 10 minutes before mixing to remove excess moisture and achieve a creamy, thick texture.

Using too much xanthan gum causing slimy texture.

Why it happens Xanthan gum is powerful; adding more than needed or mixing too aggressively can create an unpleasant sliminess.

Fix Use about 1 teaspoon per batch and whisk gently but thoroughly. Add gradually and test texture before adding more.

Using dried basil instead of fresh resulting in dull flavor.

Why it happens Dried herbs lose aromatic oils during processing, so they don’t provide the fresh, vibrant taste fresh basil offers.

Fix Use fresh basil leaves finely chopped for best flavor. If unavailable, increase lemon juice slightly to compensate.

Not chilling the mixture before eating making it loose and bland.

Why it happens Cold temperature firms low-fat dairy and enhances flavor melding. Eating immediately leaves the snack watery and less tasty.

Fix Refrigerate the mixture for at least 30 minutes before serving to improve texture and flavor.

Adding cucumber too early causing water release and sogginess.

Why it happens Cucumbers release water when mixed too soon, diluting the snack and ruining texture.

Fix Dice cucumber just before serving and fold in gently as the last step to maintain crunch and prevent excess moisture.

Pro Tips

Drain your cottage cheese thoroughly before mixing.
Excess moisture in cottage cheese leads to watery texture, which dilutes flavor and makes the snack less satisfying. Draining ensures a thicker, creamier base that holds seasonings and herbs well.
Add xanthan gum slowly and whisk well to avoid clumps.
Xanthan gum thickens by hydrating and binding water molecules. If added too quickly or without proper mixing, it forms lumps that ruin the smooth texture. Gradual addition and vigorous whisking create a silky consistency.
Chill the snack for at least 30 minutes before serving.
Cold temperatures help low-fat dairy proteins firm up and flavors meld. Serving immediately can result in a loose texture and muted taste, reducing enjoyment and satiety.
Use fresh herbs instead of dried for vibrant flavor.
Fresh basil contains volatile oils that provide brightness and aroma. Dried herbs tend to be muted and can make the snack taste flat, which may encourage adding unnecessary salt or fat.
Fold in crunchy vegetables last to maintain texture.
Adding cucumbers or celery too early releases water, diluting the mixture. Folding them in gently at the end preserves their crunch and adds satisfying volume.
Adjust lemon juice to taste for balanced acidity.
Acidity brightens flavor and masks chalkiness in low-fat dairy. Too little makes the snack bland; too much can overpower. Tasting as you go ensures perfect balance.
Portion into individual containers for easy grab-and-go snacks.
Meal prepping portions helps control calories and encourages consistent snacking habits. It also prevents overeating by limiting the amount available at once.

Variations

Spinach and Herb Basil Cheese

Swap half the basil for fresh spinach and add parsley and chives for a green powerhouse snack. Spinach adds fiber and volume without many calories, and the additional herbs give a complex fresh flavor. Protein remains high, calories slightly increase due to extra veggies but still under 160 calories per serving.

Note: Spinach releases water quickly, so drain or pat dry before mixing to avoid watery texture.

Lemon Zest and Dill Basil Cheese

Add lemon zest and fresh dill to the basil cheese for a bright, tangy twist. Dill complements the lemon and basil, creating a refreshing snack perfect for summer. Macros stay similar, with a slight flavor shift that keeps it interesting.

Note: Use fresh dill finely chopped and add zest just before chilling to preserve aroma.

Smoked Paprika and Chili Basil Cheese

Introduce a smoky heat by mixing in smoked paprika and a pinch of chili flakes. This savory variation is great for spice lovers and adds depth without extra calories. Protein remains high; calories stay low.

Note: Add spices gradually and taste to avoid overpowering the fresh basil flavor.

Egg White and Basil Cheese Spread

Fold in whipped egg whites for extra volume and fluffiness. This increases protein content while keeping calories low. The texture becomes lighter and airier, making it feel like a creamy mousse.

Note: Whip egg whites to soft peaks and fold gently into the basil cheese to maintain airiness.

Greek Yogurt and Basil Cheese Mix

Replace half the cottage cheese with zero-fat Greek yogurt for a tangier, creamier texture. This variation is still high protein and low calorie but has a silkier mouthfeel and a slight tartness that balances the basil.

Note: Use thick Greek yogurt and drain excess whey if needed to prevent watery texture.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This snack should not be left at room temperature for more than 1 hour due to the dairy content. Keep covered to prevent drying out and contamination. Water separation may occur if left warm, making texture less appealing.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container for up to 3 days. The flavor develops and thickens slightly when chilled. The snack holds its volume well and tastes best cold or slightly chilled.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as the dairy and cucumber separate and become icy, ruining texture and flavor.

Reheating

This snack is best eaten cold or at room temperature. Reheating can cause separation and loss of creaminess, so avoid warming.

Make Ahead

Prepare the basil cheese mixture and chopped cucumber separately 1-2 days ahead. Mix cucumber in just before serving to maintain crunch. Store the cheese base chilled in airtight containers for easy grab-and-go snacking.

FAQ

Can I substitute protein powder for cottage cheese in this recipe?
You can use unflavored whey isolate protein powder mixed with Greek yogurt or water, but texture will be less creamy and more chalky without the dairy curds. Adding xanthan gum helps, but the fresh basil flavor may be less pronounced. For best results and texture, cottage cheese is preferred.
How do I avoid a watery texture in this low-fat basil cheese snack?
Draining the cottage cheese well is key to avoiding watery texture. Also, add cucumbers just before serving, and use xanthan gum to bind moisture. Chilling the mixture helps it firm up. Skipping these steps often leads to separation and watery consistency.
Is this snack suitable for meal prep and how long does it last?
Yes, it’s great for meal prep! Store in airtight containers in the fridge for up to 3 days. Add crunchy veggies like cucumber fresh before eating to maintain texture. The flavors improve after chilling, making it convenient for busy days.
Can I eat this snack while cutting or on a low-calorie diet?
Absolutely! This snack is designed to be high in protein and low in calories, helping with satiety and muscle preservation during cutting. The volume eating aspect keeps you full without excess calories.
What if I don’t have fresh basil, can I use dried?
Dried basil can be used but it won’t deliver the same fresh, vibrant flavor. You might want to increase lemon juice or add other fresh herbs to compensate. Fresh basil is highly recommended for best taste.
How can I prevent the protein powder or dairy from tasting chalky?
Adding lemon juice and xanthan gum helps mask chalkiness and improves mouthfeel. Also, using cold liquids and chilling the snack before eating reduces chalky perception. Avoid overmixing to keep smooth texture.
Can I add a sweetener to this snack?
This recipe is savory, but if you want a hint of sweetness, use a natural zero-calorie sweetener like stevia or monk fruit sparingly. Too much sweetener may clash with the basil and lemon flavors.
Is xanthan gum necessary, or can I skip it?
Xanthan gum isn’t strictly necessary but greatly improves creaminess and prevents watery separation, especially in low-fat dairy snacks. If you skip it, expect a looser texture and shorter fullness duration.
