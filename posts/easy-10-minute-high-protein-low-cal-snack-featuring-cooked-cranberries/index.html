Easy 10-Minute High-Protein Low-Cal Snack Featuring Cooked Cranberries

Chef
Published on November 20, 2026
10 min Prep Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty
4.5 (53 ratings)✓ Thanks for rating!

I love this quick snack where cooked cranberries add a tart punch to a creamy, high-protein base. It’s filling but light, perfect when you want a guilt-free treat that keeps calories low and protein high. Great for afternoon cravings or post-workout refuel!

Introduction

I’ve always struggled with feeling hungry on low-calorie days, especially when I’m trying to cut but still want to enjoy a satisfying snack. Tiny portions just don’t cut it for me. That’s why I started experimenting with cooked cranberries combined with high-protein ingredients to create a snack that feels huge but stays under 200 calories. This version is a game changer because it’s loaded with volume thanks to the water content in the cranberries and the fluffiness from xanthan gum and ice. I learned the hard way that just mixing protein powder into yogurt can get chalky or watery, so I added a touch of vanilla and a thickener to keep it creamy and smooth. Plus, cooking the cranberries softens their tartness and makes them easier to digest, which I love. I usually whip this up in the afternoon when I hit that slump or after a workout to refuel without guilt. My friends always ask how I manage to eat so much and still lose weight, and this snack is definitely part of my secret. It’s refreshing, creamy, and filling — the perfect combo for a low-calorie, high-protein treat that keeps me full for hours.

What You'll Need

Approximately 1 large bowl per serving, 6 snack servings, about 2 cups volume per serving

  • 1 cup raw cranberries, rinsed
  • 1/2 cup water
  • 1 scoop vanilla whey protein isolate powder (about 25g protein)
  • 1 cup zero-fat Greek yogurt
  • 1 tsp erythritol or monk fruit sweetener (optional)
  • 1/2 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1/4 tsp xanthan gum (for thickening)
  • Ice cubes (optional, for volume and chill)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by cooking the cranberries gently with water over medium heat. This softens them and releases their natural tart flavor without adding sugar. Let them simmer for about 5 minutes until they burst and the mixture thickens slightly. This step is crucial because raw cranberries are too firm and sour to enjoy as a snack. Cooking also helps break down the fibrous skins, making the texture smoother and easier to mix with the creamy base. Avoid boiling too hard or too long, or the berries will lose their bright color and become mushy. You want a thick, jammy consistency that still has some texture.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cranberries are soft, skins burst, and mixture looks thickened but not watery
Step 2
While the cranberries cool slightly, mix the zero-fat Greek yogurt with protein powder, vanilla extract, and erythritol if using. Whisk well to avoid clumps. The key here is to add the protein powder slowly and whisk vigorously so it hydrates properly and doesn’t get chalky. Using whey isolate helps because it dissolves better than concentrates. Adding a little vanilla masks any protein powder aftertaste. If you want extra volume and chill, fold in a few ice cubes or blend the mixture briefly. This keeps the snack refreshing and light.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Smooth, creamy mixture with no lumps and a slightly thick texture
Step 3
Sprinkle the xanthan gum evenly over the yogurt-protein mixture and whisk quickly to avoid clumps. Xanthan gum is a zero-calorie thickener that mimics creaminess and helps hold the texture without fat. This step prevents watery separation that often happens in low-fat protein snacks. Be careful not to add too much or the texture becomes gummy. About 1/4 teaspoon is perfect for this volume. Whisking fast and thoroughly is important to hydrate the gum and create a stable, creamy base.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Mixture thickens slightly and feels smooth and creamy
Step 4
Fold the cooked cranberries gently into the yogurt-protein base. You want to keep some of the berry texture intact for contrast but distribute them evenly so every bite has that tart burst. Mixing too aggressively will break down the berries completely and lose the pleasant chew. The combination of creamy and fruity textures makes this snack more satisfying and interesting.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Evenly distributed berries with visible chunks in creamy base
Step 5
Taste and adjust sweetness if needed by adding a tiny bit more erythritol or monk fruit. Since cranberries are naturally tart, a little sweetener balances the flavor without adding calories. Be cautious not to oversweeten because that can mask the natural berry brightness and make it taste artificial.
⏱ Time: 30 seconds
✓ Doneness: Balanced tart and sweet flavor with no bitterness
Step 6
Chill the snack in the fridge for at least 15 minutes before serving. Chilling firms up the texture and allows flavors to meld. It also enhances the creamy mouthfeel since the mixture has no fat to stabilize it at room temperature. If you eat it right away, it may feel slightly loose, so patience here pays off.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Snack feels cool, thickened, and creamy when spooned
Step 7
Serve in large bowls or portion into containers for meal prep. The volume looks impressive for under 200 calories, which is great for those who hate tiny snack portions. This snack holds up well for a few hours in the fridge, making it perfect for packing into your gym bag or office drawer.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Snack holds shape and looks appetizing in serving container
Step 8
If you want to add a little texture, sprinkle a few air-popped popcorn kernels or chopped celery on top before eating. This adds crunch without adding many calories. Crunchy textures increase satiety and make the snack feel more indulgent.
⏱ Time: 30 seconds
✓ Doneness: Crunchy topping evenly scattered and fresh
Step 9
Enjoy immediately or store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. Avoid freezing as the texture changes due to high water content in cranberries and yogurt. When reheating, it’s best eaten cold or at room temperature to preserve creaminess and volume.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Snack tastes fresh and creamy when eaten cold or room temp

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the thickener and ending with a watery snack.

Why it happens People assume yogurt and protein powder alone will hold texture, but fat-free dairy tends to separate, especially with added water from cooked berries.

Fix Add a small amount of xanthan gum or guar gum to stabilize the mixture and prevent watery separation, whisking it in thoroughly.

Pro Tips

Whisk protein powder into yogurt slowly and thoroughly to avoid lumps.
Protein powder can clump when added too quickly to thick dairy, causing chalky texture. Whisking slowly allows the powder to hydrate evenly, resulting in a smooth, creamy base without unpleasant bits.
Cook cranberries just until they burst and thicken, don’t overcook.
Overcooking destroys the bright color and fresh tart flavor, turning the berries mushy and dull. Proper cooking releases water and softens skins for volume without losing appealing texture.
Add xanthan gum last and whisk immediately to prevent clumps.
Xanthan gum hydrates quickly and can form lumps if not dispersed fast. Whisking immediately ensures even thickening and a smooth mouthfeel, essential for creamy low-fat snacks.

Variations

Berry Medley Protein Snack

Swap cranberries for a mix of cooked blueberries and raspberries for a sweeter, antioxidant-rich version. The cooking process remains the same, but expect a slightly softer texture and a sweeter flavor profile. Protein and calorie counts stay similar.

Note: Different berries release varying amounts of water, so adjust cooking time to avoid excess liquid and maintain thickness.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Due to zero-fat dairy and cooked berries, this snack should not be left at room temperature for more than 1 hour. Exposure to warmth can cause separation and spoilage. Cover with plastic wrap or store in airtight containers to minimize drying.

Refrigerator

Store in airtight containers for up to 3 days. The snack firms up when chilled, improving creaminess and texture. Flavors meld nicely after a few hours, making it ideal for meal prep. Stir gently before serving if some separation occurs.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as the high water content in cranberries and yogurt causes icy texture and loss of creaminess. Thawing leads to separation and graininess.

Reheating

This snack is best eaten cold or at room temperature. Reheating can cause protein to denature and xanthan gum to lose its thickening ability, resulting in watery or grainy texture. If preferred warm, gently stir in a splash of unsweetened almond milk to restore creaminess.

Make Ahead

Cook cranberries up to 2 days ahead and store separately in the fridge. Mix yogurt, protein powder, and xanthan gum fresh before serving to maintain maximum creaminess and volume. Add berries last to keep texture vibrant and prevent sogginess.

FAQ

Can I use frozen cranberries instead of fresh?
Yes, frozen cranberries work well and are a convenient alternative. Thaw them slightly before cooking to reduce excess water. Cooking time may be a bit longer to achieve the right thickness. Frozen berries retain most nutrients and flavor, making them a great choice.
What if I don’t have xanthan gum?
You can substitute with a small amount of guar gum or chia seeds ground finely, but xanthan gum gives the best creamy texture without grit. Without a thickener, the snack may be watery and separate quickly, so adding some form of binder is highly recommended.
How do I avoid the chalky protein powder taste?
Use a high-quality whey isolate and add it slowly while whisking vigorously. Adding vanilla extract and chilling the mixture also helps mask chalkiness. Avoid mixing protein powder directly into hot liquids or thick yogurt without enough liquid to hydrate it properly.
Is this snack suitable for cutting or weight loss?
Absolutely! It’s high in protein to support muscle retention and low in calories to fit into a deficit. The volume from cooked cranberries and yogurt helps you feel full longer, making it easier to stick to your calorie goals.
Can I make this snack vegan?
You can try using plant-based protein powder and coconut yogurt, but texture and flavor will differ. Plant proteins often don’t dissolve as smoothly, and coconut yogurt has more fat and calories. Experiment with thickening agents to achieve a similar creamy texture.
How long does this snack keep in the fridge?
Stored in an airtight container, it keeps well for up to 3 days. After that, the texture may degrade and separation may increase. Always give it a good stir before eating if stored for more than a day.
Can I add nuts or seeds for extra crunch?
Yes, adding a small sprinkle of chopped nuts or seeds on top is a great way to add texture and healthy fats. Keep portions small to maintain the low-calorie profile, as nuts are calorie-dense.
What’s the best time to eat this snack?
It’s perfect for an afternoon energy boost or post-workout refuel. The protein supports muscle recovery while the volume and tartness satisfy cravings without excess calories.
