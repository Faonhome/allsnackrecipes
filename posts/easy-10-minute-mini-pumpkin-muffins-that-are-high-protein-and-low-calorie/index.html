Easy 10-Minute Mini Pumpkin Muffins That Are High Protein and Low Calorie

These mini pumpkin muffins pack a surprising punch of protein with minimal calories, making them perfect for a guilt-free snack or post-workout treat. Using egg whites and pumpkin puree keeps them moist and fluffy without extra fat. I love grabbing one during my afternoon slump!

What You'll Need

Approximately 12 mini muffins, about 1 muffin per serving, 12 snack servings

  • 1 cup pumpkin puree, canned or fresh
  • 4 large egg whites, room temperature
  • 1/2 cup oat flour
  • 1 scoop vanilla whey protein isolate powder
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1/2 tsp cinnamon
  • 1/4 tsp nutmeg
  • 1/4 tsp salt
  • 2 tbsp erythritol or preferred zero-calorie sweetener
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and line a mini muffin tin with paper liners or lightly grease it. This step ensures even cooking and prevents sticking, which is especially important for low-fat recipes like this where the muffins can stick easily. Using a mini muffin tin helps keep portions controlled and gives that satisfying volume without extra calories. Avoid skipping the liners unless you’re confident in your tin’s non-stick quality, as these moist muffins can be tricky to remove otherwise.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Oven preheated and muffin tin prepared with liners or greased
Step 2
In a large bowl, whisk together the pumpkin puree, egg whites, vanilla extract, and erythritol until smooth and well combined. Mixing these wet ingredients first helps hydrate the protein powder and oat flour evenly, preventing clumps and ensuring a uniform texture. Whisking egg whites separately before adding can help with fluffiness, but for speed, this combined whisking works well. Don’t rush this step — smooth batter means better muffins.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Wet ingredients are homogenous with no lumps or streaks
Step 3
In a separate bowl, sift together the oat flour, whey protein powder, baking powder, cinnamon, nutmeg, and salt. Sifting helps aerate the dry ingredients and prevents protein powder from clumping, which is a common issue in low-fat baking. This step also ensures even distribution of spices and leavening agents, critical for muffin rise and flavor balance. If you skip sifting, you might get dense spots or uneven texture.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are light, fluffy, and evenly mixed
Step 4
Gradually fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture using a spatula. Folding gently prevents deflating the egg whites too much, which helps keep the muffins airy. Overmixing here can make the muffins dense and tough, so mix just until combined. The batter will be thick but scoopable. This technique balances protein powder’s tendency to dry out baked goods with enough moisture from pumpkin and egg whites.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is uniform in color and consistency, thick but smooth
Step 5
Spoon the batter evenly into the prepared mini muffin tin, filling each cup about 3/4 full. This allows room for the muffins to rise without overflowing. Using a small cookie scoop or spoon helps portion control and keeps baking times consistent. Avoid overfilling, which causes spills and uneven cooking.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: All muffin cups filled evenly, batter level just below the rim
Step 6
Bake the muffins in the preheated oven for 10 to 12 minutes. Start checking at 10 minutes by inserting a toothpick into the center; it should come out mostly clean with a few moist crumbs. The short bake time prevents drying out these low-fat muffins. If you leave them too long, they’ll become crumbly and lose moisture. Keep an eye on them to catch the perfect texture.
⏱ Time: 10-12 minutes
✓ Doneness: Toothpick inserted in center comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs
Step 7
Remove the muffins from the oven and let them cool in the tin for 5 minutes before transferring to a wire rack. Cooling in the pan prevents breakage, but removing them too soon can cause sticking. The wire rack lets air circulate so the muffins don’t become soggy on the bottom. This step is key for maintaining texture and making sure they hold their shape.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Muffins are firm enough to handle but still warm
Step 8
Once completely cooled, store the mini muffins in an airtight container in the fridge. This keeps them fresh and prevents drying out. Because these muffins are low fat and high protein, they can stiffen a bit when chilled but still taste great. Reheat briefly in the microwave for a soft texture or eat cold as a quick snack.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Muffins stored properly and maintain texture for several days
Step 9
Enjoy your mini pumpkin muffins as a snack, breakfast, or post-workout fuel. They’re perfect for volume eating because you get 12 small muffins that satisfy hunger without excess calories. Pair with a cup of black coffee or herbal tea for a cozy treat. Remember, portion control and ingredient balance make these muffins a guilt-free indulgence!
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Snack eaten happily with satisfied appetite

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Muffins turn out too watery or don’t hold shape.

Why it happens Often caused by skipping the oat flour or not measuring pumpkin puree accurately, leading to excess moisture.

Fix Measure ingredients carefully and ensure you include oat flour as a binder. If batter seems too wet, add a tablespoon more oat flour. Also, avoid using pumpkin pie filling instead of plain puree.

Chalky or gritty protein taste.

Why it happens Using protein powders with fillers or not hydrating powder well can cause unpleasant texture.

Fix Use a high-quality whey isolate and mix dry ingredients thoroughly before adding wet. Sift powders and whisk wet ingredients well to hydrate protein fully.

Muffins are dense or tough.

Why it happens Overmixing batter or skipping whisking egg whites deflates air bubbles needed for fluffiness.

Fix Fold dry ingredients gently and consider whipping egg whites separately before folding in for extra lift.

Muffins stick to pan.

Why it happens Not greasing the tin or skipping liners causes sticking, especially with low-fat batters.

Fix Use paper liners or grease pan thoroughly with a non-stick spray or oil to ensure easy removal.

Muffins dry out quickly.

Why it happens Leaving muffins uncovered at room temperature or baking too long causes moisture loss.

Fix Store muffins in airtight containers in fridge and monitor baking time carefully. Reheat briefly before eating if desired.

Pro Tips

Whisk egg whites separately before mixing to add extra fluffiness.
Whipping egg whites incorporates air, which helps the muffins rise and keeps them light despite no added fat. If you skip this, the muffins may be denser and less airy, losing volume and making them feel heavier.
Sift dry ingredients to prevent protein powder clumps.
Protein powder tends to clump when mixed with wet ingredients, especially in low-fat recipes. Sifting aerates the powder and evenly distributes leavening, ensuring smooth batter and consistent muffin texture.
Use a mini muffin tin for portion control and quick baking.
Mini muffins bake faster and allow you to enjoy multiple small snacks, promoting volume eating. Larger muffins take longer and may dry out as you try to cook them through.
Don’t overmix the batter once dry ingredients are added.
Overmixing develops gluten and deflates air bubbles, resulting in dense, tough muffins. Gentle folding keeps the texture tender and light.
Cool muffins on a wire rack to prevent sogginess.
Cooling in the tin traps steam, making bottoms soggy. A wire rack allows air circulation to keep muffins firm and maintain their texture.
Store muffins in airtight containers in the fridge.
Low-fat muffins dry out faster at room temperature. Refrigeration keeps them fresh longer but can stiffen the texture slightly, which can be remedied by brief warming.
Use a toothpick to test doneness early.
Since these muffins bake quickly, checking at 10 minutes prevents overbaking and drying out, preserving moisture and softness.

Variations

Chocolate Chip Pumpkin Mini Muffins

Add 2 tablespoons of sugar-free mini chocolate chips to the batter for a sweet twist. This adds a bit of indulgence with minimal calories. The chocolate pairs perfectly with pumpkin and spices. Protein and calories remain similar, but texture gains some melty pockets.

Note: Fold chips in gently at the end to avoid sinking. Bake same time; chips may brown slightly.

Gluten-Free Almond Flour Pumpkin Muffins

Swap oat flour for almond flour for a nutty flavor and gluten-free option. Almond flour is higher in fat, so reduce sweetener slightly. Texture will be denser and moister. Protein remains high but calories increase a bit due to fat content.

Note: Almond flour absorbs less moisture; reduce pumpkin puree by 1-2 tablespoons to keep batter balanced.

Vegan Pumpkin Mini Muffins

Replace egg whites with aquafaba (chickpea liquid) whipped to stiff peaks. Use plant-based protein powder. Texture will be slightly different—less dense and more airy. Sweetener and spices remain the same. Great for vegan high-protein snacking.

Note: Whip aquafaba for 5-7 minutes until peaks form. Fold in gently to maintain volume.

Spiced Maple Pumpkin Muffins

Swap erythritol for 1 tablespoon sugar-free maple syrup and add a pinch of ground cloves and ginger for extra warmth. This adds a richer flavor profile while keeping calories low. Protein content stays about the same.

Note: Liquid sweetener adds moisture; reduce pumpkin puree by 1 tablespoon to keep batter consistency.

Pumpkin Banana Protein Mini Muffins

Add 1/4 cup mashed ripe banana to the pumpkin mixture for natural sweetness and potassium boost. Reduce erythritol by half. Texture will be moister and slightly denser, but still high protein and low calorie.

Note: Banana adds moisture; adjust oat flour by adding 1 tablespoon more if batter feels too wet.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

These muffins can be kept at room temperature for up to 4 hours if covered tightly to prevent drying. Because they are low-fat and contain egg whites, they are best consumed soon after baking to avoid spoilage or texture changes. Warm environments may cause slight water separation from pumpkin puree.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container for up to 4 days. Refrigeration firms the muffins slightly but preserves freshness. They may taste better chilled or warmed briefly in the microwave for 10-15 seconds to soften.

Freezer

Freeze muffins in a sealed container or freezer bag for up to 2 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temperature for 1-2 hours. Texture may be a bit drier after freezing; warming before eating helps restore softness.

Reheating

Reheat muffins in the microwave for 10-15 seconds or in a toaster oven at 300°F for 3-5 minutes. Avoid overheating which can dry them out. Adding a splash of almond milk before warming helps restore moisture.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the batter 1 day ahead and keep it covered in the fridge. Bake fresh muffins the next day for best texture and volume. Alternatively, bake muffins ahead and freeze for quick snacks. Add spices and protein powder fresh if mixing batter early to prevent settling.

FAQ

Can I use whole eggs instead of egg whites?
Yes, you can substitute whole eggs but this will increase fat and calorie content. The muffins will be richer and denser. Using egg whites keeps the recipe low-calorie and fluffy, but whole eggs add moisture and flavor if you prefer.
How do I prevent the muffins from being too dry?
Avoid overbaking and measure pumpkin puree accurately to keep moisture balanced. Adding pumpkin and egg whites provides hydration, but baking too long or skipping the puree can dry muffins out. Also, don’t overmix batter which can toughen texture.
Can I use a different protein powder?
Whey isolate is best for low calorie and texture, but you can use plant-based powders. Some plant proteins may cause graininess or dryness, so you might need to adjust liquids or baking time slightly.
How long do these muffins keep fresh?
Stored in an airtight container in the fridge, they stay fresh for up to 4 days. For longer storage, freeze them. Avoid leaving at room temperature for more than a few hours due to egg content.
Are these muffins suitable for meal prep?
Absolutely! They’re perfect for meal prep since they’re portion-controlled, high protein, and low calorie. Bake a batch on Sunday and grab them throughout the week for snacks or breakfast.
Can I add nuts or seeds for crunch?
You can add a small amount (1-2 tbsp) of chopped nuts or seeds, but this will increase calories and fat. For strict low-calorie goals, it’s best to keep the recipe nut-free.
What’s the best way to sweeten these muffins without sugar?
Erythritol or monk fruit sweeteners work well without adding calories or aftertaste when measured properly. Avoid liquid sweeteners unless you adjust other liquids in the recipe.
Can I make these muffins larger?
Yes, but baking time will increase. Mini muffins bake quickly and help with portion control. Larger muffins might become drier or denser if not adjusted properly.
